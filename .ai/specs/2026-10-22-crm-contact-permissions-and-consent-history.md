# CRM contact permissions and consent history

## 📝 TLDR

Add person-level suppression controls and address-aware, purpose-specific permission history in `customers`. Preserve evidence of grants, withdrawals and corrections, and enforce the current decision before CRM outreach reaches an integrated sender. This design supports [issue #7700](https://github.com/open-mercato/open-mercato/issues/7700), the [Phase 1 umbrella](2026-10-22-crm-field-model-phase-1.md), and the separate [contact points capability](2026-10-22-crm-contact-points.md); it does not certify legal compliance or control messages sent outside Open Mercato.

## Resolved assumptions (autonomous defaults)

| Question | Default | Rationale / gate |
|---|---|---|
| Q1. Combine contact storage and permission policy? | Separate specifications; permissions build on contact points. | Each has a distinct release and failure boundary. |
| Q2. Reuse the auth consent ledger service? | Reuse evidence/projection patterns, with customers-owned entities and no auth-user relation. | The referenced auth ledger is a proposal, not an implemented service at this baseline; a CRM person need not be a login user. |
| Q3. Do `newsletter_opt_in=true` and lifecycle `subscriber` prove consent? | Import them as `legacy_unverified` evidence with `unknown` permission and no invented grant date or legal basis. | **⚠ NEEDS HUMAN CONFIRMATION** before activating the changed outreach policy or retiring legacy fields; mapping those booleans directly to verified permission could create unsupported legal claims. |
| Q4. How are legal bases interpreted? | Record all three bases, but the initial built-in outreach evaluator authorizes only evidenced consent. | Contract/legitimate-interest assertions remain reviewable evidence; enabling them as outreach authority needs a separately reviewed tenant policy, not a default inferred from the enum. |
| Q5. Does `email_bounced` follow the person? | Expose it as the current primary email's delivery-state projection. | Bounce remains attached to the address; switching primary does not transfer it. |
| Q6. Can withdrawal be undone? | Never erase it or silently reactivate a grant; require a new evidenced assertion. | Preserve an accurate chronology and the subject's current restriction. |

## 📝 Overview

Current people have a seeded `newsletter_opt_in` custom field, but no customers-owned structured permission history. `auth/data/entities.ts` implements `UserConsent`; the proposed [`2026-08-18-tenant-legal-documents-and-consent-versioning.md`](2026-08-18-tenant-legal-documents-and-consent-versioning.md) describes an append-only ledger and mutable projection that must not be assumed available. This feature neither changes auth consent hashes nor conflates staff accounts, portal accounts and CRM people.

The scope includes recording/reviewing permissions, suppressing CRM outreach, bounded sender integration and evidence-preserving migration. Bulk campaigns, public preference centres, legal-document publishing, automated legitimate-interest assessment, external vendor suppression synchronisation and a general privacy-erasure orchestrator are non-goals.

## 📝 Problem Statement

A person can have several destinations with different preferences; an address change must not transfer consent or hide an earlier withdrawal. [Microsoft's documented consent model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/customer-insights/journeys/real-time-marketing-email-text-consent) evaluates contact-point and purpose/topic permission immediately before sending. [HubSpot documents](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/add-multiple-email-addresses-to-a-contact) address-level bounce and unsubscribe state, reinforcing the distinction between person and destination.

[ICO guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/consent/how-should-we-obtain-record-and-manage-consent/) identifies evidence about who consented, when/how and what statement they saw, plus withdrawal records. That supports preserving evidence fields; jurisdiction, purpose and legal interpretation still require the tenant's policy. The [research note](assets/crm-field-model-phase-1/research.md) distinguishes documented behavior from individual pain reports and records the legal guidance's review caveat.

## 📝 Proposed Solution

Separate three concepts: suppression flags override everything, permission assertions describe an evidenced basis for one purpose/destination, and delivery health describes reachability. The UI says “Permission recorded” or “Evidence needs review”, not “GDPR compliant”. A grant never resets suppression or bounce state. Clearing a flag never grants permission. A changed canonical destination starts with unknown permission; a display-only case/whitespace edit retains the same destination's permission lookup and delivery health/provenance. The companion contact-point rule applies equally through legacy primary-field writes, so neither path can clear a bounce block while retaining the same grant.

Purpose defaults are `newsletter`, `marketing`, `product_updates` in the customers dictionary, kind `contact-permission-purposes`, preserving the existing custom-kind route identity. Seed only an absent kind; existing entries/settings and intentionally empty dictionaries remain untouched. Settings use the existing dictionary routes and management guard. Add `GET /api/customers/contact-permissions/purposes` under the permission-read ACL, returning `{items:[{value,label,icon?,color?}]}` from the scoped dictionary helper, so reads do not inherit an unrelated generic-dictionary guard. Purpose tokens allow 1–150 characters, matching dictionary values. The initial built-in evaluator accepts only `legalBasis=consent` with recorded evidence and an existing purpose; missing/deleted purpose yields unknown and blocks CRM outreach. `contract` and `legitimate_interest` remain valid historical assertions without automatically becoming send authorization. A configurable jurisdiction/legal-basis policy engine is deferred; no undefined policy table or hidden setting is introduced. The evaluator is explicitly an operationally conservative rule, not a declaration that consent is the only lawful basis in every jurisdiction.

## 📝 Architecture

`customers` owns `CustomerContactPermission`, `CustomerConsentEvent`, the decision service, validators, commands, UI and migration. No cross-module ORM relationships or imported auth business service. Use `makeCrudRoute` with an indexer for current-state reads; lifecycle mutations use guarded command/action routes because arbitrary CRUD update/delete would corrupt the ledger. All routes export OpenAPI. Transactional commands append an event and update the current projection together, then emit typed `customers.contact_permission.recorded|withdrawn|corrected` and `customers.contact_restriction.updated` events containing IDs, scope and versions, without evidence text or addresses.

Current permission decisions read authoritative decrypted records, never an eventually consistent search index or cached allow result. Any presentation cache uses DI `cache` with tenant/organization/person tags and after-commit invalidation. The ledger is not indexed into general search or vectors. Exact parent and contact-point scope is validated even when a UUID exists in another organization.

### Proposed outbound enforcement seam

The existing concrete send path is `customers/api/people/[id]/emails/route.ts` → `communication_channels/lib/send-as-user.ts` → messaging/outbound delivery. It requires `customers.email.compose` and accepts `to`, `cc`, `bcc`. `communication_channels/commands/deliver-outbound-message.ts` invokes `adapter.sendMessage`; `messages/workers/send-email.worker.ts` separately calls `sendMessageEmailToExternal`, whose sender uses shared `sendEmail`. Gmail and IMAP adapters are present under their provider packages. These are actual call sites to cover, not evidence of a universal interception layer.

This spec explicitly proposes a narrow **messages-owned outbound policy contract**, invoked by both external-delivery paths immediately before their provider call; no runtime change occurs in this spec PR. Its registration interface is `registerOutboundPolicy({ id, evaluate })`, and evaluation accepts `{ tenantId, organizationId, messageId, recipients, requiredPolicyIds, policyContext }` and returns `{ allowed, reasonCode, evaluatedAt, decisionRefs }`. `recipients` contains normalized destination plus `to|cc|bcc` role; `decisionRefs` contains `{ policyId, resourceKind, resourceId, updatedAt }` version references, without raw addresses. `customers` owns optional registration of `customers.crm_outreach` and its implementation; sender modules import only the generic contract, never customers entities/services. Registration uses existing module DI initialization rather than a new discovery convention.

Add three server-owned fields to `messages`: `outbound_required_policy_ids` (JSON string array, default empty), `outbound_policy_context` (nullable encrypted JSON) and `outbound_policy_result` (nullable encrypted JSON of the latest decision above). Context contains `{ version: 1, purpose, recipients: [{ role, inputIndex, personId, contactPointId, destinationSnapshot }] }`; CRM organization scope comes from the message. These fields are excluded from ordinary message write schemas; trusted compose commands populate them and retries preserve them. Add messages encryption-map backfill and schema snapshot. Existing channel metadata may carry an ID reference but is not the authority. The latest result plus existing delivery-failure state explains blocked delivery; immutable consent history remains the authority for evidence, not this retry-overwritten diagnostic result.

The CRM composer resolves all recipients, adds purpose and trusted person/contact-point identities, and persists `requiredPolicyIds: ['customers.crm_outreach']` plus the trusted context with the message. Generic messages APIs cannot delete or downgrade this context on CRM-linked replies/retries. Customers-owned command/API interceptors also classify sends associated with CRM person objects, closing the generic message-composer route. Every `to`/`cc`/`bcc` recipient must map unambiguously within the selected organization; unknown, ambiguous or legacy-unparsed destinations fail closed for CRM outreach. The root person alone does not authorize every recipient. Existing arbitrary-recipient behavior consequently changes only for these CRM-context sends, and rollout is covered by the confirmation gate.

Enqueue-time evaluation provides immediate feedback; sender-time evaluation uses fresh flags, canonical identity, permission and policy. Missing required registration, unavailable database/decryption, unknown evidence or changed destination blocks delivery and preserves a recorded decision. No-retry policy denials differ from retryable infrastructure failures. A persisted allow at enqueue is never a delivery token. Worker retries evaluate again. A withdrawal committed before this final evaluation blocks; after provider acceptance, delivery cannot be recalled. Serialize all permission stream creation, grants, withdrawals, restriction writes and final sender evaluation under one tenant/organization policy lock, then acquire affected person/target rows in stable UUID order. Use existing `withAdvisoryXactLock` with namespace `customers_outreach_policy` and tenant/organization key; its callback owns the transaction and passes the same entity manager to inner command writes (no nested transaction or fork outside it). This deliberately coarser lock also works without a lookup secret and prevents different-person streams for one destination racing the decision; no address-derived lock key or unkeyed PII digest is stored. Final multi-recipient evaluation takes one consistent locked snapshot. Keep the transaction short, release it before network delivery, and document the unavoidable evaluation-to-provider/network window rather than claim distributed atomic delivery. If a withdrawal commits before the final decision snapshot it denies; a later withdrawal cannot revoke a decision already handed to a provider. Monitor policy-lock contention; no network/provider call is allowed while holding it.

Non-CRM transactional/auth/security emails keep existing semantics; a caller cannot label CRM marketing as transactional to bypass classification. External clients, manual mail applications, call dialers, SMS/post vendors and adapters bypassing these two integration paths are not controlled. For phone/SMS/post, expose decision evaluation and suppress the CRM action where available; do not advertise provider enforcement until an adapter has integration tests. Existing queued CRM messages without resolvable context are quarantined for review on activation, never assumed authorized.

## 📝 Data Model

All rows are scoped by required `tenant_id`, `organization_id`, `person_entity_id`; the parent must be a live person in that scope. UUID references are IDs only. Timestamps are UTC.

| Entity / columns | Type and meaning |
|---|---|
| `customer_people`: `do_not_contact`, `do_not_email`, `do_not_call` | Boolean default false; mutated through person commands with parent `updated_at`; suppression changes also append restriction events |
| `customer_contact_permissions`: `id`, scope/parent IDs, `created_at`, `updated_at` | Required UUIDs and versioned timestamps; no ordinary deletion |
| `channel`, `purpose` | `email|phone|sms|post`; stable dictionary purpose key |
| `target_kind`, `contact_point_id`, `address_id` | `contact_point|postal_address|person`; required compatible scoped ID for first two, null otherwise; email/phone/SMS assertions require a contact point; person target only for post |
| `destination_snapshot`, `destination_hash` | Encrypted canonical destination snapshot and nullable keyed lookup hash using contact-point hashing rules; both null for a person-level post target; ID alone never transfers permission after a value edit |
| `state`, `legal_basis`, `evidence_quality` | `unknown|permitted|withdrawn`; nullable `consent|legitimate_interest|contract`; `recorded|legacy_unverified` |
| `granted_at`, `withdrawn_at`, `latest_event_id` | Nullable evidenced grant/withdrawal times; required most recent event UUID |
| `customer_consent_events`: `id`, scope/parent IDs, `permission_id`, `created_at`, `updated_at` | Required UUIDs/timestamps; permission ID nullable for person restriction events and unresolved legacy-evidence events; immutable timestamps returned for reference |
| `event_type`, `occurred_at`, `recorded_at`, `actor_user_id` | `asserted|withdrawn|corrected|legacy_imported|restriction_changed`; evidenced time nullable for legacy, server recorded time, nullable actor UUID |
| `supersedes_event_id`, `idempotency_key` | Nullable referenced event in same stream; required opaque key unique per scope and operation |
| `snapshot` | Encrypted JSON of channel, purpose, target identity, destination, state/basis, source, statement/version/reference, evidence text, and prior/new suppression flags as applicable |
| `legacy_evidence_event_id` | Nullable scalar event UUID linking an assertion to a same-person/same-scope unresolved `legacy_imported` observation; does not supersede a withdrawal or grant permission by itself. |
| `source`, `evidence_reference`, `reason` | Bounded encrypted text; source required; external reference is not fetched automatically |

A permission stream is unique for person/channel/purpose/canonical target, excluding legal basis so a different basis cannot bypass withdrawal. Contact-point IDs survive deletion, while encrypted destination snapshots remain evidence. Exact-target withdrawal defeats all earlier assertions for that target/channel/purpose; other purposes remain separately evaluated. The same canonical address deleted and recreated under that person resolves the original stream. Sender evaluation also checks outstanding withdrawals for the same canonical destination/channel/purpose across person streams in the same organization, including archived parents, so duplicate creation cannot bypass a restriction. An evidenced reassertion only compensates its own stream; it cannot clear a different person stream. Conflicting identity/evidence remains blocked for manual review. Trusted evaluator reads may inspect these scoped restrictions without exposing hidden-person IDs or evidence in response reason codes; no cross-organization matching is allowed. Suppression records are checked before evaluating any permission. If a newer assertion exists, it must explicitly reference the withdrawal and provide fresh evidence; correction cannot manufacture permission.

Declare all destination/evidence/provenance/reason/snapshot fields in `customers/encryption.ts` and ship encryption-map backfill for existing tenants. Hashes follow shared helpers as specified by contact points; without keyed hashing, compare scoped decrypted canonical targets under the organization policy lock followed by person/target locks rather than store unkeyed PII digests. Index scoped person/channel/purpose/time plus stream identity. Do not expose hashes publicly or claim a hash proves legal validity. The existing person-delete command physically removes the person/profile. Neither physical deletion, create-undo nor archival may cascade-delete permission projections/events or unlink destination restriction history: their person IDs are durable scalar references without a deleting parent FK. Trusted sender restriction checks query scoped retained streams directly, without an inner join requiring a live person. Normal permission UI still requires an accessible live parent; restoring the same person ID exposes its retained evidence again. Delete/restore does not emit a new grant or reset a withdrawal. Retain unresolved legacy observations under the same rule. Authorized privacy erasure must handle these encrypted snapshots and audit payloads under the applicable retention process; this feature adds no ordinary erasure endpoint or invented retention period.


For these new keyed-only lookup columns, leave `hashField` unset on the encrypted-field map. The command calls the canonical hash helper and stores only keyed `v2:` output or null in the separate digest column. The existing encryption service would otherwise overwrite a null digest with its unkeyed fallback during encryption. Verify the persisted result after actual encrypt/flush with and without a lookup secret, not only the pre-persistence command object.
## 📝 API Contracts

All routes require authentication. Reads require `customers.people.view` plus new `customers.contact_permissions.view`; permission/restriction writes require `customers.people.manage` plus `customers.contact_permissions.manage`. New features are added to `acl.ts`/`setup.ts`, wildcard-aware; employee access is not silently expanded. Existing compose ACL remains mandatory for email send. The evaluate endpoint uses the permission-read ACL; internal sender evaluation uses trusted worker scope, not impersonated reader access. Cross-scope references return 404, invalid fields 422, missing required versions 428, stale versions the shared 409 conflict body. Existing people requests without new restriction fields retain their established version contract; requests that change suppression require the parent header.

| Route | Input | Response |
|---|---|---|
| `GET /api/customers/contact-permissions` | `personId`, optional `id/channel/purpose`, `page/pageSize≤100` | `{ items, total, page, pageSize, totalPages, personUpdatedAt }` |
| `GET /api/customers/contact-permissions/{id}/events` | `page/pageSize≤100` | Same pagination envelope with immutable events |
| `POST /api/customers/contact-permissions` | `{ personId, permissionId?, channel, purpose, targetKind, contactPointId?, addressId?, legalBasis, occurredAt, source, statement?, statementVersion?, evidenceReference?, evidenceText?, reason?, supersedesWithdrawalEventId?, legacyEvidenceEventId?, idempotencyKey, personUpdatedAt, targetUpdatedAt? }` | 201 `{ permission, event, personUpdatedAt }` |
| `POST /api/customers/contact-permissions/{id}/withdraw` | `{ occurredAt, source, reason?, idempotencyKey }` | 200 same mutation envelope |
| `POST /api/customers/contact-permissions/{id}/correct` | `{ supersedesEventId, reason, correctedSource?, correctedOccurredAt?, correctedStatement?, correctedStatementVersion?, correctedEvidenceReference?, correctedEvidenceText?, idempotencyKey }` | 200 same envelope; no permission/basis/destination change |
| `PUT /api/customers/people` | Existing person payload plus `doNotContact?`, `doNotEmail?`, `doNotCall?`; existing `id` and parent version | Existing response plus additive flags and fresh `updatedAt` |
| `POST /api/customers/contact-permissions/evaluate` | `{ personId, channel, purpose, contactPointId?, addressId? }` | `{ allowed, reasonCode, permissionId, evaluatedAt, personUpdatedAt, targetUpdatedAt, permissionUpdatedAt }`, nullable references explicit null |
| `POST /api/customers/people/{id}/emails` | Existing composer body plus optional `purpose` (defaults to `marketing` on activation); mapped targets are server-derived | Existing `{ messageId, threadId, queuedAt }`; denial 409 `{ error, code: 'CRM_OUTREACH_DENIED', reasonCode, recipients: [{ role, inputIndex, reasonCode }] }` |

`permission` is `{ id, personId, channel, purpose, targetKind, contactPointId, addressId, destinationSnapshot, state, legalBasis, evidenceQuality, grantedAt, withdrawnAt, latestEventId, createdAt, updatedAt }`. `event` exposes `{ id, permissionId, eventType, occurredAt, recordedAt, actorUserId, supersedesEventId, source, evidenceReference, legacyEvidenceEventId, reason, snapshot, updatedAt }` only under permission-read ACL. Lists can omit the sensitive snapshot; event detail is available via `?id=` on the events route under identical ACLs.

Withdraw/correct require the projection's `updatedAt` header and current stream check, not the immutable event timestamp. Creating a new assertion in an existing stream additionally requires `permissionId` and its `updatedAt` header; stale grants cannot overwrite withdrawal. When a stream has a withdrawal, the assertion must supply `supersedesWithdrawalEventId` equal to its latest withdrawal event and fresh evidence; persist that reference as `supersedes_event_id`. Missing references return 422; stale references return 409; references outside the authorized same stream return 404. Reject before any append or projection update. POST create checks `personUpdatedAt` and `targetUpdatedAt` under the person/target locks. Optional `legacyEvidenceEventId` must identify an unresolved `legacy_imported` event for the same person/scope; inaccessible or wrong-person references return 404 and a nonlegacy event returns 422. It is stored separately from the same-stream withdrawal reference and cannot replace fresh grant evidence. Server-generated scope/actor/recorded time never come from request fields. Replayed idempotency keys return the prior result only when payloads match, otherwise 409. Evidence strings have explicit Zod limits: source/reason 500/2000, statement/evidenceText 10000, version 100, reference 2000; no URLs are dereferenced. Evidenced consent requires statement or evidence reference plus source and occurred time; other bases additionally require a rationale in `reason`. No `PUT`/`PATCH`/`DELETE` history route exists; return 405.

Evaluation is read-only and cannot authorize a future send. `doNotContact` denies every CRM outreach channel; `doNotEmail` denies email; `doNotCall` denies voice phone, not SMS. Email bounced/invalid and invalid phone also deny; SMS needs its own permitted purpose record. Withdrawal, unknown evidence, missing policy and missing mapping deny in that order after suppression. `reasonCode` is a stable enum: `SUPPRESSED_GLOBAL|SUPPRESSED_CHANNEL|DESTINATION_INVALID|DESTINATION_BOUNCED|WITHDRAWN|PERMISSION_UNKNOWN|POLICY_UNAVAILABLE|RECIPIENT_UNRESOLVED|ALLOWED`.

Person GETs expose `emailBounced` derived from the primary email point. Manual delivery-health changes use an added guarded `POST /api/customers/contact-points/{id}/delivery-status` with `{ deliveryStatus, source, reason?, entityUpdatedAt }`, child version header, parent `.manage` ACL and same scoped command patterns; provider updates use the same command with authenticated integration scope/idempotency and transactional reads of current versions. Response is `{ item, entityUpdatedAt }` using the contact-point shape. This action records the prior/new health and provenance in encrypted command audit snapshots and sets the new delivery timestamp/source. A value edit with unchanged canonical identity preserves those fields and cannot implicitly invoke a health reset. Setting deliverable never grants permission. It is included in this specification because its meaning is needed for the suppression decision.

## 📝 Migration & Backward Compatibility

Run additive schema/encryption migration before a resumable scoped legacy-evidence importer. Each source custom-field value or `subscriber` marker gets a stable migration idempotency key and encrypted provenance snapshot; if both exist, retain both observations in one stream rather than duplicate permission. Record original values and source record version, import time separately, `occurredAt=null` when unknown, `legalBasis=null`, `state=unknown`. `newsletter_opt_in=false` is absence/negative legacy evidence, not proof of a documented withdrawal; retain it without inventing a date. If the person has no valid canonical destination, append a `legacy_imported` event with null `permission_id` and an encrypted unresolved-target source snapshot; create no permission projection and never fabricate an address. The events route also supports `GET /api/customers/contact-permissions/legacy-events?personId=...` with the same permission-read ACL and paginated event envelope. A later verified target assertion references the source observation for audit but does not convert it into a grant automatically. Existing source fields remain readable for at least one minor version.

The classification lifecycle slice preserves `subscriber`; health and renewal never touch it. Any later approved replacement or retirement of that marker must wait for an import receipt, so interrupted imports never lose source evidence. Disable old newsletter UI writes only after the evidence panel ships. Compatibility writes to `newsletter_opt_in` append unverified evidence and never activate permission; old reads retain their legacy value. Record the new authority and bridge in `UPGRADE_NOTES.md`. No silent sync writes a boolean back from a legal-basis assertion.

Policy enforcement activates only after the high-stakes assumption and migration report are reviewed; deploy code/schema first while existing enforcement remains unchanged, with an explicit activation checkpoint. Once activated, rollback may hide the new editor, but must preserve sender suppression and evidence; disabling a failed evaluator would reopen prohibited outreach. Infrastructure failure queues/blocks CRM delivery. Rollback to a binary without enforcement requires pausing CRM outbound queues first. No automatic destructive down migration or history purge.

## 📝 Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

A permission withdrawal between enqueue and sender evaluation denies delivery; a provider-accepted message cannot be recalled. Missing customers registration blocks CRM context and leaves unrelated transactional email operational. All recipients must pass before an atomic enqueue decision; do not send a subset silently. A destination edit invalidates lookup of earlier evidence for the new address; deleting/recreating the old address preserves its withdrawal. Provider bounce events validate tenant/org and destination snapshot, dedupe delivery IDs and never bounce a different newly selected primary.

A database failure rolls back both ledger append and projection update. Side-effect retries use event IDs and do not append duplicate evidence. Ordinary command undo is disabled for ledger assertions/withdrawals; correction or a new evidenced assertion is the compensation. Suppression undo requires current parent version and is audited; it cannot erase history or revive withdrawn permission. Audit redaction/privacy processing must include encrypted command snapshots, not only business rows.

## 📝 Risks & Impact Review

This is a high-impact **implementation** because it can stop previously allowed CRM sends and proposes a shared outbound guard seam across customers/messages/communication_channels. The design-only PR remains draft until the legacy activation policy is confirmed. Fail-closed decisions favor predictable suppression but require actionable blocked-send UI and operational retry/quarantine visibility. It cannot prevent manual off-platform outreach or guarantee a provider cancels an accepted send. The narrow contract avoids coupling auth or transactional sender semantics to CRM policy.

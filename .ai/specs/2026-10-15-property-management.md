## TLDR

**Key Points:**
- A core module for operators of leased and bookable space (coworking, serviced offices). It models **buildings → floors → spaces → units** in metres, draws them to scale, and runs the two ways a unit earns money — **leases** (billed monthly on a schedule) and **bookings** (charged per booking) — with the customer-portal side of both.
- Units are typed and priced by the existing **catalog**: type = category, product = product, and whether a unit is leased or booked is a property of its **product**. Leases and bookings **snapshot the product and price** they were made at; units keep a **product history**, leases a **version history**, and both stay consistent with the `sales` documents that bill them.

**Scope:**
- Property structure: buildings (optional opening hours, availability, archive), floors (size, numbering with ground floor 0, laid out from below/above-ground counts), spaces (non-overlapping rectangles, one default space per floor), units (size + position, drag-and-drop placement, product history), common areas.
- Availability on building / floor / space / unit, inherited downwards: blocks new leases and bookings, **cancels bookings** in the period (customers notified), **flags leases** "Unit unavailable".
- Floor plan (to-scale status view) and floor setup pages.
- Leases on **two paths** chosen on one form: a **lease proposal** (a sales quote with a monthly recurrence that the customer's portal user approves or declines; the unit is reserved meanwhile) or a **direct lease** (a recurring sales order created directly; active as soon as it exists). Every lease is billed by a **recurring sales order** — monthly, one period per month of the term — and sales issues each invoice on the anniversary of the lease start (`.ai/specs/2026-10-21-sales-recurring-orders.md`). Terms are whole months. Product snapshot; version history; extensions and rate changes start a new recurring order. One lease at a time per unit; a company may lease any number of units.
- Bookings: product + price snapshot; only within opening hours and outside unavailable periods; a **sales order per confirmed booking**.
- Customer portal: my bookings, book (review shows the unit on its floor), my leases + extension offers, bell notifications — each gated by a portal feature.

**Out of scope (separately specified or deferred):** credits; payment collection; invoice generation and billing schedules (sales recurring orders, `.ai/specs/2026-10-21-sales-recurring-orders.md`); rent escalation, deposits; uploaded floor-plan backgrounds; non-rectangular geometry; the portal's access, events, invoices, support and extras sections.

**Concerns:**
- The module is large; it ships in seven phases, each independently usable (Implementation Plan).

## Decisions after review

Raised by the review of #7472 and settled by the maintainer (defaults accepted):

- **D1 — Superseded (2026-10-21): whole-month terms on a recurring order.** A lease term is a number of months; its recurring sales order bills one whole month per anniversary of the start date. There is no first- or last-month proration; settling a lease that ends early follows D3.
- **D2 — Portal bell entries via an additive `notifications` API.** The portal bell already exists (`PortalNotificationBell`, `usePortalNotifications`, `customer_accounts/api/portal/notifications*`) and reads `notifications` rows whose recipient is the portal user, but nothing can create one: `notificationService` checks every recipient against staff `users`. This spec adds `createForCustomerUsers` to the `notifications` module (see Portal notifications); it ships with this work (commit `80aed32f6` on this branch), ahead of Phase 7.
- **D3 — Early lease end stays.** `lease.end` ends a lease early by cancelling every recurring order of the lease that reaches past the new end (`sales.orders.recurrence.cancel`), effective the day after the new end date — which may be in the future (notice periods); sales keeps billing until then. The manager picks how the month already invoiced is settled — prorate, credit the whole invoice, or keep it — pre-set to the organization's sales default. Notice and handover stay outside this module.
- **D4 — Proposals in History.** Proposals (new and extension) live in `LeaseProposal`; the History card lists them as rows with no invoices until approved.
- **D5 — Backoffice booking is an API capability only.** A manager can book on a customer's behalf through the API (`customer_user_id` resolved from the booking person's single portal account, else null — see Bookings API); there is no backoffice booking screen in this iteration.
- **D6 — Delete only a never-used building.** A building that ever had a lease or booking is archived, not deleted. "Ever used" counts the building's soft-deleted units too, and the archive preview's `canDelete` follows the same rule. A deleted lease counts only while its delete is still pending (`pending_change`) or while it carries `billing_attention`; a settled delete (never invoiced) also releases the unit for building delete.
- **D7 — Proposal expiry in days.** A proposal stays open for N days (form field, prefilled from the `proposalExpiryDays` setting, default 14); the respond-by date and the quote's `validUntil` derive from it.

## Overview

Operators of flexible workspace sell one building two ways: units leased for months (private offices, company units, parking spaces, dedicated desks) and units booked by the hour or day (meeting rooms, flex desks). Open Mercato has the CRM, the catalog and sales, but no notion of *where* something is sold, how a building is divided, or which unit is occupied or booked today. This module adds that layer for building managers (backoffice) and for tenants and members (customer portal).

> **Market Reference:**
> - **LibreBooking** (OSS resource booking) — blackouts that collide with reservations offer *delete / skip / reject* and notify affected users. **Adopted:** unavailability cancels bookings and notifies. **Rejected:** *reject* — a manager closing a floor for a leak must win; *skip* leaves bookings in a closed room.
> - **Seatsurfing** (OSS desk booking) — drag-and-drop desks on an uploaded floor-plan image. **Adopted:** drag-and-drop placement. **Rejected (deferred):** image-based geometry — this module stores metres so scale, fit and overlap can be validated; a background image is a later layer on top.
> - **ERPNext real-estate apps** (Frappe) — a scheduler issues a Sales Invoice per lease billing cycle; escalation, proration and deposits on the contract. **Adopted:** scheduled invoicing against the existing sales documents (here through sales recurring orders). **Deferred:** escalation, deposits. Terms are whole months (D1).

## Problem Statement

- **No spatial model.** Nothing records buildings, floors, how a floor is divided, or the units on it, so occupancy and availability live in spreadsheets and nobody can check a layout fits.
- **Leasing and booking are disconnected from the catalog and from sales.** What a unit *is* and costs should come from the catalog; what was sold must survive later catalog changes; and the invoices a tenant receives must agree with their lease.
- **Closures are manual.** Taking a building, floor or space out of use means hand-cancelling bookings and remembering which tenants are affected.
- **No self-service.** Tenants and members cannot book, see their lease or answer an extension offer without contacting the manager.

## Proposed Solution

A single core module, `property_management`, delivered in seven phases (structure → leases → bookings → availability → invoicing → floor plan → portal). It owns the spatial model, availability, leases, bookings and their portal pages; it **references** catalog products/categories, CRM companies/people and sales documents by FK id, and reaches other modules only through their commands, events, enrichers and widget injection.

### Design Decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| One umbrella spec, phased (Q1 = A) | The capabilities share one data model (unit) and one availability resolver; phasing keeps each step shippable. |
| Lives in `packages/core/src/modules/property_management` (Q2 = A, confirmed by the maintainer) | Official modules are being consolidated into core. |
| Module id `property_management` (Q7, confirmed by the maintainer) | Domain noun; precedent for non-plural ids: `planner`, `sales`, `catalog`, `wms`, `staff`, `inbox_ops`, `data_sync`. Entities, events and commands stay singular. |
| Opening hours in a **small module-owned table** | Opening hours are one open–close window per weekday per building — a basic setting. Planner's rule engine and its `AvailabilityRulesEditor` are bound to planner's own subjects (`member|resource|ruleset`) and call `/api/planner/availability*` directly, so reusing them would need a planner change for no gain. The module stores the windows itself and computes slots with a few lines of its own code. (Supersedes Q3 = B.) |
| Own bookings table, not `resources` (Q4 = B) | `resources` serves staff-schedulable resources; units need geometry, product snapshots and lease/booking exclusivity. |
| Geometry in metres, rectangles only | Validatable (fit, overlap) and drawn to scale; matches the prototype. Non-rectangular shapes deferred. |
| Leasable/bookable is a **product** property stored in a module-owned mapping (`ProductUse`) | Catalog categories are hierarchical and one category can hold both kinds (Open space: flex desk vs dedicated desk); catalog has no such flag and must not gain module-specific columns. Exposed on products via a response enricher (eudr/wms precedent). |
| Leases on **two paths**, both ending in a **recurring order** | **Lease proposal**: `sales.quotes.create` with `recurrence: { frequency: 'monthly', startsOn, periodCount: termMonths }` and one line (the product, quantity 1, unit price = monthly rate), answered by the customer's portal user; approval runs `sales.quotes.convert_to_order`, which deletes the quote and creates the recurring order with the same schedule. **Direct lease**: `sales.orders.create` with the same recurrence and line, for a lease agreed elsewhere. Either way the lease version points to its **order** (never the quote), and **sales** issues every invoice on the anniversary of the lease start — this module keeps no billing ledger and runs no invoicing worker. |
| **Order per confirmed booking** | One `sales.orders.create` per booking (product + price snapshot). Orders without a source quote are a supported path: `orderCreateSchema` has no quote reference, the backoffice creates orders directly (`/backend/sales/documents/create?kind=order`), and `warranty_claims` does the same from code. |
| Settings in a **module-owned table** | `property_management_settings`, one row per organization — the `sales_settings` / `customer_settings` pattern. `moduleConfigService` is tenant-scoped and shared by 15 modules; giving it an organization scope would mean replacing a unique index on `module_configs` and backfilling rows. |
| Managers see **assigned buildings** only | The requirements limit a manager to the buildings they oversee; organization scope alone cannot. A `BuildingManager` assignment plus a `manage_all` feature for administrators, enforced by one resolver. |
| **Sales operation record** for every sales write | Sales commits on its own and can throw after committing; one durable operation per purpose, locked while in flight and reconciled by metadata lookup, gives exactly-once documents without a sales-side change. |
| Rate changes and extensions start a **new recurring order** | Recurring orders cannot be edited after their first invoice (`.ai/specs/2026-10-21-sales-recurring-orders.md`, R5). A rate correction cancels the current order effective at its next anniversary — the first day the new order serves; sales treats `effectiveOn` as the first day no longer served, so the period starting that day is cut and the one before it was fully served (settlement `keep` settles nothing) — and creates a new one from that day for the remaining months; an extension creates a new order starting the day after the current term ends. Each lease version references its own order, so months already invoiced are never re-billed. |
| One lease at a time **per unit**, any number **per company** | A unit's lease terms never overlap; a tenant company can hold several leases (offices, parking). |
| Sales writes **run on a queue and reconcile forward** | Sales commands commit on their own and can throw after committing. Every sales write is a `SalesOperation`: recorded with its hold in the request, then created on a bounded queue that holds the operation lock through the sales call (two connections per operation, so concurrency stays bounded), reconciled by metadata lookup after any failure, and compensated only when its hold was released meanwhile. |
| Portal audience **by permission** | Portal pages, endpoints, live events and bell entries are gated by customer-role features; recipients are resolved by this module (no bridge filters by feature). |
| Unavailability **cancels bookings, flags leases** | Bookings in the period are cancelled with notification; leases are never ended by it — they are flagged "Unit unavailable" until the period ends. |
| Spaces as partitions | Spaces never overlap; resizing never moves units; a layout with any unit outside its space cannot be saved. |
| Bookings: per-unit row lock, not a DB exclusion constraint | Avoids the `btree_gist` extension dependency; overlap is checked inside the booking command under `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the unit. |

### Alternatives Considered

| Alternative | Why Rejected |
|-------------|-------------|
| Planner rules and editor for opening hours | Editor bound to planner subjects and API; extending planner's subject enum is a frozen-contract change — for a form of seven open/close times. |
| Units as `resources` resources | Different use case (staff scheduling); would drag capacity units, comments and tags in and still lack geometry. |
| Direct invoices without an order | Invoice lines carry no product reference, so lease ↔ invoice consistency would rely on metadata. |
| Billing ledger and invoicing worker in this module | What earlier revisions specified: a ledger, a monthly worker and cross-module recovery around `sales.invoices.create`, which commits on its own. Recurring orders put generation in sales, where invoice and ledger share one transaction. |
| One path only (always quote, or always direct) | Operators need both: tenants who negotiate and approve online, and leases signed on paper that must start billing now. |
| Organization scope in `moduleConfigService` | Changes a service and a unique index shared by 15 modules for one module's needs. |
| Floor plan as an image with hotspots | No scale, no fit/overlap validation, no to-scale portal map. |

### Components

| Component | Responsibility |
|-----------|----------------|
| `lib/geometry.ts` (pure) | `fitsInside(rect, container)`, `overlaps(a, b)`, `firstConflicts(rect, others)`; 0.01 m tolerance; used by commands **and** the drag-and-drop editor (same rules client and server). Positions and sizes in metres have at most 2 decimal places (more is refused with `property_management.errors.metresPrecision`, never rounded); lengths are at least 0.01 m. |
| `lib/floorNumbering.ts` (pure) | `layoutFloors(below, above)` → numbers −below…−1, 0…above−1; `minimumCounts(floors)` from floors that still hold units. |
| `lib/availabilityResolver.ts` | Effective availability of a unit at a time range = its own unavailability ∪ its space's ∪ floor's ∪ building's; building archived ⇒ unavailable. Feeds the "Unit unavailable" lease flag, slot computation and booking guards. |
| `lib/bookingSlots.ts` (pure) | Bookable windows for a unit and date = the building's opening window for that weekday, minus unavailability ranges, minus confirmed bookings, cut into whole-hour slots — all in building-local time. No opening hours ⇒ whole day. |
| `lib/leaseTerm.ts` (pure) | Term arithmetic for whole-month leases: `endsOn(startsOn, months)` with the month-end rule of the recurring-orders spec (R6), months remaining after a date, the next anniversary, and `reservedInterval(lease)` — the dates a lease occupies for conflict checks: its current term, or while an end or start-date change is pending, the union of that term and `pending_change.previous`. |
| `lib/productUse.ts` | Resolves a product's use (`leasable|bookable`) and billing unit; products eligible for a category = direct assignments of the category **and its descendants** (`descendant_ids`). |
| Workers | `property_management.lease_sweep` (daily, concurrency 1): expires unanswered proposals first, then ends leases past `ends_on` (skipping any lease with a `pending_change`, which may still revert, a pending direct extension or an open extension proposal; paging with a cursor on `(ends_on, id)` kept in the sweep cache and wrapping around, at most 20 pages and 500 attempts per run, stopping after 20 failures in a row), and re-enqueues open sales operations (proposal reminders ship with Phase 7: until then the lease sweep neither reminds nor stamps `reminded_at`) through the same cursor as the booking sweep; each step (proposals, ending leases, re-enqueueing operations) runs and reports on its own (`property_management.lease_sweep_step_failed`). `property_management.booking_sweep` (every 15 min): completes past bookings (at most 500 attempts per run, stopping after 20 failures in a row, resuming from a cursor kept in the cache), sends reminders once (`reminder_sent_at`), and re-enqueues sales operations that are not `done`, at most 500 per run, rotating through a cache cursor (see Sales operations). `property_management.unavailability_apply` (concurrency 1, 3 attempts, jobs coalesced per closure): cancels bookings for large unavailability sets in chunks of 100. `property_management.sales_operation` (queue, concurrency `OM_PROPERTY_SALES_CONCURRENCY`, default 4): runs every sales operation (see Sales operations). Scheduled through the optional `schedulerService` in `setup.ts` `seedDefaults` (warranty_claims pattern: stable UUID ids, `try/catch`). Invoices are issued by sales' own recurring-order worker, not here. |
| Time | **Building-local wall-clock time, no timezone setting.** Opening hours, booking times, lease dates and proposal deadlines all mean the building's local time and are stored without a zone (`date`, `time`, `timestamp without time zone`); no form asks for a timezone. "Now" for sweeps, reminders, cancellation deadlines and expiry is the deployment's local clock (`TZ`), which is correct while every building of an installation shares one timezone — the case today. **Deployment requirement:** `TZ` is set to the buildings' timezone (containers default to UTC, which would let a Warsaw customer cancel up to two hours after a booking starts). **Authoritative clock:** "now" is computed in Node as local wall-clock and passed to SQL as a parameter; queries never compare these columns with `now()`/`current_date`, which follow the database session's `TimeZone`. All mutations are commands (singular ids). Undo is specified per command; side effects that cannot be undone are listed.

| `property_management.unavailability.end` (make available) | restore the previous `ended_at` under building → floor(s) → units → unavailability locks, refused `409 unavailability_undo_bookings` while pending or confirmed bookings fall in the restored period; emits `property_management.unavailability.restored` | — |
| `property_management.lease.create` (direct) | none once its order exists — corrections go through `lease.update` / `lease.end`. Before that, cancelling the lease compensates its pending operation | records the lease as `pending_order` (unit held) and its `lease_order:{versionId}` sales operation: `sales.orders.create` with the monthly recurrence (`startsOn` = lease start, `periodCount` = term months) and one line; on finish the lease becomes `active`. Sales issues the invoices |
| `property_management.lease.propose` | `lease_proposal.withdraw` | records the proposal (`sending`) and its `quote:{proposalId}` sales operation, which creates the quote (`sales.quotes.create` with the monthly recurrence, `validUntil` = `respond_by`, `metadata.propertyManagementOperationId`) on the queue; the proposal becomes `sent` and the customer is notified when it is done. Lease `proposed`, unit reserved from the start |
| `property_management.lease.update / delete` | inverse while the change is pending (see Changes to existing orders). `delete` is allowed only before any invoice on **any** of the lease's orders (pre-validated by reading each order's recurrence; `409 lease_invoiced` otherwise — end it instead). It soft-deletes the lease, withdraws its open proposals, and records one `recurrence_cancel:{orderId}:{changeId}` per order, effective the order's start with `credit_full`, so nothing is ever billed. Each cancellation keeps `credit_full` but is recorded with the caller's credit permission (`allow_credit` = the caller holds `sales.credit_memos.manage`), like `lease.end`, so sales refuses a cancellation that would actually credit for a caller without it. The billed versions are re-read under the lease lock, and the delete returns `409 lease_changed` when an activation or extension finished in between; the not-invoiced check is repeated under the lease lock (`409 lease_invoiced`). Orders are **never deleted** — `sales.orders.delete` hard-deletes payments and allocations, and `409 recurring_order_invoiced` guards issued periods only. Orders whose cutoff is already at or before their start (cut entirely, e.g. by an earlier early end) are skipped. **Command context for sales calls.** No sales command is dispatched with a staff request's context: the parent's optimistic-lock header must never reach a child command, and the worker, the portal approval and `sweep:run` have no staff request at all. One helper, `lib/salesCommandContext.ts` → `buildTrustedSalesContext({ tenantId, organizationId })`, builds every sales command's context: no `request`; `auth: null` (so `ensureTenantScope` does not compare a user's tenant); `organizationScope: { tenantId, selectedId: organizationId, filterIds: [organizationId], allowedIds: [organizationId] }` and `selectedOrganizationId: organizationId`, so `ensureOrganizationScope` (`packages/shared/src/lib/commands/scope.ts`) takes the scoped branch — never the legacy no-scope branch that `OM_ENFORCE_ORG_SCOPE_STRICT` turns into `403`. The ids are trusted values: the worker reads them from the `SalesOperation` row (written in the request from the authenticated scope), and the job payload carries only the operation id (`packages/events/AGENTS.md`: never trust scope from a payload); the portal approval takes them from the authenticated customer user's organization; `sweep:run` iterates the organizations that have buildings, including deleted ones (an index-only scan of `property_management_buildings`). Feature checks happen on this module's routes before anything is recorded (see Building access and `leases/{id}/end`).

**Sales operations — one protocol for every write into sales, run on a queue.** Sales commands commit on their own: `sales.quotes.create`, `sales.orders.create` and `sales.orders.recurrence.cancel` fork their own EntityManager and commit before returning, and they can still throw *after* that commit (side effects, cache invalidation — e.g. `sales/commands/documents.ts` ~5240 and ~6378–6438). A thrown dispatch therefore does not prove nothing was created. Every sales document this module creates — proposal quote, lease order, extension order, booking order — and every status change or recurrence cancellation it makes goes through a `SalesOperation` row (invoices and credit memos are issued by sales itself) (Data Models):

1. **Record (in the request)** — in this module's transaction, insert the operation with its unique `subject_key` — keyed by the change that causes it, so a new purpose never collides with a finished one: `quote:{proposalId}`, `lease_order:{versionId}` (activation) or `lease_order:{changeId}` (correction), `extension_order:{versionId}`, `booking_order:{bookingId}`, `recurrence_cancel:{orderId}:{changeId}`, `recurrence_replace:{orderId}:{changeId}`, `order_status:{orderId}:{status}`, `quote_status:{proposalId}:{status}` (the last two set a target state, so repeating one is harmless) — its `payload_hash` (the canonical sales payload), status `pending` and a fresh `token`, together with the hold it guards (booking `pending`, lease `pending_order`, extension version `pending`, proposal `sending`) under the unit lock. Commit, then enqueue job `property_management.sales_operation` with the operation id. The request answers at once with the held state — `202 { id, status: 'pending' }` for a booking (the portal shows *Booking…*), `202 { id, status: 'pending_order' }` for a direct lease (*Activating…*); the result reaches the page through `booking.portal_changed` / the lease detail's refresh. The unique key makes a second attempt for the same purpose find the existing operation instead of starting another — **only when its `payload_hash` matches**; the same key with a different payload is refused (`409 sales_operation_conflict`), never treated as a retry. A `refused` or `compensated` operation is never reused: a retried change is a new change with a new `changeId`.
2. **Own (in the worker)** — the `property_management.sales_operation` worker (queue, concurrency `OM_PROPERTY_SALES_CONCURRENCY`, default 2, capped at `floor(DB_POOL_MAX / 3)`; jobs coalesce by `sales_operation:{operationId}`) opens a transaction and locks the operation row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE NOWAIT`; already locked → the job ends, another owner is alive — except a late-create re-check, which is re-enqueued after 10 s, at most 20 times, and is never coalesced). It keeps that transaction open for steps 3–5, so no other worker, CLI run or retry can own the operation while this one lives, even if it pauses. Sales writes through a second connection and readers fork short-lived ones, so each in-flight operation uses **up to three pooled connections**; running operations only on this bounded queue, with concurrency capped at `DB_POOL_MAX / 3`, keeps that inside the pool (`DB_POOL_MAX`, default 20). The `worker --all` budget planner still counts one connection per job — it has no per-job multiplier field (follow-up for `packages/queue`/`packages/cli`). Request handlers never hold a transaction across a *queued* sales call; the one inline sales call is the proposal approval's `convert_to_order`, which joins the approval's own transaction (see Approving a proposal).
3. **Reconcile, then create** — just before dispatching, re-check the transaction is alive (`SELECT 1` on it) — a lock lost to a dropped connection must not be followed by a write. For document-creating operations, look up a sales document carrying `metadata.propertyManagementOperationId = id` (decrypted read, see Reading sales data). Found → skip creation. Not found → dispatch the sales command with that metadata. Operations that change an existing order are reconciled by reading its state instead: `recurrence_cancel` passes the operation id as the cancel's `idempotencyKey` (sales returns the stored result on a repeat), the `scheduleVersion` read from `GET /api/sales/orders/{id}/recurrence` just before dispatch, and the operation's `allow_credit` as `allowCredit`; `recurrence_replace` and `order_status` compare the order's current recurrence or status with the target and dispatch only when it differs; a `recurrence_cancel` read back after a refusal or a timeout counts as reached when the recurrence is `cancelling` or `cancelled` with the matching effective date (a future cutoff leaves it `cancelling`). If the dispatch throws, look up again before deciding anything: a document that exists is used, never created twice. More than one match is an incident: staff get `sales_operation.duplicated`, the extras are compensated. The kept order's line is read before the extra documents are compensated, so no sales call runs while the unit lock is held. An extra lease or extension order that cannot be cancelled gets a queued `recurrence_cancel` compensation (subject `duplicate_cancel:{orderId}`, `allow_credit = true`), and the lease is flagged `billing_attention` until that compensation succeeds; moving an unbilled recurring order to the cancelled status is not possible, because sales refuses it with `409 recurring_order_use_cancel`. An extra quote whose cancellation fails is retried through a queued `quote_status:{quoteId}:canceled` operation (an existing non-terminal one is re-enqueued, never recorded twice). **Errors are classified:** transient (connection, lock or timeout errors, `5xx`) → the job is retried with backoff and `attempts` grows (on the first transient failure at or after 5 attempts — attempts also count waits for the sales contract and compensation retries — staff, the building's managers plus `manage_all` holders, get `property_management.sales_operation.stalled` and `reportError` is called, once per operation, tracked by a `stalledReported` marker in `last_error`; a failing compensation also raises `sales_operation.stalled` once, from inside the compensation run, for any compensation error except `unavailable`; the attempt is recorded on the operation row taken `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, and an operation that is already terminal is left alone); a stale `scheduleVersion` (`409` from the cancel route) is transient too — the worker re-reads the recurrence and retries; permanent (a sales `4xx` with a code — e.g. `409 recurring_order_locked`, `409 recurring_order_already_cancelled`, `409 recurring_order_invoiced`, `409 recurring_cancel_would_credit`, `422` validation) → the operation becomes `refused` with `last_error` and is never retried (a permanently refused `quote` operation withdraws its `sending` proposal — and a `proposed` lease — and emits `lease_proposal.withdrawn`); every refusal is logged and reported after commit (`reportError`, `property_management.sales_operation_refused`), and a refused booking order cancels the booking (`order_refused`) and notifies the building staff (managers plus `manage_all` holders) with `property_management.booking.order_refused` — a `ZodError` stays permanent on purpose: making it transient would hold the slot forever, since holds are never released by age; "target no longer exists" (`404` on the quote or order) counts as `done` for `quote_status` and `order_status`.
4. **Finish** — still holding the operation lock, lock the unit (leases, bookings) and check the hold still carries the operation's `token`. Valid → store the sales ids, move the hold to its final state (booking `confirmed`, lease `active`, extension version in force with the new `ends_on`, proposal `sent`), mark the operation `done`, commit. Invalid (a closure or a cancellation released the hold meanwhile) → the operation becomes `compensating`.
5. **Compensate** — undo what was created without deleting anything that could hold money: a booking order is moved to the configured cancelled status, a lease or extension order's recurrence is cancelled from its start with `credit_full` (nothing stays billed), a quote is cancelled; mark `compensated`. The hold is released only when sales positively has no document for the operation or the document is compensated (in the cancelled status, or its recurrence cancelled from its start). If compensation fails, the operation stays `compensating`, staff get `lease.order_orphaned` / `booking.order_orphaned`, and the next run retries it. Each failed compensation attempt, except `unavailable`, is logged and reported (`property_management.sales_operation_compensation_failed`), in addition to the one-time stalled alert.

A hold is released only by compensation, a cancellation or a closure — each clears the hold's `token` in the same transaction, which is what makes a late step 4 compensate instead of confirming. Nothing releases a hold for being old: the `booking_sweep` and `lease_sweep` workers only re-enqueue operations that are not `done` (`SKIP LOCKED`, so a live owner is never disturbed). Every conflict check (lease overlap, booking overlap, product change, unavailability preview) treats `pending` bookings, `pending_order` leases and pending extension versions as taken. Postgres must not end idle transactions sooner than the longest sales call: every sales call (writes and reads) is bounded by a timeout of 75% of `DB_IDLE_IN_TRANSACTION_TIMEOUT_MS` (90 s when it is unset or 0) and is preceded by `select 1` on the owning transaction, which resets the idle timer and re-checks that the transaction is alive. A timeout is transient (lookup first, then retry); a create that settles after its timeout re-enqueues the operation. A document-creating operation delivered again after it became terminal looks its documents up again, compensates every document other than `sales_document_id` and sends `sales_operation.duplicated` (outcome `reconciled`). A permanent sales `4xx` on `recurrence_cancel`, `order_status` or `quote_status` is read back first and counts as `done` when the target state is already reached. An invalid hold only marks the operation `compensating` and commits; compensation runs in a fresh run, so no sales call is made while a unit lock is held. Remaining risk: if the process dies after a timeout, a document committed later is caught only when the operation is delivered again.

**Lock order.** Everything that takes more than one lock takes them in this order: sales operation → building → floor(s) in id order → product use (`product_use.set` takes its row `FOR UPDATE`; unit create, moves to another product and their undos take the target product's row `FOR SHARE`) → unit(s) in id order → booking → unavailability (`FOR SHARE`; `unavailability.end` and the worker's chunk bookkeeping take it `FOR UPDATE` in transactions that hold no other lock; the undo of `unavailability.end` takes it `FOR UPDATE` at this place, after the units; the undo of `unavailability.create` takes it `FOR UPDATE` alone) → proposal. Step 1 only *inserts* operation rows (never locks an existing one) while it holds a unit lock; no code locks an existing operation row while holding a building, floor or unit lock. A worker handling a refused change locks the change's other operation rows right after its own, in id order with `NOWAIT`, before any building, floor or unit lock; if one is locked, the run is retried, which re-sends the refused sales call (harmless, since it was refused).

**Changes to existing orders — pending, reverted on a permanent refusal.** `lease.end`, a rate correction, a start-date change and `lease.delete` commit a lease change first and change sales afterwards, so the lease change is held as **pending** until its operations finish: the lease stores `pending_change` (`{ kind: end|delete|correction|reschedule, changeId, previous, operationIds }` — `previous` holds the values the change replaced: `ends_on`, `starts_on`, `status`, `current_version_id`), the new version (if any) is `pending`, the lease detail shows *Billing update pending*, and a second change to the same lease is refused (`409 lease_change_pending`) until it settles. A change's operations run **in a fixed order — creations first, cancellations last**: only the first is enqueued; finishing one enqueues the next (`sequence + 1` of the same `change_id`), and the worker defers any *pending* operation whose predecessor is not `done` (compensations are never deferred), so a re-enqueue by `booking_sweep` cannot run them out of order. When all are `done`, `pending_change` is cleared and the version comes into force. When one is `refused`: the change's creations that already finished are compensated (step 5), and **only once every compensation is `compensated`** does the lease revert to `previous`, the pending version become `reverted`, and the building's managers get `lease.billing_change_refused` naming the sales error; while a compensation is still failing, the change stays pending and the lease is flagged `billing_attention`. A refusal can leave earlier *cancellations* of the same change in place only when two orders were cancelled and the second was refused — sales has no un-cancel — in which case the lease keeps the new end, is flagged `billing_attention`, and staff are notified; pre-validation makes this reachable only through a concurrent edit in the sales backoffice. `billing_attention` is also set by a refused activation while the lease is still `pending_order`, never by a refused quote; it is cleared when a compensation succeeds or a change completes and no other operation of the lease is still `compensating` (an irreversible refusal keeps it until a later change completes). A later finished extension order or sent quote of the lease clears `lastBillingRefusal` unless that refusal still holds billing attention; extension refusals are recorded with `kind: 'extension'` and never hold billing attention. While a change is pending, every other lease change, extension (direct or proposed) and proposal approval is refused with `409 lease_change_pending`, and `lease_sweep` leaves the lease alone — as it does while a direct extension is pending or an extension proposal is open (proposals are expired before leases are ended). **A pending change keeps the dates it may restore:** while an end or start-date change is pending, every conflict check that reads lease dates (lease and proposal overlap, extensions, product changes, the unavailability preview, available units) uses `reservedInterval` — the union of the previous and the new term — so the months an end appears to release stay taken until sales confirms it. Finishing the change (the union collapses to the new term) and reverting it (`previous` is restored) take the lease's unit lock, the same lock every lease or proposal admission takes, so a revert can never overlap a lease admitted meanwhile. A correction changes no dates, so it keeps no union; a deleted lease releases its unit at once, and restoring a refused delete re-checks the unit (other leases, proposals and holding bookings in the previous term) under the unit lock before it clears `deleted_at` (see `lease.delete` above).

**Reading sales data.** Everything this module needs about invoices comes from sales' own API (`GET /api/sales/orders/{id}/recurrence` for a lease order's periods, invoices and credit memos — the History card and the lease detail use it). Sales offers no lookup by metadata, so the **operation lookup** reads the sales entities directly — an exception to "only through commands, events and enrichers": `findWithDecryption` on `SalesOrder` / `SalesQuote`, narrowed by organization, tenant and `deleted_at is null`, `created_at ≥ operation.created_at − 1 day`, and the payload's `channel_id` and `customer_entity_id`; the decrypted `metadata.propertyManagementOperationId` is matched in memory, in pages of 100 (keyset on `created_at, id`), with at most 1000 candidates; past the cap the lookup stops and reports `property_management.sales_document_lookup_capped`. (`metadata` is encrypted when tenant data encryption is on, so it cannot be filtered in SQL.) **Remaining raw reads (decision 2026-11-18):** every other direct read selects plaintext columns only and is an interim until the sales recurrence API ships; each runs on the caller's transaction connection (`em.getTransactionContext()`) — except the `sales_settings` read and its `information_schema` probe in `lib/salesRecurrence.ts`, which still run on a fork (`em.fork()`) outside the caller's transaction (known gap, see the round-11 changelog) — never writes and never joins into this module's tables: `sales_orders` / `sales_quotes` (id, number, status, status_entry_id, deleted_at); `sales_order_lines.id`; `sales_order_recurrences` / `sales_recurring_periods`; `sales_invoices.invoice_number` and `sales_credit_memos.credit_memo_number`; `sales_channels.id`; `dictionaries` / `dictionary_entries` ids; `sales_settings.recurring_cancel_settlement` plus its `information_schema` probe; the auth ACL tables, ids, `features_json` and `is_super_admin` only (`lib/staffRecipients.ts`); and `catalog_products` ids for the unit product picker (`lib/productUse.ts`; the catalog module declares no encryption map, so the read and its title/subtitle/description/sku/handle search predicate are plaintext). The approval's quote lock and line read (below, Phase 7) follow the same rule. The sales reads live in `lib/salesLookups.ts`. The recurrence read falls back to `invoicesAvailable: false` with no periods only when `sales_recurring_periods` is missing (Postgres `42P01`); every other error propagates. Inside a caller transaction the periods read runs behind a savepoint (`property_management_recurring_periods`), rolled back on any error, so a missing table cannot abort the caller's transaction. The order status entry is looked up by the dictionary's `normalized_value` (trimmed and lower-cased, as sales does).

**Approving a proposal — one transaction, every check before conversion.** `sales.quotes.convert_to_order` accepts the caller's `ctx.transactionalEm`, so the conversion joins this module's transaction; but the command bus runs a command's post-commit work (events, indexing, cache invalidation) as soon as it returns when it runs on a caller's transaction, so nothing may fail *after* the conversion. Approval therefore checks everything first:

1. Open a transaction; lock the proposal and the unit, then the proposal's quote row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE` through `lib/salesLookups.ts`) — the same lock conversion takes, so a concurrent quote-line edit waits or aborts.
2. Re-check: the proposal is still answerable (`sent`, or `declined` before `respond_by`); the unit is available with no other lease, open proposal or pending hold overlapping the term; the quote's lines **and its recurrence** (frequency, start, anchor day, number of periods), read under that lock, match the proposal, and the quote's customer (`customerEntityId`) is still the lease's `customer_company_id` — a customer-only edit in sales changes neither lines nor schedule, and conversion copies the quote's customer into the order — otherwise `409 quote_changed`; and the start date has not passed (sales refuses that too, `422 recurring_quote_started`).
3. Dispatch `sales.quotes.convert_to_order` with the transaction's EntityManager. Conversion does not check the quote's status, so the quote is not flipped first; it is deleted by the conversion.
4. Activate the lease (or write the `extended` version and move `ends_on`), store the order and order-line ids, mark the proposal `approved`, record the sales operation `order_status:{orderId}:confirmed` (an idempotent `sales.orders.update` to the confirmed status) and commit; the operation runs on the queue. Sales issues the invoices on the term's anniversaries.

A failure in steps 1–2 changes nothing. After step 3 only this module's own writes remain; if one fails (an infrastructure error), the transaction rolls back but the conversion's post-commit events have already been emitted for an order that does not exist — a documented, alerting edge case (`lease_proposal.approval_failed` staff notification), not a path the design relies on.

**Layout writes — building, then floor locks, shared with closures.** Every command that changes geometry — creating, moving, resizing or deleting a unit, space or common area, resizing a floor — first locks the building row, then the floor row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`), then reads the floor's spaces, units and common areas and validates against them. Units move only within their floor (another space or position); moving a unit to another floor is not supported yet (`422 unit_floor_move_unsupported`). `floor.update` and its undo bump the building's `updated_at`, so a stale building form (which sends the whole layout) gets `409` instead of reverting floor edits — kept by design, because every floor field `floor.update` edits is part of the building form's layout payload; building archive, delete and `layout_replace` check the optimistic-lock version after taking the building lock; unit, space and common-area update/delete and `floor.update` check the record's own version against the row read after taking the floor lock. `floor.layout_replace`, which creates and removes floors, takes the same building lock and then its floors in id order, so writers of floors that do not exist yet are serialized too. `unavailability.create` (confirm) takes the same building → floor(s) locks before it enumerates the units under its subject (see `unavailabilities`), so a unit move and a closure are serialized: a move either commits first and the closure then finds the unit in its new place, or waits and then sees the closure. **A move must not carry bookings into a closure:** after the locks, a move whose destination (space, floor) has an unavailability overlapping any `pending` or `confirmed` booking of the unit — the unit's own unavailability is unchanged by a move, the building's is the same on both sides — is refused with `409 unit_move_destination_unavailable` listing those bookings; the manager cancels them or ends the closure first. A leased unit may move into an unavailable space: the lease is then flagged *Unit unavailable* by the resolver, as with any closure.

**Events** (`createModuleEvents`, `module.entity.action`, past tense) — frozen once released:
`property_management.building.{created,updated,archived,deleted}`, `.floor.{created,updated,deleted}`, `.space.{created,updated,deleted}`, `.unit.{created,updated,deleted,product_changed}`, `.common_area.{created,updated,deleted}`, `.unavailability.{created,ended,restored}`, `.lease.{created,updated,activated,extended,ended,deleted,order_orphaned}`, `.lease_proposal.{sent,withdrawn,approved,declined,expired}`, `.booking.{created,cancelled,completed,order_orphaned}`. `lease_proposal.approved` and `lease_proposal.declined` are declared with `excludeFromTriggers: true` until Phase 7 (portal approve and decline) emits them; Phase 7 removes the flag. These **domain events are always emitted** — for portal and backoffice actions alike (a manager's booking with no `customer_user_id` included) — and none of them is `portalBroadcast`, so search indexing, persistent subscribers and workflows see every change. Lease, proposal and booking lifecycle events — and the building, unit-product and unavailability events — are emitted `persistent: true` with the trusted `tenantId` / `organizationId` in the emit options (the bus and the workflow trigger read scope from the options, not the payload). The `building.updated` and `floor.updated` payloads carry `nameChanged: boolean` (additive); the dependent unit search reindex runs only on a rename, and once per save — a building rename saved with its layout reindexes through `building.updated` only, its `floor.updated` events carry `nameChanged: false`.

**Portal events** are separate, portal-only ids, following the `warranty_claims.claim.portal_status_changed` / `staff.timesheets.time_report.portal_published` precedent: `property_management.booking.portal_changed`, `.lease.portal_changed`, `.lease_proposal.portal_changed` (payload: the record id and what changed — created, cancelled, reminder, sent, withdrawn, expired, extended, ended). Each has `portalBroadcast: true` and `excludeFromTriggers: true`, and is emitted only through one helper, `emitPortalEvent`, which requires a non-empty `recipientUserIds` and emits nothing otherwise. This matters because the portal stream treats a payload without recipients as organization-wide (`customer_accounts/api/portal/events/stream.ts` `matchesAudience`): a portal event must never leave without a recipient list. Payloads carry ids only; the page refetches through its feature-gated endpoint.

**Notifications** (`notifications.ts`): portal recipients — `property_management.booking.reminder`, `.booking.cancelled`, `.lease_proposal.sent`, `.lease_proposal.reminder`; staff recipients — `.lease_proposal.answered`, `.lease.order_orphaned`, `.lease.billing_change_refused`, `.lease.document_status_refused`, `.booking.order_orphaned`, `.booking.order_cancel_refused`, `.booking.order_refused`, `.sales_operation.stalled` (sent with `createBatch` to explicit staff user ids: the building's managers from `BuildingManager` plus holders of `property_management.buildings.manage_all` in the same organization, resolved with `rbacService.userHasAllFeatures` in that organization's scope — wildcard-aware, so `*` and `property_management.*` grants count — the building's managers always, and `manage_all` holders checked over at most 200 candidates; above that the list is truncated and a warning logged, never skipped (unlike `createForFeature`, which returns no recipients past its cap). `createForFeature({ restrictRecipientsToOrganization: true })` would already narrow to the organization, but knows nothing of building assignments; `sales_operation.duplicated`, `lease_proposal.approval_failed` and `lease.billing_change_refused` for a lease flagged `billing_attention` go to `manage_all` holders). Portal notifications are `Notification` rows whose recipient is the customer user (as read by `customer_accounts/api/portal/notifications.ts`), created with the new `createForCustomerUsers` and addressed to the same recipients as the portal events.

### Billing (sales recurring orders)

- Billing is owned by sales (`.ai/specs/2026-10-21-sales-recurring-orders.md`). Each lease term is one recurring sales order: frequency monthly, start = lease start, number of periods = term months, one line — the leased product at the monthly rate.
- Sales issues each invoice on the anniversary of the lease start, in advance, for the month that starts that day (a lease starting on the 31st follows the month-end rule). There is no invoice-day setting and no proration of first or last months.
- Ending early cancels the recurrence of every order of the lease that reaches past the new end, with the settlement the manager picks (D3); sales issues any credit memo.
- Rate changes and extensions start a new recurring order (Design Decisions); each lease version references its own order, and the History card lists each version's invoices by reading them from sales (`sales.recurring_order.invoice_issued` keeps the card fresh).
- This module has no billing ledger, no invoicing worker and no billing CLI.

## Data Models

All tables `property_management_*`, UUID PKs, `organization_id` + `tenant_id` on every row, `created_at`, `updated_at` (optimistic locking, default ON), `deleted_at` where soft-deletable. Two tables have no `updated_at`, by design: `BuildingManager` (an assignment row, replaced as a set by the building form, which is locked on the building's own `updated_at`) and `UnitProductPeriod` (append-only history; a period is closed by setting `valid_to` under the unit lock, never edited by a user). Lengths in metres: `numeric(7,2)`; positions are a rectangle's top-left corner from the floor's top-left. Only the unique fields are listed.

### Building
`name`, `address` (text), `status` (`active|archived`), `archived_at`, `opening_hours_enabled` (bool). Index `(organization_id, status)`.

### Floor
`building_id`, `number` (int; ground = 0, below ground negative), `name`, `size_x`, `size_y`. Unique `(building_id, number)` where not deleted. Floor counts (below/above ground) are derived, never stored.

### Space
`floor_id`, `name`, `purpose` (text, nullable), `x`, `y`, `width`, `depth`, `is_default` (bool). Invariant: inside the floor, no overlap with other spaces of the floor. Every floor is created with one default space covering it.

### Unit
`floor_id`, `space_id`, `name`, `catalog_category_id`, `catalog_product_id`, `x`, `y`, `width`, `depth`. Invariants: fully inside its space; no overlap with another unit or common area of the floor; product assigned to the category or one of its descendants. Indexes `(organization_id, floor_id)`, `(organization_id, space_id)`, `(organization_id, catalog_product_id)`.

### UnitProductPeriod (append-only)
`unit_id`, `catalog_product_id`, `product_name`, `use` (`leasable|bookable`), `billing_unit` (`month|hour|day`), `price_amount`, `currency_code`, `price_kind_id`, `valid_from`, `valid_to` (nullable = current). Exactly one open period per unit (partial unique index). Index `(organization_id, unit_id, valid_from)`; partial index `property_management_unit_product_periods_open_product_idx` `(organization_id, catalog_product_id) where valid_to is null` (`Migration20261118120000_property_management`) serves the open-period lookup by product. Append-only: no `updated_at` (see the Data Models preamble).

### CommonArea
`floor_id`, `name`, `kind` (`corridor|kitchen|toilets|lobby|stairs_lifts|storage|other`), `x`, `y`, `width`, `depth`. Inside the floor; no overlap with units or other common areas; spaces may overlap it.

### ProductUse
`catalog_product_id` (unique per organization), `use` (`leasable|bookable`), `billing_unit` (`month` for leasable; `hour|day` for bookable).

### OpeningHours
`building_id`, `weekday` (0 = Monday … 6 = Sunday), `opens_at`, `closes_at` (`time`, building-local; `closes_at > opens_at`, `closes_at` may be `24:00` for open until midnight, no overnight windows). Unique `(building_id, weekday)`; a missing weekday means closed that day. Replaced as a set by `building.update`. Ignored when the building's `opening_hours_enabled` is false.

### Unavailability
`subject_type` (`building|floor|space|unit`), `subject_id`, `reason_entry_id` (dictionary), `reason_text`, `starts_at`, `ends_at` (nullable = open-ended), `ended_at` (set when made available early), `cancelled_booking_count`, `apply_status` (`applied|applying`). Index `(organization_id, subject_type, subject_id, starts_at)`. `subject_id` is polymorphic, so the command loads the subject with the caller's `organization_id` before inserting — never trusts the id alone.

### Lease
`reference` (unique per org, `L-YYYY-NNN`, issued from a per-organization counter row locked `FOR UPDATE`), `unit_id`, `customer_company_id`, `primary_contact_person_id`, `status` (`proposed|pending_order|active|declined|withdrawn|expired|ended`), `starts_on`, `term_months`, `ends_on` (derived from `starts_on` + `term_months`), `current_version_id`, `hold_token`, `sales_order_id` (denormalized from the current version — every version keeps its own order and order line; ending and deleting act on every version's order, never only this one), `pending_change` (jsonb, nullable — see Changes to existing orders), `billing_attention` (bool). Leases of one unit (active, about to start, or `proposed` with an open proposal — a proposal reserves the unit) **must not have overlapping terms** (checked under the unit row lock) — so the next tenant's lease can be signed while the current one runs. A lease with a pending end or start-date change occupies the union of its previous and new term (`reservedInterval`, see Changes to existing orders) until the change finishes or reverts. A company may hold any number of leases on different units. Creating or approving a lease also fails while confirmed bookings fall inside the term. "Unit unavailable" is **derived** (resolver) and returned by the list API, filterable. Index `(organization_id, unit_id, status)`; the lease sweep uses `property_management_leases_sweep_idx` `(organization_id, status, ends_on, id)` (`Migration20261117223143_property_management`).

### SalesOperation
`kind` (`quote|quote_status|lease_order|extension_order|booking_order|order_status|recurrence_cancel|recurrence_replace`), `subject_key` (unique per organization), `payload_hash`, `change_id` (nullable — groups the operations of one lease change, run in `sequence` order), `sequence`, `target_type`, `target_id`, `status` (`pending|done|refused|compensating|compensated|failed`), `token` (uuid, also written on the hold it guards), `allow_credit` (bool; `recurrence_cancel` only — the requesting user's `sales.credit_memos.manage`, or `true` for system cancellations), `sales_document_type`, `sales_document_id`, `attempts`, `last_error`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. Index `property_management_sales_operations_lease_idx` `(organization_id, lease_id, status)` (`Migration20261117205358_property_management`) serves the per-lease open-operation reads. Every sales document this module creates carries `metadata.propertyManagementOperationId`. Not user-editable; listed for staff on the lease and booking detail when not `done`.

### BuildingManager
`building_id`, `user_id` (staff user). Unique `(building_id, user_id)`. Assigned on the building form; a building may have several managers and a manager several buildings.

### LeaseReferenceCounter
`organization_id` (unique with `tenant_id`), `year`, `last_number`. Locked `FOR UPDATE` when a lease is created; the reference is `L-{year}-{last_number + 1}` zero-padded to 3 digits. Not user-editable.

### PropertyManagementSettings
One row per organization (unique `(organization_id, tenant_id)`): `billing_channel_id` (nullable — no default), `cancelled_order_status_entry_id` (nullable; required before bookings, since booking orders are cancelled, never deleted), `lease_ending_threshold_days` (default 60), `booking_reminder_minutes` (default 60), `proposal_expiry_days` (1–90, default 14). Missing row = defaults. Written by `property_management.settings.save`.

### LeaseVersion (append-only)
`lease_id`, `version_no`, `event` (`activated|corrected|extended|rescheduled`), `state` (`pending|in_force|reverted`), `catalog_product_id`, `product_name`, `monthly_rate`, `currency_code`, `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `sales_order_id`, `sales_order_line_id`, `effective_from`. The product is copied from the unit's open `UnitProductPeriod` when the lease is created or proposed, and never changes afterwards.

### LeaseProposal
`lease_id`, `kind` (`new|extension`), `sales_quote_id` (until approval — the quote is deleted on conversion), `sales_order_id` (after approval), `starts_on`, `ends_on`, `monthly_rate`, `currency_code`, `message` (encrypted), `expires_in_days`, `respond_by` (= sent date + `expires_in_days`, end of that local day; also the quote's `validUntil`), `status` (`sending|sent|approved|declined|withdrawn|expired`), `answered_at`, `answered_by_customer_user_id`, `reminded_at`. A proposal is `sending` from the request until its `quote` operation finishes (→ `sent`) or is compensated. Partial unique: one proposal per lease in status `sending`, `sent` or `declined` (a time-free predicate); the commands' open-proposal check counts the same statuses under the unit lock, so a proposal whose quote is still being created is open; the expiry worker moves both to `expired` once `respond_by` passes, and approval re-checks `respond_by` itself. Shown in the lease History as rows with no invoices until approved.

### Booking
`unit_id`, `customer_company_id` (nullable for members), `booked_by_person_id`, `customer_user_id` (nullable — backoffice-made bookings), `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `status` (`pending|confirmed|completed|cancelled`; `pending` only between the hold and the order link, and it already blocks the slot), `hold_token` (the guarding sales operation's token; cleared when the hold is released), `cancel_reason` (`customer|manager|unavailability|order_refused`; `order_refused` when sales refuses the booking order for good), `unavailability_id`, `catalog_product_id`, `product_name`, `unit_price`, `quantity`, `billing_unit`, `total_amount`, `currency_code`, `sales_order_id`, `reminder_sent_at`, `notes` (encrypted). Indexes `(organization_id, unit_id, starts_at)`, `(organization_id, customer_user_id, starts_at)`, `(organization_id, status, starts_at)`, `property_management_bookings_building_idx` `(organization_id, building_id, starts_at)` (`Migration20261111195427_property_management`), `property_management_bookings_unavailability_idx` `(organization_id, unavailability_id)` (`Migration20261112225910_property_management`) and the sweep cursor index `property_management_bookings_sweep_idx` `(organization_id, status, ends_at, id)` (`Migration20261112235012_property_management`).

**Booking rules (MVP):** slots start on the hour; an hourly booking is 1–10 whole hours, quantity = hours; a daily booking (flex desk) is one day from opening to closing, quantity 1; a booking never crosses midnight or the building's opening hours; price = `catalogPricingService` price for the product × quantity, snapshotted. Customers may cancel until the booking starts; no cancellation fee in this iteration.

### Encryption (`encryption.ts`)
`Booking.notes`, `LeaseProposal.message` — free text written to or about people. Read through `findWithDecryption`. Building addresses are business addresses, not personal data.

### Custom entities (`ce.ts`)
`property_management:building`, `:unit`, `:lease` declared (with `E.property_management.*` ids) so operators can add custom fields. Building, unit and lease create/update accept `customFields` (or `cf_*` / `cf:*` keys, lifted by `liftCustomFields`), including the custom lease `POST`; the values are written inside the entity's own transaction, so they commit with it; list responses return `customValues` / `customFields` (`decorateCustomFields` with `stripPrefixedKeys`); update undos restore them (`buildCustomFieldResetMap`; older log entries without `custom` restore nothing). The building, unit and new-lease forms show a custom-fields group. Deviation: lease custom fields cannot be edited after creation and the lease detail page does not show them yet.

## API Contracts

Standard CRUD (`makeCrudRoute`, zod, `openApi`, `updatedAt` in responses, `pageSize ≤ 100`) for `buildings`, `floors`, `spaces`, `units`, `common-areas`, `product-uses`, `leases`, `bookings` (list + detail; create/cancel are custom). The building-scoped list routes (buildings, floors, spaces, common-areas, units, leases, bookings) set `disableListCache: true`, so with `ENABLE_CRUD_API_CACHE` a manager removed from a building stops seeing its rows at once; list `search` is at most 200 characters. Only non-CRUD contracts are listed.

### `POST /api/property_management/buildings/{id}/floors/layout`
`{ below: int ≥ 0, above: int ≥ 1, floors: [{ number, name, sizeX, sizeY }] }` → `200 { floors }`. `422` with `{ minimumAbove, minimumBelow, blockingFloor }` when a removed floor still holds units. Optimistic lock on the building.

### `GET /api/property_management/floors/{id}/layout?date=YYYY-MM-DD`
The response always carries `crmAccess: { people, companies }`: `lease.contactName`, `lease.contactEmail` and `bookings[].personName` are present only with `customers.people.view`, `lease.companyName`, `proposal.companyName` and `bookings[].companyName` only with `customers.companies.view` (wildcard-aware, resolved once per request); otherwise the keys are omitted and the ids stay. One call for the floor plan and setup pages: `building`, `floor`, the building's `floors`, `spaces`, `units` (rect, category and product names, `use`, `hasBlockers`) and `commonAreas`. Availability is not part of the setup payload (the setup page reads `GET unavailabilities?buildingIds=…&active=true` for its switches and hatching). When `date` is given the response also carries `date`, `leaseEndingThresholdDays` and `unavailableAreas` (`[{ subjectType: building|floor|space, subjectId, reasonLabel, reasonText, from, until }]`, the areas of this floor unavailable during the day, for hatching), and every unit carries: `status` (`occupied|available|unavailable|booked|free`); `lease` (`{ id, reference, status, startsOn, endsOn, daysLeft, customerCompanyId, companyName, contactPersonId, contactName, contactEmail, extensionRespondBy, extensionStatus: sending|sent|declined|null }`, the lease in force on the date, `daysLeft` counted from it); `leaseEnding` (0 ≤ `daysLeft` ≤ `leaseEndingThresholdDays`); `proposed` and `proposal` (`{ id, leaseId, reference, status: sending|sent|declined|null, respondBy, startsOn, endsOn, customerCompanyId, companyName }`, an open new-lease proposal whose term has not ended); `bookings` (the day's bookings in start order with person and company); `unavailable` (`{ source: building|floor|space|unit|building_archived, reasonLabel, reasonText, from, until }`; `reasonLabel` is the dictionary reason's label, or null when the closure names none); `reservation` (`{ leaseId, reference, until }` when no lease is in force but a pending end or reschedule still reserves the unit under its previous term). The status part takes 6 queries, independent of unit count, and a 7th (the reason labels) when any current or future closure of the building, the floor, its spaces or units names a dictionary reason — closures are read from the day onwards, not only those in force on it (see Phase 6 deviations).

### `POST /api/property_management/unavailabilities?dryRun=true|false`
`{ subjectType, subjectId, reasonEntryId?, reasonText: string(1..200), startsAt, endsAt?, previewToken? }`. `startsAt` cannot be before today (`422 unavailability_start_in_past`); the dialog's From picker starts on, and offers nothing before, the deployment-local today that `GET unavailability-reasons` returns as `today`, and uses the browser's date only until that response arrives. Dry run → `200 { units, bookingsToCancel: [{id, unit, startsAt, person}], leasesToFlag: [{id, reference, company, unit}], previewToken }` (`previewToken` = hash of the subject, the range and the affected booking ids). The dry run also returns `crmAccess: { people, companies }`. `bookingsToCancel[].person` is present only with `customers.people.view` and `leasesToFlag[].company` only with `customers.companies.view` (wildcard-aware, resolved once per request). Otherwise those keys are omitted, the booking and lease ids stay, and no CRM record is read. Confirm must send the `previewToken`; if the affected set changed since the preview → `409 preview_stale` and the dialog re-previews. Confirm → `201 { id, cancelledBookings, queuedBookings, flaggedLeases, applyStatus, progressJobId }`: up to 200 bookings are cancelled in the request; more are queued to `unavailability_apply` (chunks of 100, progress in the top bar; `queuedBookings` counts them and `progressJobId` is the top-bar job, `null` when nothing is queued or the progress module is absent) while the unavailability row itself takes effect immediately. Confirm first locks the subject's building row and its floor row(s) in id order — the locks every layout write takes (see Layout writes) — then enumerates the units under the subject and locks their rows (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, in id order) before inserting the row — the same unit lock `booking.create` takes — so a concurrent booking either commits first and is cancelled with the rest, or waits and then sees the unavailability and is refused, and a concurrent unit move cannot enter or leave the subject between enumeration and insert. `409 subject_archived` on confirm when the subject's building is archived (checked under the building lock); the dry run does not refuse an archived building. When no cancelled order status is configured and an affected booking has an order or is still `pending`, both the dry run and the confirm refuse up front with `422 cancelled_status_missing` (one SQL flag over every affected booking, queued ones included — `needs_cancelled_status` in `summarizeClosureBookings`; the preview token is unchanged), so the dialog shows the settings hint before anything is cancelled. An invalid body is `422` with translated `fieldErrors` (`property_management.*` keys translated, anything else the translated *Invalid input*) and no raw zod `issues`. Requires `property_management.availability.manage`.

### `POST /api/property_management/unavailabilities/{id}/end` → `200` (make available now).

### `GET /api/property_management/units/{id}/slots?date=YYYY-MM-DD`
`{ openingHours: [{start, end}] | null, slots: [{ start, end, state: 'free'|'booked' }], price: { amount, currency, billingUnit }, billingChannelConfigured }`. `price` is the price of one billing unit (quantity 1, one hour or one day); a booking resolves its price for its own quantity, so with tiered catalog prices the booked unit price can differ from the slot price. Until the billing sales channel is chosen in the settings, `billingChannelConfigured` is false and `price.amount` and `price.currency` are null — creating a booking is refused with `422 billing_channel_missing` until then. Backoffice and portal (portal variant scoped to bookable units).

### `POST /api/property_management/bookings` · `POST …/bookings/{id}/cancel` (backoffice)
Create: `{ unitId, startsAt, endsAt, bookedByPersonId, customerCompanyId?, customerUserId?, notes? }` → `booking.create` (same rules, price snapshot and order as the portal; the booking total is unit price × quantity, computed in decimal by `lib/money.ts`). `customerUserId`, when passed, must be an active portal user of the organization whose person is the booking person and whose company is empty or the booked company — and empty when no company is booked (`422 customerUserMismatch`) — `422` otherwise. When it is omitted, the booking person's portal account is linked if exactly one active `CustomerUser` of the organization has `personEntityId` = the person and a `customerEntityId` that is empty or the booked company (only portal users with no company when no company is booked); with none or several it stays null, so a caller that knows the account passes it. Without the `customer_accounts` module nothing is resolved and a passed `customerUserId` is `422 customerUserNotFound` → `202 { id, status: 'pending', operationId }` (`operationId` is the queued sales operation that creates the order); `409` slot taken / outside opening hours / unit unavailable; `422 booking_price_missing` when no price resolves for the unit's product; `422` with field error `bookingPersonNotInCompany` on `bookedByPersonId` when a `customerCompanyId` is passed and the person does not belong to that company (the person–company membership check filters by organization and tenant). Cancel → `booking.cancel` with `cancel_reason = manager` (the optimistic lock is checked against the row locked inside the transaction) → `200 { ok: true, id, status: 'cancelled', operationId }` (`operationId` is the queued sales operation that cancels the order, `null` when none was queued); `409 booking_not_cancellable` (with the current `status`) when the booking is no longer pending or confirmed; `409 booking_already_ended` when the booking's end (building-local wall clock) is at or before now, even while the booking sweep has not yet marked it `completed` (the same `endsAt <= now` comparison as the sweep). It applies only to `reason: manager`; a customer cancel keeps its `409 booking_already_started` rule and a closure cancel is unaffected. `422 cancelled_status_missing` when no cancelled order status is configured and the booking has an order or is still `pending`. Both require `property_management.bookings.manage`. No backoffice booking screen in this iteration (D5).

`GET /api/property_management/bookings` returns a top-level `crmAccess: { people, companies }` next to `items`, also on an empty page. `items[].bookedByName` is present only with `customers.people.view` and `items[].companyName` only with `customers.companies.view` (wildcard-aware, resolved once per request). Otherwise the keys are omitted, `bookedByPersonId` and `customerCompanyId` stay, and the customers query is skipped. Deleted CRM records are never named (`null`). Each item carries `cancellable: boolean` (additive, review round 15): true when the booking is pending or confirmed and its end is still after the deployment-local now — the same `endsAt <= now` comparison that makes the manager cancel return `409 booking_already_ended` (`lib/bookingCancel.ts`, shared by `summarizeBookings` and `booking.cancel`).

### `POST /api/property_management/units/{id}/product`
`{ catalogProductId }` → closes the open product period and opens a new one. `409` while the unit has an active lease (product locked); allowed with upcoming bookings.

### `POST /api/property_management/leases` (custom create)
`{ unitId, customerCompanyId, primaryContactPersonId?, startsOn (for a proposal: on or after its respond-by date, since sales refuses a recurring quote accepted after its start), termMonths: int ≥ 1, monthlyRate, asProposal: boolean, expiresInDays?: int 1..90 (proposal only; defaults to the `proposalExpiryDays` setting), message? }`. `asProposal: false` → `lease.create` → `202 { id, status: 'pending_order' }` (the lease is `active` once its recurring order exists; sales invoices it from the start date); `true` → `lease.propose` → `202 { id, proposalId, status: 'sending' }`. `422` if the unit is unavailable for new leases; `409` if another lease or open proposal of the unit overlaps the term. Every referenced id — unit, `customerCompanyId`, `primaryContactPersonId` (which must also belong to that company), the unit's catalog product — is loaded within the caller's organization; anything else is `422`, never a cross-organization reference. `monthlyRate` is a number or a decimal string, greater than 0 (at least 0.0001) and at most 999,999,999, with at most 4 decimal places; a value with more decimals is refused (`property_management.errors.rateTooPrecise`), never rounded. It is normalized to a canonical decimal string, stored, and sent to sales as `unitPriceNet`. An invalid body is `400 { error, fieldErrors }`: messages that are `property_management.*` keys are translated, any other field gets the translated *Invalid input*, and raw zod `issues` are never returned (`handleRouteError` in `api/routeContext.ts`, `translateZodFieldErrors` in `lib/zodFieldErrors.ts`), so the form shows `rateTooPrecise` on its `monthlyRate` field. The form's unit picker reads `GET leases/available-units`: units filtered in SQL to products whose current use is `leasable`, read in at most 20 pages of units, `search` at most 200 characters.

### `POST /api/property_management/leases/{id}/end`
`{ endsOn, settlement?: 'prorate'|'credit_full'|'keep' }` — ends the lease at that date; every order reaching past it is cancelled from the next day with the settlement (default: the organization's sales setting). Requires `property_management.leases.manage`. The settlement is resolved first (omitted → the organization default); if the cancellation would credit anything — `prorate` or `credit_full` on an issued final month, or any already-issued month after a past end date (credited in full whatever the settlement) — the caller also needs `sales.credit_memos.manage` (`403` otherwise). That check sees only invoices issued so far, and the cancellation runs later on the queue with no user, so it is also enforced when the cancellation runs: each `recurrence_cancel` operation records `allow_credit` (whether the caller holds `sales.credit_memos.manage`), and the worker passes it to sales as `allowCredit`. Sales decides under its own recurrence row lock — the lock its invoicing worker takes — so an invoice issued between this check and the cancellation is seen: with `allowCredit: false` a cancellation that would credit anything is refused (`409 recurring_cancel_would_credit`), a permanent refusal — the lease change reverts (Changes to existing orders) and the building's managers get `lease.billing_change_refused`, asking for someone with credit permission to end the lease. The End lease dialog previews the credit and offers only credit-free choices to a manager without it, saying why. System cancellations — the compensation of a lease or extension order — record `allow_credit = true` (recorded in the Risk Register). `lease.delete` records the caller's `sales.credit_memos.manage` as `allow_credit`, like an end: it is refused once any order is invoiced (`409 lease_invoiced`), so its `credit_full` cancellations credit nothing unless an invoice is issued between the check and the cancellation, and then sales refuses them for a caller without credit permission. `409 lease_end_later_than_current`, `409 lease_already_ended`, `409 lease_change_pending`, `409 lease_billing_cancel_unavailable` (while `sales.orders.recurrence.cancel` is not registered with the `allowCredit` capability marker: an early end of an active lease, or any end while a `lease_order` or `extension_order` operation is pending). The `GET` preview returns `cancelAvailable` and `ordersToCancel`; the End lease dialog disables *End lease* and says why when the cancel is unavailable and orders would be cancelled.

### `GET /api/property_management/leases/{id}` · `DELETE /api/property_management/leases?id=`
The detail returns the lease, its versions, proposals, open operations, billing hints and a top-level `salesCancelAvailable: boolean` (whether `sales.orders.recurrence.cancel` is registered). While it is false the lease page disables *End lease*, and *Delete lease* when the lease has a billed order that is not already cut or a pending order operation, with a note explaining what stays allowed (ending at term; deleting a proposed lease, an activating lease without an order in flight, or a lease whose orders are all cut). A missing field is treated as `true`. The sales values are gated on the caller's sales features (wildcard-aware): order numbers (`lease.salesOrderNumber`, `versions[].salesOrderNumber`) need `sales.orders.view`, quote numbers (`proposals[].salesQuoteNumber`) need `sales.quotes.view`, and each version's `invoices` rows — credit memos included (invoice and credit-memo ids and numbers, billed and credited factors) — need `sales.invoices.manage`, because the sales module has no invoices-view feature. Without the feature the field is omitted. A top-level `salesAccess: { orders, quotes, invoices }` says which groups are included. The ids (`salesOrderId`, `salesQuoteId`) and `recurrence` stay, because the End and Delete dialogs need them. The lease page links an order or quote only when its number was returned; otherwise it shows a short note that viewing it needs sales access, and a hidden `invoices` column says the same. A response without `salesAccess` is treated as having no sales access. The same rule applies to the lists: `GET /api/property_management/leases` and `GET /api/property_management/bookings` return `salesOrderNumber: null` unless the caller has `sales.orders.view` (resolved once per request, wildcard-aware) and always keep `salesOrderId`; the leases list then shows *—*, and the bookings list shows *—* with no link (the *Order cancelled* note stays). CRM names follow the same rule. The detail returns a top-level `crmAccess: { people, companies }`: `lease.contactName` needs `customers.people.view` and `lease.companyName` needs `customers.companies.view`. `GET /api/property_management/leases` returns `crmAccess` at the top level next to `items` and gates `items[].contactName` and `items[].companyName` the same way. Without the feature the key is omitted, `primaryContactPersonId` and `customerCompanyId` stay, and the customers query is skipped (wildcard-aware, resolved once per request; deleted CRM records are never named). `DELETE` runs `lease.delete`: `409 lease_invoiced`, `409 lease_change_pending`, `409 lease_billing_cancel_unavailable`.

### `POST /api/property_management/leases/{id}/extensions` · `DELETE …/proposals/{proposalId}`
`{ months: int ≥ 1, monthlyRate, asProposal: boolean, expiresInDays?, message? }` (the extension starts the day after the current term ends) → `lease.extend` or `lease.propose_extension`. `monthlyRate` follows the lease-create rules (number or decimal string, 0.0001…999,999,999, at most 4 decimals — more is `rateTooPrecise`, not rounded — normalized to a canonical string, stored and sent to sales as `unitPriceNet`); an invalid body is `400` with translated `fieldErrors` and no `issues`, which the Extend dialog shows under the matching input (months, rate, expiry, message). `409` when an open proposal exists. `DELETE` withdraws an open proposal (new or extension).

### `GET` · `PUT /api/property_management/settings`
`{ billingChannelId: uuid | null, cancelledOrderStatusEntryId: uuid | null, leaseEndingThresholdDays: int 1..365, bookingReminderMinutes: int 5..1440, proposalExpiryDays: int 1..90 }`; `GET` returns the organization's row with defaults applied. `PUT` runs `property_management.settings.save` (mutation guards, optimistic lock on the row; two concurrent first saves → `409 settings_changed`) and validates that the channel and status entry belong to the caller's organization (`422` otherwise). A value outside its limits is `422` with translated `fieldErrors` (`property_management.errors.leaseEndingThresholdInvalid`, `.bookingReminderInvalid`, `.proposalExpiryInvalid`); any other invalid field gets the translated generic *Invalid input*, never a raw zod message. The organization comes from `resolveSingleOrganizationIdOrDeny` (as `sales/api/settings/document-numbers`), never the body. `PUT` requires `property_management.manage` and `property_management.buildings.manage_all` (route and the `settings.save` command, execute and undo — the settings are organization-wide; `403 settings_forbidden` otherwise); the save checks the optimistic lock against the row it holds `FOR UPDATE`. Undo refuses with `409 settings_changed` when the row changed since the save, and with `422` when a restored channel or status entry is no longer valid. The full `GET` response carries `canEdit` (true when the rbac service grants both `property_management.manage` and `property_management.buildings.manage_all`; super admin is decided by rbac too — no `isSuperAdmin` shortcut), and the settings page is read-only with a notice when it is false. Both `GET` shapes carry `today`, the deployment's local date (`YYYY-MM-DD`), which the lease forms use as their date default. `GET` requires `property_management.view` at the route and then, in the handler (rbac service, wildcard-aware): with `property_management.manage` it returns every setting plus `billingChannelConfigured`; with only `property_management.leases.manage` it returns a read-only subset `{ readOnly: true, proposalExpiryDays, billingChannelConfigured, today }` (the lease form and Extend dialog need the expiry default and the missing-channel warning); otherwise `403`. The lease form shows an error with *Try again* when the settings or buildings cannot be loaded, and the company and contact pickers say when the caller cannot read CRM companies or people. The sales-channel empty state links to *Create a sales channel* only for a viewer with `sales.channels.manage` (the sales channels page's feature); without it the message shows with no link.

### Building access (staff)

Managers see and change only the buildings they are assigned to (US-C2, US-C3). A staff user holding `property_management.buildings.manage_all` (granted to `admin` by default) sees every building of the organization; anyone else sees the buildings listed for them in `BuildingManager`. One resolver, `lib/buildingAccess.ts` → `permittedBuildingIds(user, org)`, is applied by every staff path: building, floor, space, unit, common-area, lease and booking lists and details (lists filtered, details `404` outside the set), the layout endpoint, every mutation of a building and of anything inside it, availability previews and confirms, the floor plan's location switcher, and the lists' own search boxes (which run against the filtered list queries; list search is a plain `ILIKE` substring match). The CRUD lists set their own tenant, organization and building scope (`omitAutomaticTenantOrgScope` plus `crudScopeFilters`), so full exports (`exportScope=full`) apply the same building restriction as the list. They show rows of the selected organization only, not its child organizations (`crudScopeFilters` pins `organization_id` to the resolved organization). The module's tenant, organization and building limits are sealed under `$and` (`sealListScope` in `api/crudShared.ts`), so a client advanced filter (`filter[...]`) can only narrow a list, never widen it; full export stays scoped. **Global search (Cmd+K)** cannot filter by record — the global route narrows only by organization and by each entity's `aclFeatures` — so the module's search entities declare `aclFeatures: ['property_management.buildings.manage_all']`: only unrestricted staff find buildings, units and leases there; a building-scoped manager finds none of them in global search, even in their own buildings, and searches through the module's lists. A record-level filter seam in `search` would lift this and is out of scope. A manager with no assignments gets the "no buildings assigned" state (screen 4). A context with no login resolves to no buildings unless it is a trusted system context (`ctx.systemActor === true`, set by `buildTrustedSalesContext` for workers, sweeps and CLI); the same rule applies to the commands that check `manage_all` themselves — `product_use.set`, `settings.save` and the manager assignment of `building.create` / `building.update` refuse with `403` when there is neither a login nor `systemActor`. Assignments are edited on the building form by holders of `manage_all`. The portal is unaffected — customers are scoped by their company and user.

### Portal access

Portal features follow the existing `portal.<resource>.<verb>` convention and are granted through `defaultCustomerRoleFeatures` (existing tenants: `yarn mercato customer_accounts sync-customer-role-acls`). Reused: `portal.invoices.view` for invoices (the portal's Invoices section, owned elsewhere). The bell needs no feature — it is part of the portal shell for every signed-in user, and each row is addressed to one user. New, because nothing existing covers units, bookings or leases:

| Feature | Grants | `portal_admin` | `buyer` | `viewer` |
|---------|--------|:--:|:--:|:--:|
| `portal.property_bookings.view` | Bookings page, unit list and slots | ✓ (`portal.*`) | ✓ | ✓ |
| `portal.property_bookings.manage` | Book and cancel own bookings | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| `portal.property_leases.view` | My leases page | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| `portal.property_leases.manage` | Approve or decline lease and extension proposals (a company commitment) | ✓ | — | — |

**Recipients.** Neither event bridge filters by feature, so this module resolves recipients before calling `emitPortalEvent`, as `warranty_claims` and `staff` do: booking events go to the booking's `customer_user_id` when that user holds `portal.property_bookings.view`; lease events go to the active customer users of the lease's company (`customer_entity_id`, capped at 100) who hold `portal.property_leases.view` (proposals: `.manage`), checked with `CustomerRbacService.userHasAllFeatures`. An empty list means no portal event and no bell entry — the domain event is emitted regardless. Bell entries use the same list.

Portal scoping: bookings are scoped to the **customer user** (`customer_user_id`) — a member without a company books and sees only their own bookings; leases are scoped to the customer user's company (`CustomerUser.customerEntityId`), never to a body parameter. `GET portal/units` runs a constant 5 queries per request (building + floors/units, opening rules, unavailability, confirmed bookings for the date, prices via `resolvePriceMany`); slots are computed in memory.

Every endpoint that takes an id of another record (unit, company, person, product, sales channel, status entry) loads it within the caller's organization before use; a foreign or missing id is `422`.

Errors use the platform's error envelope; messages are i18n keys, never raw strings.

## Search (`search.ts`)

Fulltext only (no vector) through the query index (`indexer: { entityType }` on every CRUD route):
- Every entity declares `aclFeatures: ['property_management.buildings.manage_all']`, so global search (Cmd+K) shows these records only to unrestricted staff (see Building access); building-scoped managers search through the module's filtered lists.
- `property_management:building` — name, address.
- `property_management:unit` — name, building name, floor name, product name (from the open product period).
- `property_management:lease` — reference and unit name (denormalized at index time; reindexed on `lease.*` and `unit.updated`). The CRM company name is encrypted, so it is never indexed, stored in the presenter or sent for embedding; `customer_company_id` stays `hashOnly`. Search results show the lease reference and unit.
- Persistent subscribers keep the denormalized names fresh: `unit.updated` reindexes the unit's leases, `building.updated` and `floor.updated` reindex their units (`lib/searchReindex.ts`: one `query_index.upsert_one` per record up to 500, a scoped `query_index.reindex` above that). The `building.updated` / `floor.updated` dependent reindex runs only when the payload's `nameChanged` is not `false` (a missing field counts as changed), so only on a rename and once per save.
- A query failure while building a search source is logged and reported (`property_management.search_source_lookup_failed`), and the record is still indexed without the related name.

Bookings are not indexed: the Bookings list filters by date, location and status, and its search box runs against the list query.

## Internationalization (i18n)

All labels under `property_management.*` in `i18n/*.json` (en, pl at minimum); `useT()` client-side, `resolveTranslations()` server-side. Unit types and products show catalog names (already translated by catalog). Dictionary entries for unavailability reasons are translatable.

Per organization, in the module's own `PropertyManagementSettings` row (Data Models), read and written through `GET`/`PUT /api/property_management/settings` — the `sales_settings` pattern: `billingChannelId` (sales channel for lease and booking documents — required before activation/booking; the API returns `422 billing_channel_missing` otherwise), `cancelledOrderStatusEntryId` (sales order status used for cancelled bookings — required before booking, `422 cancelled_status_missing`), `leaseEndingThresholdDays` (int, default 60), `bookingReminderMinutes` (int, default 60), `proposalExpiryDays` (int 1–90, default 14 — prefills the proposal forms). `moduleConfigService` is not used: it is tenant-scoped (Design Decisions).

## Portal notifications (addition to `notifications`)

The portal already shows a bell (`packages/ui/src/portal/components/PortalNotificationBell.tsx`, `PortalNotificationPanel.tsx`, `usePortalNotifications`) and serves list, unread count, mark read, mark all read and dismiss from `customer_accounts/api/portal/notifications*`, reading `notifications` rows where `recipient_user_id` is the portal user. The live refresh exists too (`notifications.notification.created` is `portalBroadcast`). What is missing is a way to create such a row: every `notificationService` method checks recipients against staff `users` (`getScopedNotificationRecipientUserIds`), so a customer user is rejected, and no module writes portal bell entries today — warranty claims, staff time reports and workflows only send live portal events.

**Change (additive, in `packages/core/src/modules/notifications`):**
- `NotificationService.createForCustomerUsers(input: { recipientCustomerUserIds: string[]; type; titleKey; bodyKey?; titleVariables?; bodyVariables?; linkHref?; sourceModule; sourceEntityType?; sourceEntityId?; groupKey?; severity? }, ctx)` — a new optional method on the DI service (BC §9: methods may be added).
- Recipients are validated against `customer_users` (same tenant, same organization, active, not deleted); any id that fails is rejected, exactly as the staff methods reject foreign users.
- `customer_accounts` is a soft-optional peer: when the module is not enabled, `createForCustomerUsers` resolves no recipients without touching `customer_users`, and the call is rejected with `404`.
- Rows reuse `notificationFactory` and the existing table — `recipient_user_id` holds the customer user id; no column, index or migration change (the column has no foreign key).
- In-app only: no email or push channel delivery, because channel preferences (`notification_preferences`) are staff-user settings.
- Emits the existing `notifications.notification.created` / `batch_created` events with `recipientUserIds`, so the open portal bell refreshes through the existing portal stream.
- Unit tests: a customer user of the tenant gets a row; a staff id, a customer of another organization and a deleted customer user are rejected; no channel delivery is attempted. Integration: a row created this way is returned by `GET /api/customer_accounts/portal/notifications` for that user and not for another.

Ships with this work (commit `80aed32f6` on this branch, reviewed with the `notifications` maintainers) rather than as its own PR; Phase 7 uses it for bell entries.

2. 3. Rate corrections, start-date changes and extensions as new recurring orders. *Integration:* a correction stops the old order at its next anniversary and starts a new one for the remaining months; a correction exactly at an anniversary invoices that month once; two consecutive corrections each create their follow-on order; two start-date changes before the first invoice both reach the order; an extension's order starts the day after the term ends; no month is invoiced twice; a correction followed by an end before its anniversary cancels the new order from its start and re-cancels the old order earlier — its cutoff (the anniversary) is later than the new `effectiveOn` — with the chosen settlement, so a prorate end credits the unused part of the final month (no 409); only an order whose cutoff is already at or before the new `effectiveOn` is skipped.

**Deviation — sales-operation interleavings (2026-11-17).** The *Sales operations* cases that need a fault injected or two owners racing — an error thrown after the sales order commits, two workers owning one operation, a reused key with a different payload, a permanent refusal, revert after compensation and the worker under `OM_ENFORCE_ORG_SCOPE_STRICT=true` — cannot be forced through the HTTP API. They are covered by unit tests only (`__tests__/salesOperationRunner.test.ts`, `__tests__/salesCommandContext.test.ts`), with no Playwright placeholder. Every interactive backoffice route the integration suite opens is also a hydration smoke: it watches for uncaught page errors and hydration mismatches (`__integration__/hydrationWatch.ts`).

**Local behaviour behind sales (2026-11-17, round 8).** Where the asserted behaviour is local, a spec no longer skips because sales cannot cancel recurring orders yet. The sweep skipping a lease with a pending change (TC-PM-017) and the released months staying taken while an end is pending (TC-PM-016) assert the `409 lease_billing_cancel_unavailable` of the API end and then pin the pending end directly in the database under the spec's own change id (`pinPendingEnd` / `releasePinnedChange` in `__integration__/leaseFixtures.ts`; `pinPendingEnd` builds `pending_change` with the serializer `lease.end` uses, `lib/pendingChange.ts`, and moves to the API end once sales Phases 2–3 land); only the sales confirmation stays behind `SALES_PHASES_SKIP_REASON` / `SALES_CANCEL_SKIP_REASON`. The booking sweep (TC-PM-024) loses the booking order job on purpose: the spec holds the operation row while the sales queue drains, so the worker finds it locked and drops the job, back-dates the booking and its operation by 30 days, sweeps and then expects the booking confirmed — with the app's own background worker (`AUTO_SPAWN_WORKERS` on) winning the race the case is skipped locally, as CI drains the queues itself (`AUTO_SPAWN_WORKERS=false`). A worker-dependent spec skips when its queue cannot be drained in the environment; it fails instead when workers are required (`skipOrFailUnsettled`, `workersRequired()` in `__integration__/leaseFixtures.ts`): with `OM_PM_REQUIRE_WORKERS=1`, and by default whenever `CI` is set — `OM_PM_REQUIRE_WORKERS=0` turns the skip back on there.

**Deviation — unit-only cases (2026-11-18, round 11).** These listed cases are covered by unit tests only, with no Playwright placeholder: (1) *the `recurring_cancel_would_credit` refusal of an End lease* — it needs an invoice issued after the End lease request passed its credit check and before the worker runs the cancellation; with the app's background worker on, the cancellation runs as soon as the request commits, so the window cannot be forced, and sales recurring orders Phases 2–3 (invoicing, cancel) are not shipped yet. Unit: `__tests__/salesOperationRunner.test.ts` (the refusal is permanent), `__tests__/leaseOperationHandlers.test.ts` (the lease reverts, `lastBillingRefusal` records it, `lease.billing_change_refused` reaches the building staff). TC-PM-016 asserts the deterministic half instead: a `keep` end by a manager without `sales.credit_memos.manage` is accepted by sales with no credit memo and no refusal. (2) *Delete after an invoice → `409 lease_invoiced`* — behind sales Phases 2–3 (no invoice can be issued yet); unit: `__tests__/leaseCommands.test.ts`. (3) *Booking order creation paused, unit closed, creation resumed* — the pause has to fall between the sales order commit and the operation's finish (fault injection); unit: `__tests__/bookingOperationHandlers.test.ts` (the order of a booking released during creation is moved to the cancelled status, never deleted) and `__tests__/salesOperationRunner.test.ts` (a hold released before or during creation is compensated). ## Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

| Scenario | Behaviour the user sees |
|----------|-------------------------|
| Opening hours are narrowed while bookings exist outside the new hours | Existing bookings are kept; the save dialog lists them so the manager can cancel individually. New bookings follow the new hours. |
| A space is shrunk so a unit would stick out | Save is disabled; the message names every unit outside the space (US-G4). |
| A floor is resized smaller than its spaces/units | `422` naming what would fall outside; the floor size field shows the error. |
| A unit's product is deleted in the catalog | Snapshots keep leases, bookings and history intact; the unit shows a "product missing" warning and cannot take new leases or bookings until a product is set. |
| A product's use changes from bookable to leasable in the catalog while units have future bookings | The product-use edit is refused with the list of affected units and bookings. |
| Creating the order fails, or the worker dies mid-way | The hold stays; the operation is retried from the queue and links the order if sales already created it. The page keeps showing *Booking…* / *Activating…*; on the first failure at or after 5 attempts staff (building managers plus `manage_all` holders) get `property_management.sales_operation.stalled` once per operation, `reportError` is called, and the manager can cancel, which compensates. |
| Approval fails part-way (conversion, overlap, lock timeout) | The whole approval rolls back: the quote still exists, the proposal keeps its state, the portal shows the error and the customer can approve again. |
| The customer approves after the deadline | `409 proposal_expired`; the card shows the proposal as expired and suggests contacting the building manager. |
| A proposal's quote is edited in the sales backoffice after it was sent | Approval is refused with `409 quote_changed`; the manager withdraws and proposes again. |
| The unit becomes unavailable while a proposal is open | Approval is refused until it is available again for the term; the proposal is not withdrawn automatically. |
| The hold was released while its order was being created (closure, cancellation) | The finish step sees the cleared token and compensates the new order (cancelled status for a booking order, recurrence cancelled from its start for a lease order — never deleted); if that fails too, staff get an "orphaned order" notification naming it and the next run retries. |
| Sales refuses a lease's billing change for good (an end, a start-date change, a deletion) | The lease returns to its previous dates and status, the detail shows the sales error, and the building's managers are notified; a second change waits until the first has settled. |
| The scheduler module is not installed | Lease invoices are issued by sales; see the recurring-orders spec (banner and `mercato sales run-recurring-invoicing`). This module's sweeps can be run from the CLI (`mercato property_management sweep:run`). |
| Two managers make overlapping floors/spaces unavailable | Both rows stand; the effective availability is their union; ending one leaves the other in force. |
| A manager ends a lease early, and another manager tries to lease the unit from the day after the new end before sales has processed the end | `409` — the unit stays taken for the original term until the end finishes; if sales refuses it, the lease returns to its original end without overlapping anything. |
| A manager moves a unit with upcoming bookings into a space or floor that is closed at those times | `409 unit_move_destination_unavailable`; the form lists the bookings, and the manager cancels them or ends the closure before moving. |
| A manager without credit permission ends a lease, and sales issues an invoice before the cancellation runs | When the cancellation would create a credit (e.g. `prorate` on the now-issued final month), it is refused instead of crediting; with `keep` it succeeds and nothing is credited. On a refusal, the lease keeps its previous end, the detail shows the sales error, and the managers are notified to have someone with credit permission end it. |
| A building is archived while the portal page is open | The next booking attempt fails with `409 location_unavailable`; the location switcher marks it unavailable on reload. |

## Risks & Impact Review

### Data Integrity Failures
- **Double booking** under concurrency — mitigated by the unit row lock + overlap check in one transaction; integration test with parallel requests.
- **Layout drift** (a unit outside its space) — invariants enforced by commands, not only the UI; space updates re-validate contained units.

### Cascading Failures & Side Effects
- **Unavailability cancels many bookings** — dry run shows exactly what is cancelled; the confirm button states the count; up to 200 cancellations run in the confirm transaction, larger sets in chunks on the `unavailability_apply` worker; notifications emitted after commit.
- **Sales command failure during booking, lease creation or approval** — sales documents commit on their own; every write goes through a sales operation that is locked while in flight, reconciled by metadata lookup after any failure (including one thrown after the sales commit), and compensated when its hold was released meanwhile; approval is one transaction.

### Tenant & Data Isolation Risks
- Portal endpoints derive the company from `CustomerUser.customerEntityId`, never from the request body; every query filters by `organization_id`.
- A payload modified by a mutation guard can never change the tenant, the organization or the route ids: commands are built from the guarded payload through `guardedCommandInput`, which re-applies them.

### Migration & Deployment Risks
- New tables only in this module. Other modules change only through their own PRs — `sales` recurring orders (#7765) and the sales AGENTS.md wording (#7475) — except the additive `notifications.createForCustomerUsers`, which ships with this work; this module's search config and catalog widget/enricher are additive.

### Operational Risks
- **Scheduler absent** — no automatic invoices (sales) and no sweeps; banners on the leases page; both modules offer a CLI run.
- **Sales channel not configured** — creating or proposing a lease and booking return `422 billing_channel_missing`; settings page links to configuration.

### Phase 4 — deviations from the spec
- `Unavailability` carries a denormalized `building_id` (building access filter, per-building listing for the switches), `progress_job_id`, and a `deleted_at` (undo of `unavailability.create` soft-deletes the row; cancelled bookings are not restored). `starts_at`, `ends_at` and `ended_at` are zone-less wall-clock `timestamp`s like the booking times; the dialog picks whole days (from 00:00, until = the end of the chosen day).
- Ending sets `ended_at` and leaves `ends_at` as planned; the effective end is the earlier of the two, so ending a period that has not started yet voids it. Undo of `end` restores the previous `ended_at` only when no pending or confirmed booking still to come falls inside the restored window; otherwise it is refused with `409 unavailability_undo_bookings` (count and up to 100 bookings) and nothing is cancelled.
- The affected bookings are `pending` / `confirmed` bookings overlapping the period that have not ended yet. The dry run accepts a missing description; its booking and lease lists are capped at 100 entries with totals (`bookingCount`, `leaseCount`, `unitCount`), while the token hashes every affected id. The token is SHA-256 over `subjectType|subjectId|startsAt|endsAt|` followed by the comma-joined ids, sorted, of the pending, confirmed and completed bookings in the range from the closure's start (not from now), so a token confirms only its own subject and range, and a booking that ends — or is completed — between preview and confirm does not make the preview stale; the bookings counted and cancelled are still those not ended at confirm. Preview and confirm compute it the same way, in SQL. Extra codes: `422 preview_token_required`, `422 unavailability_in_past`, `422 unavailability_start_in_past` (a start before today; today and later are accepted), `422 unavailability_reason_invalid`; a foreign or unassigned subject is `404`. The dry run does not refuse an archived building (only confirm returns `409 subject_archived`, under the building lock). Confirm also returns `queuedBookings` and `progressJobId` (see Unavailabilities API).
- Customer (portal) notifications for bookings cancelled by a closure ship with Phase 7; Phase 4 emits the `booking.cancelled` domain event (with `cancelReason: 'unavailability'`) per booking and `unavailability.created` / `.ended`.
- The `unavailability_apply` job carries the unavailability id (not booking ids): the worker re-reads the period and the units under its subject on every chunk, cancels through the `booking.cancel` command (which now accepts `reason: 'unavailability'` with `unavailabilityId`; the backoffice cancel route still forces `manager`), skips bookings that can no longer be cancelled, and stops when the period was ended meanwhile. The booking sweep re-enqueues rows still `applying` after 15 minutes with the row's `progress_job_id` (the worker writes a missing id onto the row and forgets one whose job no longer exists).
- *Unit unavailable* is shown for a proposed, activating or active lease whose remaining reserved term (from today) overlaps an unavailability, current or upcoming; `unitUnavailableFrom` / `Until` describe the first merged span. The filter computes the matching lease ids in one SQL query (`EXISTS` over the unit's own, space, floor and building closures against the lease's reserved term), without loading closures, units or leases.
- New leases and proposals are refused when any unavailability overlaps the term; direct extensions and extension proposals are checked over the extension window too (`assertUnitAvailableForTerm` in `commands/lease-extension.ts`, under the unit lock — tenth review round), and proposal approval (Phase 7) will reuse it.
- The unit-move guard checks the destination space (and floor, should a unit ever change floor); `unit.update` cannot move a unit between floors today.
- Helper endpoints: `GET /api/property_management/unavailabilities` (list for the switches; at most 500 rows ordered by `startsAt`, not paginated; returns `total` and `truncated` along with `items`, `truncated` true when more match; `items` and `total` come from one SQL predicate (no in-memory re-filter); documented deviation from the ≤100 page-size rule: up to 500 rows unpaged, because the availability switches and hatching need a building's whole set, with `truncated` flagging overflow; `POST` validation returns translated `fieldErrors` and no `issues`) and `GET /api/property_management/unavailability-reasons` (the dictionaries API needs `dictionaries.view`, which building managers lack; returns `{ items, today }`, where `today` is the deployment-local date the closure dialog starts from; `items` is empty while the dictionary is inactive, and `assertReasonEntry` then refuses its entries with `422 unavailability_reason_invalid` too). The reasons dictionary is seeded for new organizations only (the module has not been deployed): the seed creates the dictionary and its default entries only when no dictionary with the key exists — a soft-deleted one is left alone, without error, and default entries an admin removed are not re-added; its entries are plain dictionary labels, not translated per locale. The seeded dictionary name, description and default labels are seed data that the tenant then owns, and `property_management/i18n/.hardcoded-allowlist.json` records them as such.
- The switch is off while the subject has its own current or scheduled period ("Unavailable · from …"); switching it on ends all of them. The floor page shows availability as a card and in its tables; the hatched unavailable areas in the layout drawing shipped with Phase 6 (floor plan and floor setup drawing).
- Building `create` also accepts opening hours (the form is shared). Times are validated as `HH:MM`; opening runs 00:00–23:59 and closing may also be `24:00` (open until midnight), which the editor offers as an *Until midnight* checkbox per day and the slots, booking rules and outside-hours check accept.
- Under its locks the confirm reads only the unit ids it needs and counts the flagged leases in SQL (it only returns `flaggedLeases`); every availability query (resolver, slots, leases list, available units, floor status) filters by building id as well as subject id so it uses the `(organization_id, building_id, starts_at)` index.

### Phase 6 — deviations from the spec
- **Query count.** The status part of `GET floors/{id}/layout?date=` takes **6 queries** independent of the unit count (leases, open proposals, the day's bookings, customers, unavailabilities, settings; the lease query is shared with the setup payload's blocker check), and a 7th when any current or future closure of the building, the floor, its spaces or units names a dictionary reason (the reason labels, 2026-11-12) — the closures are read from the day onwards, so a reasoned closure that starts later fires it too. The whole call takes **16**: floor, building, the building's floors, spaces, units, common areas, product names, category names, product uses, upcoming bookings (setup `hasBlockers`), plus the 6 — and the access resolver's RBAC / `BuildingManager` lookups. Without `date` it is 11, as before. The spec's "6" is read as the status projection's budget; folding the setup lookups would mean dropping catalog-sourced names from the floor page.
- An archived building is read from the already-loaded building row instead of the resolver's `loadArchivedBuildingIds` query (same result, one query fewer).
- `booked` also counts `completed` bookings of the day (the 15-minute sweep completes past bookings, which would otherwise turn a unit that was used this morning into *free*); `pending` and `confirmed` count as the spec says. `occupied` also counts an `ended` lease whose (possibly shortened) term covers the date, so past dates show who was there; `pending_order` (activating) counts as in force.
- `unavailable` is any resolver window touching the day, not only one covering it whole (the dialog's periods are whole days; a closure starting at 14:00 makes the unit unavailable that day). It wins the fill; the lease and bookings are still returned and shown in the panel.
- `daysLeft` and `leaseEnding` are counted from the viewed date, not from today. *Proposed* is set only for an open **new-lease** proposal; an open extension offer on the lease in force is returned as `lease.extensionRespondBy` and shown in the *Lease ends* notice, without the badge.
- The unit's current product and use are used for every date (product history is not replayed for past or future dates).
- The prototype's *Unit type* legend group (Private office / Company unit / Meeting room) is not shown: unit types are catalog categories per organization, so the tile names the category and the icon tells leasable from bookable instead. The date picker is in (the prototype had none).
- `FloorPlanCanvas` reuses `FloorDrawing` for the dimension labels and grid instead of a separate `DimensionRulers` leaf; the leaves are `UnitTile`, `SpaceZone`, `FloorPlanLegend`, `FloorPlanToolbar`, `UnitDetailDrawer` / `UnitDetailSections`.

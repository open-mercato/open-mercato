## 📝 TLDR

An ordinary user delete leaves devices, notification copies, saved views, dashboards, keys, signup data and personal credentials. A team member also has HR and working-time records that may outlive their login. **Proposed (future):** module-owned classes export and erase per-user stores, report the team member as a separate related subject, and retain only the declared employment records until a tenant-defined end. A marked member is blanked later by a retention set. Every staff and planner table has an outcome below.

The staff account itself belongs to the contract/erasure specs. The program's A9 owns the broken user-delete subscriber repair; communication coverage owns disconnecting channels. No class here reads their tables directly.

## 📝 Overview

This carrier covers the people-ops modules of Track C's coverage roll-out: `staff`, `planner`, `onboarding`, `devices`, `push_notifications`, `notifications`, `api_keys`, `perspectives`, `dashboards` and the `integrations` tables. It is OSS under DEC-2: every class, set, pruner and seam lives in the module that owns the data; enterprise adds only scheduling and holds through the shared services. Phase 1 needs only the operational-pruner foundation. Phase 2 needs the erasure run (C4), the program's A9, which alone delivers the three integrations credential methods, and communication coverage Phase 1; Phase 3 adds the contract's Phase 7 settings, the doctor's R3-4/R4-7 tier and record retention's DEC-C. Phases 2 and 3 also register their modules' tenant-export (C8) readers, which needs tenant exit's Phase 2 helper. C5's census, C8's export and G6's verification suite consume its declarations. Each phase ships export and erase together and leaves its modules' tables reachable, deleted, retained with an end, or classified.

| 2 | Kept employment records | Keep the member's name, user link, time entries, leave records and financial report contributions identified until the tenant age under `prefer: 'delete'`; the kept block is the structured record only — dates, statuses, durations, leave type (`unavailability_reason_entry_id`), amounts and ids — while leave `note`, `decision_comment` and `unavailability_reason_value`, report-event `reason` and non-allowlisted event `metadata` are cleared at initial erasure, and time-entry `notes` are kept only on entries locked into a report (`locked_report_id` set) as the description of work billed to a customer; as C4 assumption 18 specifies; set `anonymizeOwnerWhenRetained: false`. A retained identified root is never reported anonymized | Employment law commonly requires working-time, leave and pay records to be kept for a period after the work (Art. 17(3)(b)); kept identified they still prove what they record, and everything else is erased now. The tenant, as controller, sets the period. That necessity covers the structured record, not free text: a leave note or absence-reason text can carry health data (Art. 9), and the reason entry id already records the leave type. Billed work descriptions are the one free text kept, because the customer invoice they support is itself a retained commercial record. Pending leave keeps the block as `open_case` with no end until decided, and every run report lists pending requests whose end date has passed so an operator decides them; future approved leave moves the end forward. Malformed dates or an invalid timezone refuse that member for manual review | There is no employment-end column on the member entity. ### Classes

| Class | Subject kinds | `erasureGroup` | Export / erasure |
|---|---|---|---|
| `staff.team_members` | `staff:team_member`; `auth:user` | `records` | Owns member kind. For `auth:user`, find linked members and report them as related only: no member mutation. For member subjects use the matrix below |
| `planner.availability` | `staff:team_member` | `records` | Export and delete member rules; shared rule-set configuration stays |
| `staff.customer_copies` | `customers:person`; `customer_accounts:user` | `records` | Export/clear customer snapshots, portal request/task copies and portal comment author copies as below |
| `staff.authorship` | `auth:user` | `records` | Export attributable comment/decision/report-event authorship about others; retain as `other_party`, with id-only attribution and no unrelated person's private text in the export |
| `onboarding.requests` | `auth:user` | `records` | Export signup fields of every row carrying the verified user's id, without password/token/lookup hashes; delete those rows whatever their status |
| `notifications.inbox` | `auth:user`; `customer_accounts:user` | `records` | Export inbox and own preferences; delete both |
| `perspectives.views` | `auth:user` | `records` | Export/delete private views |
| `dashboards.layouts` | `auth:user` | `records` | Export/delete own layout and own widget rows |
| `push_notifications.deliveries` | `auth:user` | `records` | Export delivery content/status/dates without token snapshots/provider secrets; delete own deliveries |
| `devices.user_devices` | `auth:user` | `credentials` | Export platform/versions/last seen, never token; hard-delete devices |
| `api_keys.keys` | `auth:user` | `credentials` | Export name/date, never key hash/token/secret; delete rows created by or issued to the user |

The `auth:user` path of `staff.team_members` is discoverable because that kind is declared. Export and dry run report linked member references; the run refuses an unnamed related member unless explicitly excluded, as the erasure contract requires. No link means no related subject. When both user and member are named, each path processes only its own reviewed subject.

`staff.team_members` sets `anonymizeOwnerWhenRetained: false` for its `staff:team_member` owner path. Consume C4 assumption 18, “The operation” and “The run”, step 7: the flag prevents dependent retention from coercing the reviewed `delete` preference into `anonymize`. Under `delete`, the declared identifying employment block may remain `retained` / `legal_retention` or `open_case`; explicit `--prefer anonymize` while legal retention keeps an identified employment record is refused by `checkErasable` in pre-flight before any write with `STAFF_EMPLOYMENT_RETENTION_REQUIRES_REVIEW`, and rechecked before owner mutation. The refusal message tells the operator to run with `--prefer delete`: it deletes everything not under legal retention and keeps the identified employment root, reported as `retained` with `retainReason: 'legal_retention'` and its `retainedUntil`. Pending leave retains its existing `open_case` handling. It never means the named root is anonymous. Assumption 2 and C4 assumption 18 settle this policy. When no identifying block needs retention, anonymize clears it. Set `privacy_retained_at` while any identifying employment block survives and return `retained`; `anonymizedAt` stays null. Only final identifying-field clearing removes `user_id`, clears that mark and sets `anonymized_at`. The flag has no effect on the related-only `auth:user` path.

### Staff and planner table matrix

All rows, including soft-deleted rows, are scoped to the tenant and optional organization. “Now” means initial member erasure. “Final” means erasure already past the age or the later employment set. Kept rows use `legal_retention`, except pending leave's `open_case`; shared work/authorship retained without a defined end is reported as `shared_record`/`other_party`. Do not label a section anonymized while a declared identifying copy remains.

For exports select only the subject's contribution. Redact other people's names, private bodies and credentials. Fields listed as configuration are exempt from subject erasure because they have no subject ownership/link, not because arbitrary free text is guaranteed non-personal. Unlinked mentions in shared configuration/work text are an explicit limit.

### Retention anchor, markers and set

Compute the employment anchor from all subject rows, including soft-deleted ones, as the maximum of two kinds of local date, each then taken at its UTC day end: a time entry's work `date` (a SQL date, used as is), and an approved leave request's `end_date` converted to its local calendar date in that request's own `timezone` column. Rejected requests are not anchor inputs, because they record no time off; their rows follow the member's end. Shared work records whose notification copies name no subject are not cleared: when `staff.team_members` clears the member's assignee or default-assignee link on `staff_time_tasks` and `staff_time_projects`, it makes no `staff:staff_time_task` or `staff:time_project` call, because assignee notifications carry only the task's reference and shared title (`time-task-assignee-notification.ts`) and budget alerts only the project name (`time-project-budget-threshold-notification.ts`); the employment reports it retains unchanged at initial erasure need no call until final blanking retitles them. `staff.authorship` and the related-only `auth:user` path of `staff.team_members` mutate no source record and make no call. For the message source, communication's “Source notification copies” remains the design owner of which messages are cleared. Both return `{ affected, historyRecords, sections }`, with `sections: PrivacyErasureSectionResult[]` carrying separate cleared sections and exact colleague notification/push survivor evidence from those owners, including their actual row ids and columns. Preserve those sections and every other user's edited history identity; message ids and recipient counts cannot substitute for owner evidence. The `notifications.inbox` and `push_notifications.deliveries` classes here still delete the erased recipient's own rows; they do not duplicate message-source traversal or delete colleagues' inboxes. Missing source-clearing support leaves that communication section incomplete; retry workers re-read cleared source state. Device/email copies already delivered remain outside database erasure.

Communication's “Known limits” explicitly reports the absent `auth:user` address aliases: staff forwarding addresses/names, inbox message outside fields and address-only call participants cannot be reached by staff id. Every staff account export/erasure report carries that unresolved coverage alongside its id-based sections. This spec does not silently turn those records into complete staff coverage.

### Portal customer copies

For a CRM customer, `staff.customer_copies` clears their matched customer display snapshots on projects, reports and tickets, and replaces the `title` of every report whose `customer_id` is the subject with that report's `reference`: `defaultReportTitle` (`staff/lib/timesheets-reports/reportConfigState.ts`) builds titles from the customer's name, and an edited title is free text about the same engagement. For a reporting portal user it also clears `customer_snapshot.reporterDisplayName`, nulls `reported_by_customer_user_id`, clears the request snapshot, replaces the linked portal task's title with its reference and clears description. For a commenting portal user clear their comment body, set `author_display_snapshot` to the shared display-name placeholder and null `author_customer_user_id`. Leave other users' comments and internal tasks alone. Return every edited report, ticket, task and comment as history records under R3-1, and clear their notification copies through the generic seam above. A company customer's display name is not cleared merely because an unrelated portal author is erased.

### Unfinished signups

A scheduled queue worker prunes rows after `OM_ONBOARDING_PENDING_RETENTION_DAYS` (integer days, minimum 1, default 30). It selects bounded keyset batches, but applies all eligibility predicates again in the DELETE statement itself:

- `user_id IS NULL` and status is pending, processing or expired;
- `COALESCE(updated_at, created_at)` is strictly before the age cutoff, with `created_at` used only for legacy null updates;
- status is not processing, or `processing_started_at IS NULL`, or it is at or before `now - 15 minutes`.

The same worker handles stale rows that already carry `user_id` (a provisioning error after `updateProvisioningIds`, reverted to `pending` by `resetProcessing`): with the last two predicates and status pending, processing or expired, it sets `password_hash = NULL` in a guarded UPDATE that rechecks every predicate, and keeps the row. The row still records the partially provisioned tenant for an operator, and the user's own erasure deletes it through `onboarding.requests`, which matches every row carrying that user id whatever its status. Nulling the hash costs no recovery: a retry cannot recreate the existing user, because `setupInitialTenant` runs with `failIfUserExists`, and the recovery path for a provisioned processing row (`completeProvisionedRequest` in `verify.ts`) uses the stored ids, not the hash.

Use one captured clock per batch. The 15-minute freshness rule matches `packages/onboarding/src/modules/onboarding/api/get/onboarding/verify.ts`; extract a shared module-local constant used by verification and pruning so it cannot drift. A stale crashed process is eligible. Recheck user, status, update time and processing time in the deletion predicate, so a reused/claimed/completed row between selection and delete survives. No personal values or secrets enter prune logs. This is system-scope age cleanup of unfinished rows, not a tenant subject erasure or legal-hold operation. Use the existing worker/scheduler contracts; no polling loop.

### Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Blank a member during their user's run | Changes another reviewed subject's root and may destroy employment evidence |
| Mark an identified member `anonymized_at` | Misstates the retained outcome and leaves UI/export reporting false |
| Refuse every member apply for manual review while employment records must stay | Leaves address, comments, job history and profile values in place with no end, although nothing requires keeping them |
| Leave every tenant-less owner row | The verified owner check already supplies tenant membership |
| Reuse only `eraseIntegrationCredentials` | It empties the secret before soft deletion, but locates by integration/scope and leaves the row; it does not enumerate all user credentials |
| Skip every processing signup forever | Crashed processing never ends without another verification call |
| Delete all rows of a report containing one member | Destroys other people's financial contributions; clear only the declared subject copies |

### Known limits

- Notification copies of source records that no listed call covers escape subject clearing: for example a staff project access request naming the requesting staff user (`staff:time_project`, whose source is the shared project, not a record the erasure clears), a source in a module with no coverage class, or a mention of the subject inside an unrelated record. D3's `notifications.inbox` set deletes read, acted-on or dismissed notifications, and unread ones past their `expires_at`, after its 30-day floor when an operator runs it; unread notifications without an expiry remain. Unlinked mentions in shared task/configuration text may also escape subject discovery. Copies of the listed source records are cleared for all recipients through the generic seam above, including push payloads; recipient-owned rows are deleted here. Shared sections actually found are reported retained with exact row evidence; unknown mentions are a coverage gap, never an anonymous result.
- Staff forwarding-address/name, inbox outside-field and address-only call copies remain unresolved because `auth:user` supplies no address aliases, as communication’s “Known limits” reports. Delivered device/email copies cannot be recalled.
- A member with no account is resolved by name candidates, then explicitly selected by id.
- Root ids and kept numeric work/financial values are tombstone-linked and may be re-identifiable with outside information; final blanking is removal of local identifying fields, not a legal anonymity guarantee.
- A user with no tenant cannot supply the owner proof needed for tenant-less row cleanup and is reported/refused by the tenant run.
- Integration logs naming a user are bounded by D1's `integrations.integration_logs` pruner (30-day window), not by subject erasure, and only while that schedule runs. External-id mappings of an erased record are kept as pseudonymous references until the integration's own sync removes them.
- Unset age, pending leave, a hold or an OSS operator not running the set can prolong employment retention; reports/checks make those states visible.
- Until Phase 2 (per-user modules, `integrations`, `onboarding`) and Phase 3 (`staff`, `planner`) ship their tenant-export readers, a tenant export reports those modules' tables as named `reader_unavailable` gaps, so a complete handover (exit 0) needs both phases.

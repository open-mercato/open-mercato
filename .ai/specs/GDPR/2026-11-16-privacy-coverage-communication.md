## 📝 TLDR

A message has a sender and recipients, and every one of them is a person. Erasing one of them must not destroy the others' record of the conversation — and must not leave the erased person's address and the text they sent behind either, including copies of it forwarded inside the company. Words quoted in a retained staff reply can survive; that is a stated limit. Today these four modules can do neither: a sent message cannot be removed at all, the `messages` table has no direction even though channel links store it, an outside correspondent's address sits in several places across two modules, inbound mail is kept whole with every quoted third party, and a phone call's participants are rewritten from the provider each time the call is read again. **Proposed (future):** a data class per module and a stated rule for each kind of record. What an outside person sent to the company is cleared, together with every internal forward that copied it. What the company's staff wrote is kept as the company's record, with the outside person's address removed, and with no automatic end; that is named as a limit. An erased person's own copies are deleted. Each module is its own phase and ships its export and its erase together.

**Not in this spec:** messages inside CRM interactions, which the CRM class covers; dead-letter queues, which the pruner spec bounds; call transcripts, which have columns and no storage yet; notifications about unrelated sources. Message-source notifications and their push-delivery copies are cleared through services specified here; the staff coverage spec covers recipient-owned notification rows.

## 📝 Overview

This carrier covers the communication modules of Track C's coverage roll-out: `messages`, `communication_channels`, `inbox_ops` and `phone_calls`, and, assigned by DEC-J, `packages/channel-*` and `packages/tillio`. It is OSS under DEC-2: every class, fill, lookup and seam lives in the module that owns the data, and enterprise adds only scheduling and holds through the shared services. Phase 1 needs the program's A9, which alone delivers the three integrations credential methods, the contract's Phase 6 aliases and identifier subject, and the erasure run's Phases 3–4; Phases 2 and 3 need the same without A9. Phase 1 also delivers the generic `notificationService.clearSourceForErasure` seam that people-ops coverage defines, which people-ops Phase 2, commerce and content-AI's `workflows.instances` then call; people-ops Phase 2 registers `integrations.user_credentials` after it. C5's census, C8's readers and G6's verification suite consume its declarations. Each phase ships one module group's export and erase together: Phase 1 makes messages and channel copies reachable and clearable by direction, Phase 2 the inbox's mail, proposals and copies, Phase 3 calls and their participants, each with its remaining baseline entries named.

## 📝 Resolved assumptions

| # | Question | Chosen | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A staff user who sent messages | The messages stay, with the sender left as an id that no longer resolves. Their own inbox rows are deleted | Staff wrote on the company's behalf, so a sent message is the company's record, retained as `evidence`; deleting it would hide it from everyone who received it. The sender column cannot be empty. A name the sender typed into the text stays with it. |
| 2 | What an outside person sent in | Subject and body cleared; the row kept, so that threads and counts hold | A private person's own words are what the erasure request is about, so the text goes. Deleting the rows would break threads and replies for the staff who took part; an id-only shell keeps them. A tenant that needs the text for a legal claim does not run the erasure, or holds the subject in Enterprise. |
| 3 | What staff sent to an outside person | Kept, with the outside address and name removed | It is the company's own correspondence. A staff reply that quotes the person keeps the quote; that is a stated limit. |
| 4 | How to tell the two apart | A new stored direction on each message | `external_messages.direction` and `message_channel_links.direction` already exist; the fill copies their direction into `messages`. | 5 | A correspondent with no record of their own | The contract's identifier subject, exported and erased with `--email` or `--phone`. Its id is the contract's derived id, which matches no column | No row stands for such a person, and a hash must not become a subject's id. |

## 📝 Problem Statement

**messages**

- Subject, body, one outside address and an outside name are encrypted; the outside-address lookup hash is a separate digest; the sender is a staff user id and cannot be empty (`messages/data/entities.ts`). The outside address is the sender on inbound mail and the recipient on outbound; the inbound-from-channel flag is supplied at compose/event time, while direction remains in channel links (`commands/messages.ts`).
- Internal recipients have a row each; that table, the linked-object table — a typed link to a record, with an optional copy of it in `entity_snapshot` that only forwards and undo restores write — and the access-token table have no tenant column (the same source; `commands/shared.ts`). Delivery failures keep provider error text in `messages.external_email_error` and `message_recipients.email_error`.
- A recipient can remove their own row; a sender's removal hides the message from everyone; nothing hard-deletes a sent message. Only action execution and confirmation change a sent message, and only its action and confirmation fields; no command edits its subject, body or address (`commands/messages.ts`; `commands/actions.ts`; `commands/confirmations.ts`).
- **A forward copies the thread.** `messages.messages.forward` creates a new message in the same thread with `parent_message_id` set to the forwarded message, subject `Fwd: ` plus that message's subject, and a body built from every live message of the thread, inbound ones included. It copies the forwarded message's linked objects, `entity_snapshot` included, and every thread message's attachment rows as new rows sharing the same `storage_path`. Off a channel thread it keeps the outside address and name; on one it drops them and files the forward as internal (`commands/messages.ts`; `lib/forwarding.ts`; `lib/attachments.ts`). No column marks a row as a forward.
- Send-as-user mail stores only the first `to` address as the outside address and the subject as the outside name; the other `to`, `cc` and `bcc` addresses are only in the outbound channel link and the daily counter (`communication_channels/lib/send-as-user.ts`).
- Per-window send counters (`message_external_email_windows`) hold a scope, a staff user id or the tenant id as `subject_id`, a window start and a count (`lib/externalEmailBudget.ts`).
- A daily counter of outside recipients stores a hash made with its own context string (`lib/externalEmailBudget.ts`), which no alias can match.
- **`external_email_hash` is written only by the encryption service, while it encrypts.** The map names it (`messages/encryption.ts`); the encrypt step sets it beside the ciphertext (`shared/lib/encryption/tenantDataEncryptionService.ts`) and returns the payload unchanged when encryption is off (the same source). The module only reads the column (`search.ts`; `api/route.ts`). With encryption off, or for a row written before the map named the hash, it is empty. Its index is on the hash alone, not led by the tenant (`data/entities.ts`).
- The messages search entity is declared with `enabled: false`, so message text is not indexed today (`search.ts`). `inbox_ops` does index the proposal `summary` (`inbox_ops/search.ts`).

**communication_channels**

- Conversations link to a CRM person by `contact_person_id` (indexed) and hold a subject. The only writer is ingest, from the contact hint's `matchedPersonId` (`commands/ingest-inbound-message.ts`). That hint comes from a plaintext email/phone lookup, which `lib/contact-resolver.ts` skips whenever tenant data encryption is on, or from an adapter, and no `packages/channel-*` adapter supplies one. The customers `link-channel-message` subscribers match people after decrypting but write CRM interactions, not the conversation. With encryption on, the program's production posture, `contact_person_id` therefore stays empty; it is filled only on deployments without encryption or on rows written before encryption was enabled. External messages hold the sender's identifier and display name in clear. Channel links hold the message as received in `channel_payload` — including the from, to, cc and bcc of inbound mail — and in `channel_metadata` the raw headers, the subject and the message ids that threading matches on (`communication_channels/data/entities.ts`; `lib/email-mime.ts`; `lib/thread-matcher.ts`).
- Messages from chat and text channels have no email address at all.
- Inbound mail becomes a `messages` row whose sender column is a system user or, by a fallback, the conversation's assignee (`commands/ingest-inbound-message.ts`).
- A channel may belong to one staff user, with an external identifier, a display name, state, `last_error` diagnostics and a pointer to a stored credential. The subscriber meant to disconnect it when that user is deleted never runs (program package A9).

**inbox_ops**

- For each inbound email it stores raw text and HTML, a cleaned text, the parsed thread, the forwarding address and name, the to and reply-to addresses, encrypted; and in clear the message ids, references and a content hash under a unique index that makes a redelivery a duplicate (`inbox_ops/data/entities.ts`; `lib/emailParser.ts`).
- The forwarder is usually a staff member. A text extraction submitted in the app stores the submitter's email as the forwarding address and their user id as `metadata.submittedByUserId`; proposals record `reviewed_by_user_id` and actions `executed_by_user_id` (`api/extract/route.ts`; `data/entities.ts`). The extraction-time CRM match is `participants[].matchedContactId` with `matchedContactType`, inside encrypted JSON (`subscribers/extractionWorker.ts`). Action `matched_entity_id` and `created_entity_id` are written only on execution (`lib/executionEngine.ts`), so those columns miss pending and rejected proposals.
- What a proposal produced points back at it: an order's metadata, a message's source id, a linked-object row (`sales/inbox-actions.ts`; `inbox_ops/lib/messagesIntegration.ts`).
- **Every inbound email is also copied into `messages`.** The copy has type `inbox_ops.email`, source `inbox_ops:inbox_email` with the email's id, and the whole raw thread, quoted third parties included, as its body, up to 50,000 characters. Its outside address is the forwarder's, usually a staff member's (`inbox_ops/lib/messagesIntegration.ts`). The messages class cannot find it by the customer's address, and no channel record says it came in. A reply drafted from a proposal is copied as `inbox_ops.reply`, addressed to the customer and written by staff (`inbox_ops/lib/messagesIntegration.ts`).
- The module has no commands; changes are made in route handlers.

**phone_calls**

- Participants hold a number, a display name and an email, encrypted with no hash, and the whole set is deleted and rewritten on every ingest (`phone_calls/data/entities.ts`; `commands/calls.ts`). The call holds the provider's raw snapshot and facts, which repeat the numbers, and a recording address. Nothing links a call to a person.

**Notification copies.** `messages/subscribers/message-notification.ts` copies the subject and outside sender into each recipient's `messages.new` notification, keyed by source `(message, messageId)`. `notifications/data/entities.ts` stores title, body and template variables; `push_notifications/data/entities.ts` stores the delivery payload. Erasing only the recipient does not clear copies about an outside sender.

**Fixtures exist** for inbound channel messages, inbound email and text extraction, and phone calls have integration tests of their own (`packages/core/src/helpers/integration/communicationChannelsFixtures.ts`, `inboxFixtures.ts`; `phone_calls/__integration__`).

## 📝 Proposed Solution

### Classes

| Class | Subject kinds | Finds by |
|---|---|---|
| `messages.messages` | `auth:user`; `customers:person`; `customer_accounts:user`; `privacy:identifier` | Sender and recipient ids, `action_taken_by_user_id`, `message_confirmations.confirmed_by_user_id` and the user's own `message_external_email_windows` rows; `external_email_hash`, now written by the module itself, against the `email_hashes` alias; `message_objects` typed links with `entity_module = 'customers'` and `entity_type = 'person'`, the only person registration in the message-object registry today (`customers/message-objects.ts`), whose `entity_id` is the person, checked through the tenant of their message; a module that later registers a message-object type for a subject kind adds its `(entity_module, entity_type)` pair to this class's fixed list, with a discovery test, in the same change; message ids handed to it by the channels class; copies handed to it by source by the inbox_ops class; forwards of every message it clears (Forwards, below) |
| `communication_channels.conversations` | `auth:user`; `customers:person`; `customer_accounts:user`; `privacy:identifier` | Channel owner, conversation/thread `assigned_user_id`, reaction `reacted_by_user_id`; B1's sender-identifier hash against email aliases; normalized-phone comparison through the bounded decrypting fallback when stored spelling cannot match phone aliases; `contact_person_id` where it is filled, which under tenant encryption it is not (Problem Statement) |
| `inbox_ops.emails` | `auth:user`; `customers:person`; `customer_accounts:user`; `privacy:identifier` | New module-owned participant lookup rows written at parsing and extraction, including extraction-time CRM matches; executed action ids as a second path; participant address hashes against `email_hashes`; copies in `messages` by source. Staff: proposal `reviewed_by_user_id`, action `executed_by_user_id` and text extractions whose `metadata.submittedByUserId` is the user |
| `phone_calls.calls` | `customers:person`; `customer_accounts:user`; `privacy:identifier` | New hashes of the participant's number and email against the aliases |

Staff export includes action/confirmation rows, conversation and thread assignments, reactions, inbox review and execution attribution, and the text extractions the user submitted. Erasure leaves those staff attribution ids as unresolved ids, clears copied reaction names, deletes the user's own `message_external_email_windows` rows (tenant-scope windows hold no person), and reports retained attribution. A text extraction the user submitted keeps its content and `metadata.submittedByUserId` as `other_party` work about its customers; its forwarding address, which is the submitter's own email, is replaced by the empty placeholder. The same address and name were copied as the outside address and name of the extraction's `inbox_ops.email` copy in `messages` (`inbox_ops/lib/messagesIntegration.ts`); the inbox class clears them by source through `messagePrivacyService.clearForErasure` under the outbound rule, so the copy keeps its text with the extraction and loses the submitter's address, name and hash. Staff-id reactions remain; external-subject reactions use constraint-safe deletion.

The four classes declare `erasureGroup: 'records'` and own no subject kind. The channels handler disconnects and clears identifying fields, state and `credentials_ref`, including on soft-deleted channels. Under R4-1, people-ops' `integrations.user_credentials` is the only class that deletes credential rows, through the integrations-owned `integrationCredentialsService`; A9's channel cascade calls its `deleteUserCredential({ scope: PrivacyScope, userId, credentialId })`. There is no `communication_channels.user_credentials` class. C4's “The run”, steps 5–6, separately requires every records class to succeed before any credential deletion; integrations then freshly lists all scoped user-owned rows, including disconnected and soft-deleted residual rows, without relying on cleared channel pointers. A missing or failed enabled channel class prevents the credentials group. Program package A9 is the sole delivery owner of `integrationCredentialsService.deleteUserCredential`, `listByUser` and `hardDeleteByUser`, with their unit tests; this spec specifies `deleteUserCredential`'s signature (API Contracts) and consumes all three, and people-ops Phase 2 consumes them for `integrations.user_credentials`.

**DSR call placement (R4-1).** In a DSR run the channels class makes no credential call: it runs in `records`, and C4 permits no credential deletion there. R4-1's call to the integrations method is placed in the credentials group, where `integrations.user_credentials` freshly lists the user's rows by user id and deletes each through the integrations service. It needs no pointer or id from the channels class, because the listing includes disconnected and soft-deleted rows. The channels class's read-only pre-flight still requires `integrationCredentialsService` by DI and, for an `auth:user` subject with any personal channel, an enabled `integrations.user_credentials` class in the run's registry; without it the subject refuses before any write, naming that class. A disconnect therefore never runs where nothing can delete the credential later, including between communication Phase 1 and people-ops Phase 2. A records failure leaves every credential in place, and a retry re-lists them. A9's ordinary-user-delete cascade has no barrier: it calls `deleteUserCredential` and clears the pointer in one transaction.

Every class declaring `privacy:identifier` also declares `customers:person` and `customer_accounts:user`, per C1's identifier coverage guard and owner `providedAliases`, under **DEC-A — Subjects known only by an identifier (`privacy:identifier`)**. Portal-user discovery is by email alias and destructive portal-account authorization follows the separate pre-flight rule below. Missing required aliases or a truncated fallback makes coverage incomplete.

A correspondent with no record is the contract's `privacy:identifier` subject (contract spec, "A subject that is only an identifier"): its id is the derived `idv1_` id, and it is erased with `mercato privacy erase --email` or `--phone`, repeating the flag on apply (erasure spec, "Erasing by email or phone"). With no lookup secret configured, erase by identifier exits 1.

**Verified attribution.** Apply C1's [ownership rule](2026-11-16-privacy-data-class-contract.md#aliases) before making data visible and C4's pre-write rule before planning history, forward, file or copy changes. An address/number lookup and a conversation's `contact_person_id` inferred only from that lookup are discovery hints. Verify the scoped owner relationship independently. For source-derived copies verify the source binding and the owning record's attribution first. Otherwise withhold the record, export an `ALIAS_ATTRIBUTION_UNVERIFIED` gap and refuse erasure before writes. Recheck the same evidence under the owner write lock. A verified direction or forward decision proves direction or provenance only. It never supplies missing subject attribution.

### What erasure does

| Record | Outcome |
|---|---|
| A message a staff user sent | **Retained**; the sender id and attachments stay. `external_email_error` is cleared |
| A staff user's recipient rows and access tokens | **Deleted**; the message stays for the others |
| An inbound message from the erased person | Subject, body, action data, `external_email_error`, recipient `email_error` and linked-record copies (`message_objects.entity_snapshot`) **cleared**; address, name and hash cleared; attachments released; the row kept. Message-source notification copies are cleared for every recipient |
| An outbound message to the erased person | Address, name, hash and `external_email_error` **cleared**; text and attachments **retained**. Channel link payloads and headers are scrubbed by message id through the channels service |
| A retained message with a linked object | Any `message_objects.entity_snapshot` on it is **cleared**, because it can copy a record about the person; the typed link (`entity_module`, `entity_type`, `entity_id`) stays as an id reference. A message found only through a typed link to the person is staff correspondence about them: exported, retained and reported with its message id for review |
| A forward of a cleared inbound or derived message | Cleared under the inbound rule whatever its own direction: subject, body, action data, error text, linked-object snapshots, address, name and hash; its own attachment rows released. See Forwards, below |
| A message with no email address, from a chat or text channel | The channels class finds it through an email/phone sender alias, or through `contact_person_id` where that is filled; it exports it through `exportForPrivacy` and calls `clearForErasure` by id under the same direction rules. A provider-only account (a Discord id) with no alias is not found; under tenant encryption no writer links its conversation to a person, so every such run reports the unlinked provider-only conversations in scope as unresolved coverage |
| A conversation whose contact attribution to the erased person has been independently verified | Every link's message-as-received cleared; headers reduced to message ids; subjects, sender identifiers, hashes and names cleared. Every inbound linked message, email included, is handed to the messages service even if its old address matches no current alias. External reactions attributable to the subject are deleted, including their provider ids; reactions on cleared inbound messages whose external actor cannot be distinguished are deleted and counted |
| An external message found only by the sender’s hash | Withhold its content and report `ALIAS_ATTRIBUTION_UNVERIFIED`. Refuse the erase plan before any data or history changes |
| A staff user's own channel | **Disconnected**; external identifier, display name, state, `last_error` and `credentials_ref` cleared. Only people-ops' `integrations.user_credentials` deletes personal credential rows, after every records class succeeds, by a fresh scoped user-id listing |
| Inbound email in `inbox_ops` tied to the person | **Cleared, with a pseudonymous deduplication shell retained**: subject replaced by a localized placeholder; `raw_text`, `raw_html`, `cleaned_text`, `thread_messages`, forwarding address/name, `to_address`, `reply_to`, `processing_error` and metadata cleared (required address columns use empty placeholders). Proposal summary, participants and translations; action description, payload and execution error; discrepancy description, expected and found values cleared. After commit, each cleared proposal's `inbox_ops:inbox_proposal` entry is removed from the search index through DI `searchIndexer.deleteRecord({ entityId, recordId, tenantId })`, as the module emits no CRUD index event. Under the erasure spec's assumption 24 this is index refresh, disclosed rather than verified: a failed removal is reported through `reportError`, does not change the committed section, and is covered by the report's `indexLifecycle` disclosure and its repair commands (`mercato search reindex --tenant <id> --entity inbox_ops:inbox_proposal --purgeFirst`). Lookup rows deleted. `attachment_ids` released and emptied. Rows, message ids, references and unkeyed `content_hash` retained solely for deduplication and explicitly reported |
| The email's copy in `messages` (`inbox_ops.email`) | Cleared under the inbound rule: subject, body, action data, linked-record copies, address, name and hash. The row is kept. The inbox_ops class asks the messages module's service to clear the messages whose source is `inbox_ops:inbox_email` with the email's id and whose type is `inbox_ops.email`, and reports the result as its own section. A reply copy (`inbox_ops.reply`) is staff's message to the customer, found by the customer's address, and follows the outbound rule |
| A message derived from a cleared inbound message | Cleared under the same rule. A Discord AI reply proposal is stored as a message whose source is `messages.message` and the inbound message's id, with a summary of it as subject and a drafted reply as body |
| A phone call with the person as a participant | Every participant row's number, name, email, both lookup hashes, `provider_participant_id` and metadata cleared; provider snapshot and facts, recording URL and recording attachment cleared; recording file released; call marked |

Each handler blanks through an internal function of its module. Internal blanking writes no undo snapshot or audit entry containing cleared values. Per **R3-1 — The audit-history rule for every erasure** (erasure spec, “The audit-history rule for every erasure (R3-1)” and API Contracts, “Audit history — `audit_logs`”), `historyRecords` includes every edited or deleted message, recipient/token/object row, channel/conversation/link/reaction, inbox email/proposal/action/discrepancy, call/participant and delegated notification/delivery record, including rows owned by other people. Delegated services return their own history records. Dry run discovers these before the first write; the erasure engine owns the two history-strip passes and execution-time undo/redo refusal (**DEC-B — The audit-history strip routine**), not these handlers.

**Pages (C4 rule 1).** Every class erases in the erasure spec's bounded pages (“The run”, rule 1; assumption 22): one transaction changes at most 500 rows of one section, and the class returns an opaque `next` cursor with `done: false` until that section is exhausted. A correspondent of several years can hold thousands of rows, so the high-volume sections are the messages class's recipient rows and cleared message bodies and the inbox class's emails and their privacy-lookup participant rows; every other section pages the same way. `messagePrivacyService.clearForErasure` and `channelPrivacyService.clearMessageCopiesForErasure` take the caller's `cursor` and return `next`/`done`, which the delegating class returns as its own cursor, so a delegated clear never commits more than one page in a transaction. Each page is idempotent: it recomputes its candidates from current data, and a row already cleared is skipped. Because forwards, derived messages and inbox copies are reached only through a parent, and C4's cursor is only a hint, a row through which others are found is cleared in the same page as those rows or after them, never before: an inbound or derived message after its selected forwards and derived copies, an inbox email and its privacy-lookup rows after its `messages` copies, and an external message's sender identifier and hash after the messages handed on from it. When they do not fit in one page, the earlier pages clear only the reached rows and the parent waits. A crash between pages therefore leaves the parent's alias or source key in place, and the resumed run finds the remaining rows again, so it completes from the run's last checkpoint without repeating or skipping a row. Dry run plans the same pages without writes; its planned history identities are the union over all pages.

Files are released through the erasure spec's attachments seam inside the clearing transaction, passing its exact trailing `{ em }` options with the current transaction manager, with that subject run's `attachmentOrigin` as the seam's `origin`; only the durable pending-deletion drain runs after commit. Cleared inbound and derived messages release their attachments; retained outbound and staff messages keep theirs. Inbox `attachment_ids` use `releaseByIds` with the expected inbox owner, verified by the attachments service; foreign ownership is skipped and reported as incomplete file cleanup, never silently called erased. For this path `messages` uses the seam, not its existing direct deletes.

### Lookup-key readiness

Before any address-hash discovery, export or erasure, consume the shared encryption owner’s `lookupKeyReadiness.verify({ tenantId, purpose }): Promise<{ verified: true; epoch: string }>` proof from completed B1 hash maintenance. The service loads the authoritative persisted epoch itself, never trusts a caller epoch, and compares current-process key material through the existing purpose-key derivation/check seam against the trusted persisted writer epoch; it returns no key or identifier hash. Purposes cover every participating email/phone hash owner. A missing proof, mismatch, pending rotation or incomplete module fill refuses with `LOOKUP_KEY_UNVERIFIED` before any write; record-id matches cannot hide an unverified alias path. First installation establishes the proof only through trusted owner initialization; a read-only privacy process cannot establish its own baseline. B1 rotation advances it only after all relevant fills verify. A two-process integration fixture writes with key A and discovers/erases with key B, and must refuse rather than report zero matches; verified same-epoch empty data remains a valid empty answer.

### Forwards

A forward copies a visibility-filtered slice ending at the selected message. Its direction is no guide to the direction of the copied content. `messagePrivacyService.clearForErasure` follows exact source membership for every message it clears under the inbound rule, including derived and inbox copies.

- **Recorded membership.** Save the selected `forwarded_from_message_id` and server-produced `forward_source_lineage` in the forward's owner transaction with its body and material copies. The closed lineage records scoped UUIDs only: the server-rendered excerpt's ordered message IDs, source/projected attachment ID pairs and source/projected linked-object snapshot ID pairs. Validate the actor's thread/channel visibility and the actual owning tenant and organization for every edge. A forward that reuses a forward as input links the immediate contributors and is traversed transitively. Keep lineage while descendants or replay can refer to it. Authoritative edges drive export, dry run and apply. Traverse indexed reverse membership with keyset pages of at most 100, deduplicate and detect cycles, and recheck the same edge set under the tenant/thread lock before writing. A changing or unexhausted graph is incomplete.
- **Legacy candidates.** A row without complete lineage is not proven to have copied its parent or every earlier message. Enumerate potential legacy forwards in the scoped thread and its parent chain using the selected parent, the real sent/created/id slice order and the historical forwarding actor's visibility where those facts can be proven. Include a row when the cleared message could have been a copied contributor through that slice or copied object lineage. Missing historical visibility, uncertain ordering or absent typed lineage conservatively keeps plausible candidates, regardless of subject wording. Exclude only where trustworthy facts disprove membership. Return `legacy_forward_ambiguous` and preserve all of a candidate's content pending authorized owner review. That scoped audited review records a confirmed forward with exact relevant source memberships and copy identifiers, or an independently authored/non-member decision under the same thread lock. Record only verified ids, the decision and a non-personal case reference. A title match or no surviving evidence never authorizes destruction. Recheck reviewed decisions and include exact lineage and resulting writes in the approved digest.
- **Outcome.** A selected forward is cleared under the inbound rule whatever its own direction: subject, body, action data, error text, `message_objects.entity_snapshot`, outside address, name and hash. Its own attachment rows are released through the seam like any cleared message's. Its notification copies are cleared through Source notification copies. It counts as a derived message in history records, export and the run report.
- **Shared file bytes.** A forward's attachment rows are new rows sharing the source rows' `storage_path`. Under C4's attachments contract, bytes still referenced by a surviving row are kept and reported as `shared_record` (`shared_files`), never deleted. An inbound file therefore survives through any row outside this traversal that shares it, and the run reports it as such.
- **Export.** A subject's export lists the selected forwards as copies of the messages they sent, flagged `freeText`.

**Derivative admission.** The messages owner maintains a durable tenant/thread privacy generation and a cleared-source tombstone, including for deleted sources. Forward creation and every source-derived message/proposal producer (Discord AI replies and inbox-to-messages copies included) read generation with their source snapshot. In the insertion transaction they acquire the same scoped thread lock used by erasure, verify the captured generation and reread the current source. A changed generation or cleared/missing source refuses the captured derivative; it may only start a new operation from independently safe current content. Erasure advances the generation and marks the source before traversing dependants, under that lock; each bounded page reuses it. Provider/model work may run outside the lock, but its result never commits without this final check. Cross-module producers call the messages-owned DI admission service and do not read its tables. Generation/tombstone cleanup cannot precede expiry of every producer retry capable of referring to that source; unknown retry lifetime preserves the tombstone. Planned `derivativeErasureRace.test.ts` pauses forward and AI producers after source read, completes erasure, resumes them and proves no text, snapshot or file reference is recreated. The reverse ordering proves erasure traverses an already committed derivative. Potential legacy candidates remain untouched until their membership is reviewed.

**Forward preview and edits.** `GET /api/messages/[id]/forward-preview` keeps `subject` and `body` and adds an opaque, short-lived `previewProof` binding tenant, organization, actor, thread, selected source, current privacy generation, ordered visible source membership and a digest of the server-rendered preview. Issue and validate it through the messages owner using the shared JWT signing helper with a distinct forward-preview purpose and key domain. Bound the token and use a digest of ordered membership rather than embedding a variable-length list. Include no rendered text, personal field or attachment metadata in the proof or audit/log output. The Composer sends this proof with its supported edited body to the existing `POST /api/messages/[id]/forward` route. Before commit acquire the scoped thread lock, re-read eligibility, re-render the exact now-visible slice and verify generation, membership and digest before accepting body, attachment or linked-object copies. A mismatch returns localized `FORWARD_PREVIEW_STALE` (409) with instructions to refresh. The Composer refreshes from the current source and does not silently resubmit the prior copied body; separately entered additions can be kept as an explicit draft for review. Do not classify deliberate manual quotations as copied preview content. During the preceding compatibility minor, ship the additive proof and updated Composer first. Deprecate edited-body requests without proof with a target enforcement version and sanitized compatibility telemetry. Make no protected-forwarding claim for that legacy path. On protected activation, a legacy request without proof can still send a server-built current copy, including when its supplied body exactly equals the current rendering; a differing legacy full body requires a fresh proof instead of being trusted as current. Upgrade notes document the refusal and compatibility window under the existing approval process. A protection gate refuses activation while required first-party writers or readers lack the proof contract.

Quotations a colleague typed into their own reply are not forwards and stay a known limit.

### Source notification copies

`notificationService.clearSourceForErasure({ scope, callerModuleId, sourceEntityType, sourceEntityIds, dryRun }, { em })` is the generic notifications seam defined in people-ops coverage, “Source notification copies”. `callerModuleId` is the id of the caller's own module, such as `messages`, `workflows`, `sales`, `checkout`, `warranty_claims` or `staff`; it is a module id, not a data-class id, because some callers, such as a retention set, are not data classes. Before any read or write, the service refuses the call when `callerModuleId` is not an enabled module, or when `sourceEntityType` does not begin with `` `${callerModuleId}:` ``. The one exception is the messages module's existing unprefixed `message` (`messages/subscribers/message-notification.ts`), accepted only with `callerModuleId: 'messages'`, so no stored notification type changes. It takes at most 100 source ids per call and runs in the caller's transaction. Rows are matched by `source_entity_type` and `source_entity_id`; the nullable `notifications.source_module`, which holds the notification type's module, is not relied on. Phase 1 delivers it. The messages service calls it with `callerModuleId: 'messages'` and `sourceEntityType: 'message'` for every cleared message, including derived and inbox copies; a delegating channels or inbox class passes through the messages service, which makes that call itself. The notifications module blanks `title`, `body`, title/body variables, `action_data`, `action_result`, arbitrary data, source-bearing links/group keys and push overrides that can carry copied text, keeping only ids and the localized cleared-message template. It calls a new push module service by notification ids to clear matching delivery payloads, `provider_response` and error text. Each service owns its tables and returns `{ affected, historyRecords, sections }`, with `sections: PrivacyErasureSectionResult[]` containing the exact retained shell evidence below and separate cleared sections. Dry run and apply obtain actual notification/delivery survivor and history identities from those owners; callers cannot infer them from message ids or recipient counts. This is a source-based operation across every recipient in the checked tenant/organization, not a deletion of colleagues' inbox rows. Missing required service support leaves the section incomplete. Delivered device or email copies cannot be recalled and are reported as a limit.

### Inbox lookup and processing barrier

`inbox_ops` adds `inbox_email_privacy_participants`, with tenant, organization, email id, nullable `contact_entity_id`, nullable `contact_entity_type`, and nullable `email_hash`. Parsing writes hashes for every parsed participant address, including the original sender in parsed threads; extraction adds the enriched participants' typed CRM matches and hashes before any action executes. The lookup is independent of proposal status. The hash is `hashForLookup` over trimmed, lower-case email with no context, written whether encryption is on or off; matching uses the contract's `lookupHashCandidates`. `mercato inbox_ops fill-privacy-lookups --tenant <id> [--after <cursor>] [--batch-size <1..100>] [--apply]` decrypts existing emails/proposals and fills empty typed matches/hashes under C1's initial-coverage convention; non-empty hash repair belongs to B1 Phase D's “Coverage-owned hash refreshes”, which invokes this owner implementation under “Maintenance write safety”, recomputing verified addresses under the current secret rather than treating generic coverage-fill permission as overwrite authority; the contract's bounded decrypting fallback covers incomplete fills and reports truncation as incomplete. Register the hash for B1 Phase D rehashing (DEC-F). Lookup rows are coverage-declared under this class and removed on erasure; they are internal, not user-editable.

A locked email in `processing` is retained as `open_case`, and its copies are left and reported for retry after processing. All worker claims, reprocess requests and erasure serialize on that email row. Otherwise erasure sets `inbox_emails.anonymized_at` in the same transaction as clearing; every claim/reprocess refuses a marked row. The final extraction write and message-copy creation re-check the locked row. A worker paused after claim therefore prevents erasure from claiming success; a late queued reprocess cannot restore cleared text. Redelivery still meets the retained deduplication keys.

### Schema and behaviour changes

- **`messages.direction`** — `internal`, `inbound` or `outbound` — is server-only. Trusted compose callers may set it; `inbox_ops` sets `inbound` on email copies and `outbound` on staff reply copies. Omitted direction is inbound for trusted `inboundFromChannel`, outbound for a server-resolved `sourceChannelType` or an outside address, and internal otherwise. Client request schemas omit it and `POST /api/messages` strips it before validation.
  - `mercato communication_channels fill-message-directions` copies each existing link's stored direction through `messagePrivacyService.recordDirections`; conflicting evidence remains unresolved. Non-channel rows with an outside address follow the verified compose rule only when their source is known; ambiguous rows remain null.
  - `mercato inbox_ops fill-message-directions` marks `inbox_ops.email` inbound and `inbox_ops.reply` outbound, correcting prior fills; the channels command leaves those source-owned rows alone, so order does not matter.
  - `messages.checkErasable` refuses before any mutation with `MESSAGE_DIRECTION_UNRESOLVED` while any matched message needs direction to choose its retain or clear rule and has missing or conflicting direction. This covers external chat or text without email, typed/source-only matches, delegated message ids and replies. A forward already covered by the always-clear rule needs no direction. Recheck the same predicate under the affected message lock before write, with the same refusal if the source state changed. Dry run counts unresolved rows and the refusal names both fill commands and the messages-owned review path for conflicting evidence. `mercato messages review-direction --tenant <id> --message <id> --direction <inbound|outbound|internal> --case-reference <code>` defaults to a scoped preview returning only candidate direction/source codes and a digest bound to the requested decision. A trusted operator verifies the authoritative origin against the channel owner's independent delivery/ingest record or the controller's reviewed case record. The case reference identifies that evidence without accepting or logging subject values. Applying the same intent with `--confirm <digest> --apply` requires authorized operator access and that verified evidence. Under the message lock recheck actual row tenant/organization, unresolved state and evidence/digest, then atomically record the decision and an audit with message ID, old/new direction, closed evidence kind and bounded case reference. Reject guessed, stale, unsupported, foreign or unprovable decisions. A valid reviewed decision outranks disputed channel history; later appearance of different authoritative evidence invalidates it and returns the message to unresolved. Keep verified inbox-owned corrections in their owner path.
- **Portal erasure authority.** Discovery by email, phone, forwarding address or provider alias remains available for controller-reviewed C4. For portal C4 and recovery, `messages.messages`, `communication_channels.conversations`, `inbox_ops.emails` and `phone_calls.calls` declare `portalAccountErasure: 'controller_review'` for their portal-account matches. A matching alias, the derived CRM person, a source-message lookup or a hash shared with another account establishes no ownership. Under `ownership: { kind: 'portal_account', accountId }`, a potentially applicable match of this kind returns `PORTAL_ATTRIBUTION_AMBIGUOUS` in pre-flight, with zero dependent mutations even if other classes have exact rows. A future owner-persisted account binding would require a separate covered path.
- **Hashes** are written by each module and filled by its command, with the contract's bounded fallback: `messages.external_email_hash`, `external_messages.sender_identifier_hash`, both call participant hashes, and the inbox lookup table's `email_hash`. Email uses the contract's normalized form with no context; phone participants use its shared digits-only helper. The B1 sender hash retains B1's stored-identifier normalization; formatted phone senders use the bounded fallback, hashing `normalizePhoneDigits` of verified decrypted text against `phone_hashes`, with truncation reported incomplete. Do not assume that stored sender hashes use digits-only normalization. Initial coverage fills use C1's locked empty-only convention. Non-empty old-secret repair is expressly owned by B1 Phase D, “Coverage-owned hash refreshes” and “Maintenance write safety”; its coordinated refreshes cover all four modules and must recompute stale non-null values under the current secret.
- **Forward source fields.** Nullable `messages.forwarded_from_message_id` and closed `forward_source_lineage` are written only by the messages owner, never accepted from the client. New forwards require complete owner-produced membership. Older potential candidates use the audited process in Forwards rather than fabricated membership. The owner indexes scoped reverse source membership and declares closed UUID-only lineage decisions for C5/C8.
- **`phone_calls.anonymized_at`** is committed with clearing participants and the other identifying fields. On an ingest update, even for a call loaded earlier, take the same scoped call-row lock as erasure in the owner write transaction and re-read the committed mark before any participant rewrite, snapshot/provider update or recording link. On a concurrent first insert collision, reload the winning scoped call under that lock before taking the update branch. A marked call updates only status, timings and duration. Do not publish identifying attachment side effects outside the guard. If ingest locks first, erasure waits and clears its committed updates; if erasure locks first, ingest cannot restore the identifying fields.
- **Daily recipient counter**: add an idempotent scheduled worker, registered by `messages/setup.ts`, that deletes `message_external_recipient_days` rows whose UTC day is older than seven days, per tenant in batches of at most 100. Its hash has a separate context and cannot be matched by subject; the privacy table declaration is bounded by this new prune, not by an existing cleanup. This is an operational counter, not subject history.
- **Indexes** are tenant-led on every hash, inbox typed contact lookup, message source and `forwarded_from_message_id`. Existing indexes stay; discovery by `message_objects` typed link uses the existing `(entity_type, entity_id)` index and then checks the tenant through the message.

### Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Delete every message an erased person took part in | Destroys other people's records. |
| Keep the text an outside person sent | See assumption 2. |
| Decide by "the only outside party" | A message stores one address; copied-in parties are in another module's JSON. Not decidable. |
| A subject whose id is the address's hash | The hash would sit in audit entries, events and export documents and outlive the erasure. |
| Delete inbound mail in `inbox_ops` | See assumption 6. |
| Clear one phone participant | Ingest replaces the whole set; a row has no identity to keep. |
| Find mentions by scanning | See assumption 7. |

### Known limits

- A person quoted in a retained staff reply, copied in on someone else's message, or named in a call's summary is not found.
- A provider-only chat account, including a Discord id with no email or phone, cannot be selected by the contract, and no writer links its conversation to a CRM person today: adapters supply no `matchedPersonId`, and the plaintext lookup is skipped under tenant encryption. Its messages are not found. Every communication run reports this provider-account limit with the count of unlinked provider-only conversations in scope as unresolved coverage; no unsupported identifier flag is added.
- Under tenant encryption `contact_person_id` stays empty, so an inbound email sent from an address the person no longer has, and that no current alias matches, is not found through its conversation.
- In a conversation whose contact is someone else, an erased person who was only copied in keeps their address in that conversation's stored headers.
- A participant named only in free text, with no extraction-time CRM match, action match or parsed email address, is not found. Pending and rejected proposals with a typed extraction-time match are covered.
- A person already anonymized in the CRM has no address left to compute aliases from; a class released later cannot find their address-keyed messages.
- Two people who share an address share its hash.
- In OSS, the only way to keep correspondence that is needed for a dispute is not to run the erasure; a legal hold is an enterprise control.
- A staff user has no owner-provided address alias today. Mail they forwarded to the inbox by email carries only their address in `forwarded_by_address`/name and in the outside address/name of its `inbox_ops.email` copy in `messages`, and address-only call participants carry no user id; none of these can be reached by staff id. Text extractions they submitted in the app, with the submitter's address on the extraction and on its `messages` copy, and their review and execution attribution, are reached by id. Staff exports omit address-only forwarded mail and calls; every staff run lists these gaps as unresolved coverage, alongside its id-based results. This is not a claim of complete staff erasure.
- Send-as-user mail stores only its first `to` address on the message. Its other `to`, `cc` and `bcc` recipients are found only if the message is matched another way, through its first address, sender or a typed link; the subject stored as the outside name is cleared with it.
- Retained staff and outbound correspondence, its linked-object rows and its files have no automatic end (Outcomes and run evidence); each run names it as an unbounded subject-data limit with the Art. 17(3) ground the controller must assert.
- Inbound file bytes shared with a row outside the forward traversal survive as `shared_record` until that row goes.
- Delivered push/email copies and calls at the telephony provider remain outside this database erasure.

## 📝 API Contracts

- The contract owns aliases and identifier derivation (DEC-A), registry discovery (R3-2) and fallback bounds. The erasure spec owns pre-flight, all history records and both strip passes (R3-1/DEC-B), and attachments. No new privacy-contract member.

Selector is `{ messageIds }` or `{ source: { entityType, entityIds, messageType } }`. Export returns deduplicated sections flagged `freeText`, including truncation. Clear handles derived messages, forwards (Forwards), linked-object snapshots, attachments and notifications, one page of at most 500 rows per section per call (What erasure does, “Pages”), and returns `{ affected, historyRecords, sections, next?, done }`, carrying exact C4 retained shell/file evidence from every owner. Callers preserve those sections in their class results. `checkForErasure` returns the selected history records and unresolved-direction count; the messages, channels and inbox classes call it from their own `checkErasable` for every id/source selector before the erasure engine permits a write. This scrub never calls the messages service again, so the reverse hand-off cannot recurse.
- Notification and push methods follow Source notification copies. Existing signatures stay. Creation/delivery retries re-read cleared source message/notification state, rather than inserting captured text; a cleared source renders the localized placeholder. Already delivered copies remain a reported limit.
- **Compose:** optional `direction` belongs only to the trusted command schema. Omit it from `composeMessageRequestSchema`, strip it in `POST /api/messages`, and derive it from server facts. A channel reply without an address is outbound. Existing URL, ACL and response contracts stay; OpenAPI uses the client schema.
- **Credential method:** DI `integrationCredentialsService` gains, delivered by program package A9 together with `listByUser` and `hardDeleteByUser`, `deleteUserCredential({ scope: PrivacyScope, userId, credentialId })`, which hard-deletes the named row only with `tenant_id = scope.tenantId`, `user_id = userId` and, when present, `(organization_id = scope.organizationId OR organization_id IS NULL)`. Tenant-wide calls keep each row's actual organization predicate. Include soft-deleted rows; never act on `user_id IS NULL`, another user or another tenant. Validate with zod; use the caller's transactional manager through DI, return safe metadata/history only, and write no secret snapshot. Missing row is idempotent. All channel/A9/repair callers pass the trusted scope. A9's channel cascade calls this method. No credential values enter the history plan. The signature above is A9's apart from the scope type's name, which becomes `PrivacyScope` once C1 Phase 1 has landed; the shapes are identical, so the change breaks no caller.

Use immutable UUID keysets, bounded batch transactions, locked empty-only initial coverage writes and an opaque scope-bound continuation with scanned/filled/skipped counts. Preview writes neither database nor KMS. Rerun from the start catches earlier-id insertions. Source-specific correction of a known erroneous inbox direction follows the explicit direction rule above, never a generic hash overwrite. No implicit all-tenant decryption or plaintext/hash logging.

It uses one implementation and B1's stored-sender normalization; the canonical command resumes by rerunning from the start, while the wrapper's cursor only narrows its bounded coverage traversal. It introduces no new canonical B1 flag.
- **Non-empty hash maintenance:** B1 Phase D's “Coverage-owned hash refreshes” explicitly coordinates `messages`, `inbox_ops`, `communication_channels` and `phone_calls`; its owning commands recompute stale non-null old-secret hashes from verified decrypted sources under the current secret, leaving already-current targets unchanged. The named commands route empty targets through C1's initial-fill writer and stale populated targets through B1's explicitly owned maintenance updater; no extra CLI mode or flag is invented. This is the B1 maintenance/rehash path, not implicit permission for any new coverage fill to overwrite populated hashes. Apply its “Maintenance write safety”: exact tenant/null-safe actual organization, conditional source-and-target comparison, per-row savepoints, no `updated_at` bump or protected-value snapshot. Missing secret/keys refuse before mutation; unreadable source, collision or concurrent change preserves that row and leaves maintenance incomplete. Ordinary writers leave null without a lookup secret. ## 📝 Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| A matched message that requires direction, including email-less external chat, with missing or conflicting evidence | Pre-flight refuses with unresolved count, both fill commands and the audited direction review path, before writes |
| The messages hash fill has not run for a tenant | Rows with an outside address and no hash are scanned by decrypting, within the contract's bound; the run says the scan was used |
| Encryption is off | The module writes the address hash itself, so the address is still found |
| The email's copy in `messages` | Found by its source, not by its outside address, which is the forwarder's; cleared with the email |
| The channels fill runs after the inbox_ops fill | It writes only rows with no direction, so the copies stay inbound |
| A message derived from a cleared inbound message | Cleared under the same rule |
| A staff user who is the fallback sender of inbound mail | That mail is not theirs; it is handled for its outside sender |
| A person with several addresses | Every alias hash is matched |
| A number stored in a different format from the CRM's | Both sides reduce to digits before hashing |
| A conversation with a company's shared mailbox | The company is not a person; nothing is cleared |
| An executed proposal's order or message | Still points at the proposal row, which remains |
| A recording stored as an attachment | Released through the seam |
| A call still in progress | `retained` / `open_case`, `retainedUntil: null`; retry after completion |
| Tables with no tenant column | Reached only through their message, whose tenant is checked first |
| The run's process has a different lookup secret from the app | Refuse address-based discovery/apply with `LOOKUP_KEY_UNVERIFIED`; no zero-match or complete result |
| `erase --email` or `--phone` with no lookup secret configured | Refused with exit 1, as the erasure spec says |
| A records class fails after disconnection | Credentials remain; deletion belongs only to the later credentials group |
| Pending/rejected proposal | Found through typed extraction-time match without executing an action |
| Unmatched original sender | Found by parsed address hash as identifier subject; redelivery stays duplicate |
| Staff address-only forwarding or call details | Reported as unresolved staff coverage, not called erased |
| A forward written before exact source lineage | A plausible slice member remains ambiguous and preserved regardless of the subject. Affected completion waits for an audited owner membership decision |
| A forward on a channel thread | Filed internal with no outside address, still cleared because it copies the inbound text |
| A forward of a forward | The scoped traversal follows reviewed immediate memberships transitively |
| A staff user with a personal channel and no `integrations.user_credentials` class enabled | Pre-flight refuses before any write |
| A subject with more than 500 recipient rows, messages or inbox emails | Erased in pages of at most 500 rows per section per transaction with a checkpoint each; a parent through which forwards, derived messages or copies are found is cleared with or after them, so a crash between pages resumes without repeating or skipping a row |
| Tenant encryption on | `contact_person_id` is empty; chat and email messages are found by sender aliases only, and unlinked provider-only conversations are counted as unresolved coverage |

## 📝 Risks & Impact Review

#### A credential is deleted before the records barrier
- **Scenario**: a records-group handler deletes a mailbox credential, so a later records failure leaves the account recoverable without it.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: personal credentials and retry safety
- **Mitigation**: The channels class only disconnects and clears pointers in records; `integrations.user_credentials` alone deletes, after every records class succeeds, by a fresh user-id listing. Unit tests and TC-PRIVACY-COMM-002 assert no records-stage deletion.
- **Residual risk**: A credentials-group failure after disconnect leaves the account locked, with its channels disconnected, until a re-run, as C4 states.

#### The company loses a record it needed
- **Scenario**: A customer's inbound emails are cleared and a dispute follows.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: the tenant's correspondence
- **Mitigation**: The dry run lists what would be cleared; staff-written replies are kept; an enterprise hold stops the run; assumption 2 states the rule.
- **Residual risk**: Irreversible once applied, and OSS has no hold.

#### Staff text is cleared by mistake
- **Scenario**: Direction has not been filled; guessing inbound would destroy staff text.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: messages written before the upgrade
- **Mitigation**: Pre-flight refuses unresolved matches before any mutation, naming both fills and the audited owner review. Trusted-only direction blocks client spoofing.
- **Residual risk**: An incorrect trusted writer or fill; the refusal protects missing direction only.

#### An erased person's words remain
- **Scenario**: They are quoted in a reply by staff, which is kept, or a colleague forwarded their message inside the company.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: erasure completeness
- **Mitigation**: Forwards follow scoped exact source membership irrespective of direction. Preserve unresolved legacy candidates pending audited owner review. Fence preview reuse before publishing an edited copy. Manually typed quotes remain a disclosed limit.
- **Residual risk**: Quoted text in retained messages, and inbound file bytes shared with a surviving row outside the traversal, reported as `shared_record`.

#### Chat messages are not found in production
- **Scenario**: With tenant encryption on, ingest never fills `contact_person_id`, so a chat conversation whose sender has no email or phone alias is linked to nobody.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: chat channels, Discord in particular
- **Mitigation**: Discovery relies on sender aliases, which work under encryption; every run counts unlinked provider-only conversations as unresolved coverage instead of claiming completeness.
- **Residual risk**: Those conversations are not exported or erased until a writer links them to a person; none exists today.

#### An outside correspondent is not found
- **Scenario**: Encryption is off, or a message predates the hash, so `external_email_hash` is empty and an erasure reports nothing found.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: messages to and from outside addresses
- **Mitigation**: `messages` writes the hash itself whether or not encryption is on, a fill command covers existing rows, and the fallback scan runs until it has. The dry run says when the scan was used.
- **Residual risk**: Missing or mismatched persisted lookup readiness blocks the operation until the owner completes verified hash maintenance; it never produces a clean zero-match result.

#### An inbound email survives in `messages`
- **Scenario**: A customer matched in inbox_ops is erased, and the email's copy in `messages` keeps the whole thread, because its outside address is the forwarder's and nothing marks it inbound.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: inbound mail copied by inbox_ops
- **Mitigation**: The inbox_ops class clears its copies by source through the messages service, under the inbound rule. Copies are composed as inbound from now on, and the inbox_ops fill marks existing ones. TC-PRIVACY-INBOX-001 checks the copy.
- **Residual risk**: Text staff copied from the email into a message of their own, as with any quote.

#### Other people's call details are cleared
- **Scenario**: A call had two outside parties and one is erased.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: that call
- **Mitigation**: Stated in assumption 8; the dry run reports every call it will clear, before apply.
- **Residual risk**: Accepted; in Enterprise a hold keeps the call.

#### A retained deduplication digest remains linkable
- **Scenario**: An operator guesses subject/sender/body text and recomputes inbox_ops' unkeyed SHA-256 content hash.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: retained inbox deduplication shells
- **Mitigation**: Report the hash and message ids as retained pseudonymous data solely for redelivery prevention; never label the shell anonymous.
- **Residual risk**: Guess confirmation remains possible while the deduplication key is kept.

#### The wrong person's messages are cleared
- **Scenario**: Two people used one address.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: address-keyed messages
- **Mitigation**: The dry run shows the count found by address.
- **Residual risk**: A shared mailbox used by several private people.

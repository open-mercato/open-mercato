# Durable authentication lifecycle audit

> **Program.** G1b of the [GDPR-ready deployments program](../../GDPR/2026-11-15-gdpr-ready-deployments-program.md), issue #8593. Enterprise `security` owns the history and retention. Any OSS producer seams require written DEC-2 carrier approval.

## TLDR

Persist scoped evidence for logout, password changes and completed resets, and applicable MFA changes. The security audit survives a collector outage after a successful operation and has its own scheduled retention. Sign-in attempt history and threshold alerts keep their separate owners.

## Problem statement

The baseline has auth and MFA events and sign-in/alert proposals. An emitted event or threshold alert is not a durable receipt proving that each sensitive operation occurred. The umbrella requires evidence for the underlying operations with a dedicated lifetime.

## Proposed solution

The staff `auth`, `customer_accounts` and Enterprise `security` mutation owners provide committed receipts for staff and portal logout, password change and reset completion, and each shipped MFA enrollment, removal, reset/recovery and bypass. Include the controller-initiated operations and shipped MFA methods in the closed inventory below; additions to that inventory require matching acceptance coverage before claiming completeness. The authenticated scope comes from the owning operation. Use a stable producer occurrence id. No receipt is based on raw request or event payloads and no delivered notification stands in for proof.

When protection is configured, use an approved transaction-aware optional seam for a durable owner receipt in the same transaction as the operation, or enqueue the receipt through an owner outbox written in that transaction. A committed receipt never depends on the collector being available. A covered MFA mutation uses one owner occurrence for G1b and the [G2 signal recovery adapter](2026-11-16-security-event-alerting.md#security-module-events-additive), when the same tenant, organization, operation, occurrence time and failure handling apply. Each required consumer acknowledges its own durable processing independently. Owner cleanup waits for both. A queued notice cannot substitute for an acknowledgement. A failed operation cannot record successful evidence. Logout follows the explicit owner protocol below; it never reports durable revocation when its authoritative store cannot commit. An emergency exception requires owner and maintainer approval under the program compatibility rules and must surface an observable incomplete audit state. Legacy OSS without the Enterprise carrier reports the history as unavailable. Do not silently switch an activated protected collector to a best-effort event.

## Architecture and data models

The producer accesses only its own state or an approved transaction-aware DI seam. The collector owns a scoped `security_authentication_audit_events` store with append-only occurrence fields and the monotonic internal settlement metadata defined below. Its rows contain a UUID, tenant id, authoritative optional organization id, occurrence time, source/occurrence id, closed operation kind, outcome and actor/target opaque identifiers. Uniqueness across tenant, producer and occurrence id makes redelivery a no-op. Retain no password, hash, recovery code, token, email address, IP/user agent or request body in this store. Unconsumed receipts are not expired by normal history cleanup. Register the daily `security.authentication_lifecycle_history` set using the retention contract below. Stop and report the retention gap if its runner or required hold guard is unavailable; unverified receipt settlement preserves the affected rows through the terminal-marker predicate below.

Classify the history table as C5 `retention_bounded` against `security.authentication_lifecycle_history` in the owner’s `privacy.ts`. Register the matching C5 `retentionBounds` declaration and owner handler with exactly the same scope, cutoff, held/unsettled state, runner status and row-eligibility rules as actual deletion. Under DEC-9, extend the security-owned `securityPrivacyTableReader` and add the scoped C8 `tableExports` entry for the same table. Include its validated fields in scope and forbid foreign-tenant, borrowed-owner or generic table reads. Use an existing owner outbox where possible. A new owner receipt table ships with C5 `runtime_state` classification and a C8 runtime-state exclusion plus its owner scan/cleanup contract. Pending receipt state is observable and settled receipts are removed by their owner. Unclassified storage or incomplete evidence keeps program closure open.

Expose history only through DI `securityAuthenticationAuditService.list({ scope, after?, limit, operationKinds? }, { principal })`, authorized by `security.authentication_history.view` declared in `security/acl.ts` and explicitly seeded only to the existing super-administrator grant. Use `rbacService.userHasAllFeatures` for server authorization and trusted tenant/organization scope; no role-name or raw ACL matching. Validate input with zod, page 1–100 by `(occurred_at,id)`, and bind opaque cursors to scope/filter. Return `{ items, next? }` containing only the store's approved evidence fields, no owner receipt payload. A missing feature refuses before query. The service applies organization enforcement on every page. An organization-restricted reader cannot see tenant-wide rows unless its authorized scope permits them. Any API/UI beyond the program's current plan needs its own accepted contract and integration tests.

### Lifecycle receipt and settlement interface

`AuthenticationLifecycleReceipt` is a closed validated envelope containing `tenantId`, nullable `organizationId`, `source`, `occurrenceId`, `occurredAt`, `operationKind`, `outcome: 'success'`, and the actor/target identities defined above. `operationKind` accepts exactly the kinds in the closed inventory, not a G2 `eventName`. The source owns and validates its supported subset; unknown kinds or borrowed-source identities refuse. Overlapping MFA operations may share an underlying owner occurrence with G2, but each adapter projects its own typed envelope without widening either consumer's discriminator.

`AuthenticationLifecycleReceiptSource` defines:

- `pagePendingTenants({ after?, limit }): Promise<{ tenantIds: string[]; next?: string }>` for the privileged recovery worker, including tenants with unobserved terminal settlement evidence;
- `pagePending({ tenantId, after?, limit }): Promise<{ occurrences: AuthenticationLifecycleReceipt[]; next?: string }>`; lifecycle occurrences remain discoverable until settlement observation is acknowledged, even after the history processing acknowledgement;
- `acknowledge({ tenantId, organizationId, occurrenceId, consumer: 'security.authentication_history' }): Promise<void>` only after the collector commits the unique history row;
- `readConsumerSettlement({ tenantId, occurrenceId }): Promise<{ status: 'settled'; settlementId: string; requiredConsumers: readonly string[] } | { status: 'pending' } | { status: 'unknown' }>`; `settled` is an immutable owner-issued proof that every consumer required for this occurrence has acknowledged durable processing;
- `acknowledgeSettlementObserved({ tenantId, occurrenceId, settlementId, consumer: 'security.authentication_history' }): Promise<void>` after the collector has durably copied that exact terminal proof to the matching history row.

All methods validate scope, source-bound identities and registered consumers; limits are 1–100 and cursors bind source, tenant where applicable, and projection version. Required consumers are frozen at occurrence commit. A later installation cannot retroactively add an obligation to a settled occurrence. `unknown`, missing, unavailable or throwing settlement reads are never treated as terminal. The G2 adapter keeps its existing independent `security.alerting` acknowledgement contract.

After importing a row, the lifecycle collector acknowledges its processing, then polls the owner settlement using the normal recovery schedule. When settled, it conditionally persists `source_settlement_id`, `source_settled_at` and the validated frozen required-consumer ids on that exact tenant/source/occurrence row, before acknowledging observation. These are collector-owned internal terminal evidence, not user-editable state or copied receipt facts. Their write is idempotent and cannot revert a settled row. The owner keeps the receipt or a minimal id-only terminal proof until observation is acknowledged; a crash before either collector commit or observation acknowledgement therefore remains recoverable. Once observed, ordinary owner cleanup may remove the receipt/proof without waiting for history expiry. History retention relies on its persisted terminal marker thereafter and never infers unsettled state merely because the cleaned owner receipt is absent. This handshake has no cycle: owner consumer acknowledgement waits only for history insertion, settlement observation waits only for the marker commit, and neither waits for history deletion.

TC-PRIVACY-AUTH-AUDIT-001 and the C5 bound/deletion parity tests cover a lifecycle-only password receipt, shared MFA consumers acknowledged in either order, G2 rejection of lifecycle operation kinds, unknown settlement, a crash before/after marker commit and observation acknowledgement, owner receipt cleanup followed by eventual history expiry, and cross-tenant settlement/ack rejection. A row without a terminal marker remains `receipt_unsettled` even if its age is past cutoff; a settled row remains eligible under the ordinary hold/age predicate after its source has been cleaned.

### Logout ordering and failure outcomes

Staff and portal owners remain separate. Each resolves the existing session using its own current credential rules (including expired access token plus valid session cookie), then row-locks that scoped session and commits revocation plus a minimal immutable pending receipt before responding. For staff, use an owner tombstone with `revoked_at`, occurrence UUID/time and pending-consumer state until collection settles; session lookup/refresh treats a tombstone exactly as a deleted session. Portal uses its owner revocation state with the same pending occurrence contract. Do not physically remove pending tombstones, overwrite occurrence identity on retries, or permit refresh from them. A duplicate logout acknowledges already-revoked state without creating another successful occurrence.

The source receipt is owner-local and does not require a collector/outbox destination to be writable. A savepoint isolates optional outbox insertion failure; commit the session revocation and minimal pending marker regardless, then let the owner's recovery adapter reconcile it. The response clears that owner's auth/session and scope cookies even on failure, using the existing names/attributes. A collector/queue failure after this commit cannot roll back revocation. Tests reuse the old server session/refresh credential and require refusal after this committed path, with eventual one-time history import.

If the authoritative owner database cannot commit revocation or a pending marker, clear browser authentication, session and scope cookies and return a sanitized 503 under the protected compatibility disposition. Do not record success or claim server revocation. Existing stateless access-token validity remains governed by the owner token contract. Session-dependent authentication and refresh fail closed while the authoritative store is unavailable.

**Cookie-only retry.** On a same-origin protected logout that cannot commit, the owner can return an independent revocation-only capability in a new owner-specific, host-only, Secure/HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie scoped to its existing logout POST path. Seal it with the shared authenticated-envelope primitive and an owner-specific logout key derived for that purpose from the existing signing-secret material. Use separate purpose bindings for staff and portal. It contains only a schema version, first-request issuance and absolute expiry (15 minutes), the owner/route audience and the bounded original session selector needed to reapply the existing owner credential rules. For staff this is a server-validated signed-session selector and/or original opaque session-cookie credential, precisely as admitted by that logout request. Portal binds the original opaque portal-session credential. The authenticated envelope preserves confidentiality of any selector credential. No selector or capability enters a body, log, audit snapshot, event, telemetry or URL.

The same POST accepts this cookie after clearing the normal cookies. Validate purpose, expiry and same-origin access independently of a logged-in principal. Re-run the exact original owner lookup and scope checks against current persisted rows, take the same scoped lock, and perform only the revocation/receipt transaction. The capability does not authenticate a request, authorize refresh or widen to another session or owner. A verified already-revoked or absent session is a successful no-op without a new occurrence. After commit or terminal verification clear the retry cookie. A second 503 retains the original expiry and never refreshes the capability. Invalid, expired or foreign capabilities cannot authorize revocation and are cleared. If safe capability creation is unavailable, the unavailable response directs the operator to the owner's authorized session revocation procedure. Document loss/expiry recovery without claiming the original logout succeeded. Explicitly approve this failure response, cookie and staff tombstone read path before protected activation. Legacy OSS behavior remains outside that activation.

### Authentication history retention

Declare `security.authentication_lifecycle_history` with `targets: ['records']`, `minimumDays: 90`, `excludableSubjectKinds: ['auth:user', 'customer_accounts:user']`, `unmatchedHoldsOptOut: 'forbidden'`, and `handlerService: 'securityAuthenticationHistoryRetentionHandler'`. Its audited tenant setting is `retention.security.authenticationLifecycleHistoryAgeDays` in C1's privacy settings registry (default 365, integer floor 90). Null/unknown tenant is refused. Every row includes `created_at`/`updated_at`; add `(tenant_id, occurred_at, id)` and `(tenant_id, created_at, id)` indexes for the read and deletion keysets.

Eligibility is `occurred_at < fixedRunStart - effectiveAgeDays` AND the collector-owned terminal settlement marker proves every frozen required owner consumer was durably acknowledged. The collector recovery handshake resolves and persists that marker through the owner adapter; missing/throwing/unknown status leaves an unmarked row `receipt_unsettled`. Retention and its read-only bound evaluator inspect the same persisted marker and never write settlement state or require a cleaned receipt to reappear. Holds on either actor or target preserve the row. Class/set holds skip the set; an unsupported or unresolved subject kind yields `hold_not_honourable` for the whole tenant/set. No unmatched-hold opt-out is allowed. Deletion uses D3's coordinator-owned fenced transaction and `writeContext`, with scoped keyset batches at most 100 and a cutoff/setting version bound into each cursor; changed settings restart/revalidate without narrowing a previously promised window.

A daily `security:authentication-history-retention` system schedule enumerates tenant ids from the history owner, including soft-deleted tenants, and invokes tenant-scoped D3 runs. Persist a fair tenant turn and each cursor; after at most 1,000 examined rows enqueue one coalesced continuation. Missing/disabled schedules, unknown workers or growing backlog remain explicit gaps. C5 `resolve` returns the same configured window, runner state and held/unsettled reasons; `evaluateFixtureRow` uses exactly this predicate and `occurred_at` anchor. Planned parity tests cover both actor and target holds, unrelated-row expiry, unknown hold kinds, unsettled receipts, cutoff boundaries, changed windows, two-tenant fairness and crash/resume. Collection/receipt cleanup and history expiry are separate obligations; neither may erase the other's unacknowledged evidence.

### Capability: authentication-lifecycle-audit

#### ADDED Requirement: Durable, bounded authentication history

- WHEN a named operation commits and its collector is temporarily unavailable
- THEN the scoped receipt remains durable and the next collector pass imports it once
- WHEN a same-origin logout from a cookie-only staff or portal browser cannot commit and the owner then recovers
- THEN the first response clears authentication cookies and reports 503, and the same browser can revoke precisely the original session through its short-lived retry-only cookie without signing in or creating duplicate evidence
- WHEN another tenant or an unauthorized organization reader requests evidence
- THEN the operation's actor, target and occurrence remain inaccessible
- WHEN a settled row is past its configured retention cutoff
- THEN the owner’s scheduled pass deletes it only if no applicable hold or unresolved receipt prevents expiry

## Migration & Backward Compatibility

Add only the protected collector, store, feature and scoped receipt seams. Existing event names, payloads, signatures and routes continue. Any changed failure handling must follow the compatibility contract and its required operator migration notes. No historical evidence is synthesized from absent receipts.

## Risks and failure modes

A state change and receipt in separate transactions can leave permanent audit gaps. Keep the durable confirmation at the operation boundary and test the post-commit interruption. A compromised history reader could infer activity across organizations. Enforce owner scope on every query. Retention must account for pending receipts and holds so deletion cannot falsely complete. The Phase 7 evidence names any accepted scope exception.

## 📝 TLDR

The OSS privacy contract gives an operator with shell access an export and an erase command. A tenant administrator has nothing: no screen to answer a request from, no way to say how long a retention set is kept, no way to stop an erasure while a dispute is open, and no record of what was done. **Proposed (future):** an enterprise module that adds tenant retention policies, legal holds, a bounded ledger of privacy operations with pseudonymous subject references, and one settings page from which an administrator exports and erases a known person — all by calling the OSS operations, never by touching another module's tables.

**Status.** Design settled. The OSS erase run (C4) is OSS under the program's decision DEC-2b, so this spec waits on that run being delivered. Its first phase also needs the contract spec's Phases 6–7 (identifier derivation, `resolveAliases` and R3-3 settings), the complete `privacySubjectService` registration and the record-retention runner and guard type, as **Phasing** lists. Its second phase also needs the export-audit helper and guard, the progress owner transactional seam and portal self-service's deletion worker for TC-PRIVACY-GOV-013. DEC-2b named upstream approvals gate implementation of its proposed OSS carrier dependencies. Its third phase also depends on the retention sets in the OSS record-retention spec and, for erasing people by age, on the inactive-subject spec.

**Not in this spec:** the erase and export operations themselves; finding a person by email or phone from the page, which stays a command because the search can take minutes; restore replay ([`2026-11-16-erasure-restore-replay.md`](2026-11-16-erasure-restore-replay.md)); request-deadline tracking; sandbox sanitization, which is a separate capability.

## 📝 Overview

The engine separates a policy, an execution ledger and a hold. Each module decides what happens to its own records. The ledger stores subject references and counts, without names, addresses or exported values.

## 📝 Problem Statement

- **No administrator surface exists.** The OSS commands need a shell. Nothing in the backend resolves a person, exports or erases.
- **No tenant-level retention exists.** Operational tables get instance-wide windows from the pruner spec; a tenant cannot say that inactive contacts are removed after two years.
- **Nothing can stop an erasure.** The OSS run consults an optional guard (`privacyErasureGuard`) and finds none. The erasure spec defines its method and that a guard which cannot answer counts as a refusal.
- **A person and their address are two subjects.** An erasure given `--email` or `--phone` offers a `privacy:identifier` subject beside whatever person matches, and every class that declares that kind finds records by the address's hashes alone (contract spec, "A subject that is only an identifier"). The guard is called once per subject (erasure spec, guard seam).
- **Nothing stops retention for a held subject.** The OSS record-retention command takes subjects to leave out and consults an optional `privacyRetentionGuard`, and finds none. The first sets declare no excludable kind; change history cannot leave out what sits inside another record's snapshot.
- **No record of privacy operations exists outside the action log**, which is itself subject to retention and cannot be listed by operation.
- **The OSS confirmation token is bound to a command-line dry run.** Any process can check it: it is keyed by a key derived from the installation's lookup secret, as the erasure spec's "The confirmation token" section describes. But it covers the dry-run report that the command printed, and it expires within the hour. A page needs a preview stored for the user who saw it, and it needs a confirmation bound to that user, which is why the engine keeps its own stored preview.
- **Finding a person by identifier is slow.** For CRM people the contract resolves by a decrypting scan that can take minutes.
- **`makeCrudRoute` defaults `orm.orgField` to `organizationId`** (`packages/shared/src/lib/crud/factory.ts:1115`) and stamps the selected organization when that field is configured (`:2601-2606`). Tenant-only routes must explicitly pass `orm.orgField: null`. Its command-backed create returns before `beforeCreate` (`:2381-2571`); factory hooks cannot enforce the module-wide authorization boundary.
- **The earlier enterprise design is not the implementation baseline.** Its request ledger and endpoints are replaced by the bounded service-based design below.
- **Enterprise mechanics in the snapshot.** Existing optional enterprise modules use `OM_ENABLE_ENTERPRISE_MODULES` plus a per-module flag (`apps/mercato/src/modules.ts:244-270`). The new flag must be present when generating the registry. Settings-page metadata uses `page.meta.ts`. The scheduler command allowlist is extended through `registerSchedulerSafeCommands` (`packages/scheduler/src/modules/scheduler/lib/scheduler-safe-commands.ts:50-59`).

## 📝 Proposed Solution

### What the engine adds

| Capability | How |
|---|---|
| **Retention policy** | Per tenant and retention set, either record or subject mode: a window at the set's floor, preview or apply, active or not. Subject sets also take a bounded daily limit, a preference, `skip` or `exclude` and an acknowledgement of disabled required source modules; record sets run until caught up and may use `allowUnmatchedHolds` (default `false`), which only a user who may also release holds can enable |
| **Legal hold** | On a data class or retention set, on a subject by kind and id, or on an address or number typed by the administrator and stored as its derived identifier id; a reason code, a reference, an optional expiry. Created, extended, released; never edited |
| **Guards** | Registers two OSS seams. `privacyErasureGuard`: a subject under an active hold, or belonging to a held class, is refused before the run changes anything. `privacyRetentionGuard`: returns the tenant's held subjects to the record-retention command, which skips a set that cannot leave them out (`hold_not_honourable`, status `skipped`). If a guard cannot read its table, it refuses |
| **Operations ledger** | Engine-started exports, erasures and retention passes; externally announced erasures; explicit replay and acknowledgement operations. Stores subject kind and id, scope, intent and counts, with an encrypted reference. CLI/portal exports and unannounced no-op erasures stay in the action log |
| **Settings page** | Data classes, tenant retention settings, policies, holds, subject requests, operations |

### What it needs from the OSS side

| Need | Where it comes from | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Retention sets** — groups of a module's own records that can be deleted by age, with a `pruneRecords` method that takes a window, a position and the subjects to leave out | [`../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-record-retention.md`](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-record-retention.md) | Specified; notifications and assistant conversations first |
| **Retention candidates** — a filtered list of people with no activity within a window, for the engine to erase through the erase run | [`../../GDPR/2026-11-16-inactive-subject-retention.md`](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-inactive-subject-retention.md), `listFilteredCandidates` | Specified; CRM people and portal users first |
| **Change-history retention** — two retention sets declared by this module over a routine in the audit module | [`2026-11-16-action-log-retention.md`](2026-11-16-action-log-retention.md) | Specified; Enterprise sets on the OSS `audit_logs` routine of DEC-B, the placement #2980 asks for |

A policy targets a declared record-retention set or subject-retention set only. `target_mode` selects the registry, `set_id` selects the set, and the floor is that declaration's `minimumDays` plus any stricter handler setting. The Data classes tab shows export/erasure coverage; the Policies tab selects retention sets and their age basis. Privacy settings used by a handler come from R3-3, "Tenant privacy settings live in `module_configs`", in the contract spec; this engine does not invent another settings store.

### How a hold matches

**Durable hold protection.** Use the OSS privacy-owned `privacyHoldProtectionService.requirePrivacyHoldProtection({ tenantId })` and its durable `privacy.holdProtectionRequired` latch in [record retention](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-record-retention.md). Arm it before accepting the first hold and backfill it for existing active holds as an upgrade prerequisite while destructive workers are fenced. A latch write or backfill failure blocks hold acceptance or rollout. Releasing the last hold leaves it armed. Disabling, uninstalling or failing to load the Enterprise module leaves the durable latch intact. A tenant may be unlatched only through that spec's audited decommission after successful reconciliation of holds. Both guards and every protected caller refuse destructive work when required protection cannot be established. Only a readable positively clear baseline in an expected OSS composition allows proceeding without a guard. An unknown or unreadable tenant state with no valid guard returns `guard_unavailable`.

The derivation and aliases come from DEC-A, "Subjects known only by an identifier (`privacy:identifier`)", and the contract spec's "Aliases" and "A subject that is only an identifier" sections. The engine never derives a new identifier format or reads an owner's tables.

- **Creation and extension.** For a `customers:person` or `customer_accounts:user` hold, call `privacySubjectService.resolveAliases` in the tenant and append its `identifier_subject_ids`; never remove an id. Store the current tenant key check and `identifier_ids_computed_at` after the first successful computation, including a successful empty result. Later computations preserve that key epoch. A typed identifier is passed to `deriveIdentifierSubjectId` in memory, and only its derived id is persisted. The typed value reaches no command payload, audit snapshot or log.
- **R3-10 — One definition of a stale hold.** An active hold is stale exactly when it carries a derived id (its subject kind is `privacy:identifier`, or `identifier_subject_ids` is non-empty) and its `identifier_key_check` is missing or differs from the current tenant check. The check is the contract's derived id for `hold-check@privacy.invalid`. All routes, guards, responses and tests use this one predicate.
- **No lookup secret (R5-11).** Refuse a typed identifier hold with `identifier_key_unavailable`. A person or portal-user hold stores an empty list, `identifier_key_check = 'none'` and null computation time; it is not stale. The contract already refuses identifier erasure then. Its direct subject hold still works, and unrelated person erasures are not blocked merely because the secret is absent. Once a secret is configured, the hold remains non-stale and awaits its first computation by the next daily pass or extension. Until every pending hold is initialized, the guards conservatively refuse all destructive erasure and retention in that tenant with `hold_identifier_resolution_failed` and a translated pending-computation reason. A different CRM or portal root can reach the same data through its aliases, and the missing set cannot be safely excluded.
- **First computation after a secret appears (R5-11).** The daily module job or next extension resolves the active person/portal-user hold's current ids through `privacySubjectService.resolveAliases` and atomically replaces `'none'` with the current tenant key check, stores the ids and advances `identifier_ids_computed_at` and `updated_at` under the own-row lock. No earlier key epoch or former-address ids exist to discard. A successful empty result still records the check and time, ending the pending state. A failed computation leaves the sentinel, empty ids and null computation time unchanged; extension refuses atomically, and the daily pass retries on its next run. This is first-key initialization only; it never replaces an established epoch.
- **Computation fails while a secret exists.** Refuse creation or extension atomically with `hold_identifier_resolution_failed`; do not persist a partly computed hold. A guard that cannot resolve an active hold's current aliases refuses with that code. An empty list from a successful resolution is a valid result, distinct from failure.
- **Refresh at the guard.** Before matching an erasure or answering retention, resolve current ids for each active person/portal-user hold with an established key check through the service, in batches of at most 100. A hold with `'none'` awaits the daily pass or extension under R5-11; guard refresh does not append ids while preserving that sentinel. For a non-stale initialized hold append new ids, preserving old ids and its key epoch. Apply refresh uses a locked own-table write and advances `updated_at`. With C4's `readOnly: true`, use the in-memory union of stored and current ids for matching without changing the hold row. A contact added since initialization is therefore covered at the next guard check, without waiting for an extension. Failures refuse the run.
- **A changed key never clears itself.** Test staleness before refresh or extension. Do not replace a stale hold's check with the current check: former-address ids cannot be rederived without their values. Extension of a stale hold returns `hold_identifier_key_changed` and changes nothing. An authorized administrator must place replacement subject/identifier holds under the current key for all current and former identifiers from their case records, then release the old hold using the separate release feature. Until release, the stale hold continues to block. Typing only current addresses is not evidence of former-address coverage.

The erasure guard refuses before the run's first mutation:

1. A subject whose kind and id equal an active subject hold (`subject_held`).
2. A subject belonging to a held data class (`subject_held`).
3. An identifier whose id equals an active identifier hold or any held `identifier_subject_ids` (`subject_held`).
4. A person or portal user whose current derived ids match those held ids (`subject_held`).
5. Every identifier, CRM-person and portal-user subject in the tenant while any active hold is stale (`hold_identifier_key_changed`).
6. Every erasure in the tenant while a secret is configured and an active person/portal-user hold still has `identifier_key_check = 'none'` (R5-11, `hold_identifier_resolution_failed`, with a pending-computation reason). This state remains distinct from a stale established key.

The retention guard, `excludedSubjects({ scope, setId })`, refuses a held set and any stale, pending-first-key or unreadable hold. Otherwise it returns held subjects plus one identifier reference per held id. DEC-C, "Retention sets and holds", governs unsupported kinds and `hold_not_honourable`. Class holds whose subject kinds cannot be attributed to a set also fail closed. `allowUnmatchedHolds` never lifts a stale-key, pending-first-key, guard-error or explicit set-hold refusal, and is never accepted for a set whose declaration carries `unmatchedHoldsOptOut: 'forbidden'` (**Policy validation and the unmatched-hold opt-out**, under API Contracts).

`setId` may also be the fixed id `forms.submission_retention`, which scheduled forms retention passes ([forms retention scheduling](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-forms-retention-scheduling.md), R3-5). It is not a registered retention set and never falls back to the `forms.submissions` data class: no set hold can name it; subject holds and their identifier references are returned as for any set; a class hold the guard cannot attribute, including one on `forms.submissions`, throws.

There remains a concurrency boundary: a contact committed after a guard's final read is not covered by that already-running erasure. A contact added and removed entirely between checks cannot be recovered from the present aliases. The holds page states this limit; operators extend the hold after changing contact points and preserve former identifiers in their case records.

Identifier ids are derived only for `customers:person` and `customer_accounts:user` holds, the two owners that supply `identifier_subject_ids` (contract spec, "Aliases"). A hold on a staff user (`auth:user`), or on any other kind, matches that subject alone; an erasure by one of the held user's addresses is a separate `privacy:identifier` subject that guard rules 3 and 4 above cannot match. When a hold targets a staff user, the create dialog and the holds list show a translated notice telling the administrator to add a typed identifier hold for each of that user's addresses and numbers.

### A subject request from the page

The page takes a known `kind:id`, pasted or supplied by a subject-page link. Identifier search remains the OSS command. After tenant-wide authorization, construct service scope as `{ tenantId }`; the operation stores its equivalent null `organization_id`, never forwards an `organizationId` member to C4 or drops a caller's organization restriction.

- **Export** records an engine-started operation, calls `privacySubjectService.exportSubjects` and returns the document through the export-audit helper `exportResponse` ([`../../GDPR/2026-11-16-export-audit-event.md`](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-export-audit-event.md)) with `resourceKind: 'privacy.subject_export'`, `format: 'json'`, `scope: 'subject'`, the document's total record count and a file name. The route is in that spec's classified list as an export, so it feeds the same `audit_logs.export.completed` event and large-export rule as every other staff download; C1's own `privacy.subject_export` evidence and this ledger row are written as well. The ledger keeps subject kind/id, classes and counts, never the download or names.
- **Erase, step one** takes `{ subject, excludedRelated, prefer, reference }`. The server supplies scope and actor, calls the R3-14, "The `eraseSubjects` entry point", with `origin: 'engine:request'`, `mode: 'dry-run'` and trailing `{ requireReviewedPlan: true }`, and stores the immutable intent: scope including explicit `organization_id = null` for the tenant-wide page, subject, `prefer`, sorted/deduplicated `excluded_related`, actor, encrypted reference, digest and `preview_expires_at` fifteen minutes later. It returns the report and operation id.
- **Digest.** Store and compare the returned R3-14 `PrivacyErasureRunResult.reportDigest` directly. Consume C4's [**Canonical report projection**](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-subject-erasure.md#canonical-report-projection) verbatim through its sole implementation in `privacy/lib/erasureReport.ts`; the engine maintains no local projection or reduced field list. Changing any material field in that owner-defined projection requires a new preview.
- **Erase, step two** accepts only `{ operationId }`. Zod rejects replacement subject, scope, preference or exclusions. Recheck the actor's feature and unrestricted scope. Load by tenant and requester, require an unexpired preview, recompute the dry run from stored intent with `requireReviewedPlan: true` and compare its digest. The following claim and job attachment occur in one transaction. A repeated request observes the stored operation and job. Changed intent or digest requires a new preview.
- **Queued apply.** Under the tenant-scoped operation row lock, claim `preview -> applying`, create a progress job through `progressService.createJobInTransaction({ jobType: 'data_erasure.subjects.erase', totalCount: 1, cancellable: false, meta: { operationId } }, { tenantId, organizationId: null, userId }, transactionalEm)` and attach its id to `progress_job_id` in one transaction. The progress owner adds this optional DI method to its interface and reuses its existing scoped job builder with the exact supplied manager. Return the persisted job and a deferred job-created publisher which the engine calls after commit. A lost notification leaves the job queryable and queue reconciliation uses the committed id. Existing two-argument `createJob` callers retain their behavior. A progress-service override missing the transactional method refuses before claiming, with no fallback to a separate transaction. Roll back the claim and job together on failure. Enqueue `{ operationId, progressJobId, tenantId }` after commit and return `202` with `{ ok: true, operationId, progressJobId }` only for the committed pair. Request retry, worker-start and daily reconciliation recover a lost enqueue using the attached job id. For an older `applying` row missing a job, all recovery paths use the same scoped row-lock helper to attach one, or use the id a concurrent recovery attached. Each worker delivery takes the operation-scoped exclusive try-lock through completion, rereads the pair and `applying` state, and ignores terminal, superseded or mismatching deliveries. A later delivery may take over after a worker dies. Before the apply, call `startJob` with the persisted job, including when the stale sweep marked it failed. While awaiting erasure, call `progressService.touchJobHeartbeat?.(progressJobId, { tenantId, organizationId: null, userId })` every quarter of `STALE_JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (60 s) using its own forked manager. Clear the keepalive in `finally`. The operation row holds the result, including while `applying` without the optional heartbeat. Once the worker updates it, complete or fail its job. Reconciliation repairs a lost terminal job update from the stored outcome without repeating erasure. Use scoped conditional operation transitions to coordinate with audit/event reconciliation. Retry any failed notification independently of operation completion. The shared top bar tracks the job and the requests tab refreshes on its terminal state.
- **Apply and recovery.** Call R3-14 `eraseSubjects({ scope, subjects: [subject], excludedRelated, prefer, actor, reference, origin: 'engine:request', mode: 'apply' }, { expectedReportDigest: operation.reportDigest })` with only stored values. The earlier route check is advisory when a job is queued. C4 compares the stored digest under its subject lock before any first destructive work and resumes committed partial work only within the immutable approved plan. An `ERASURE_PREVIEW_CHANGED` result fails the job and records an `incomplete` operation with that reason, ineligible for automatic re-enqueue. The page requires a fresh reviewed preview to proceed. A `REPLAN_REQUIRED` result after partial work stays visible for controller reconciliation and cannot expand the original approval. The audit-only reference is `privacy-operation:<operationId>`; the controller's case reference stays encrypted on the operation row. `PrivacyActor` is the erasure owner's validated attribution string; reconstruct it from the stored minimal actor reference rather than passing a new actor object. The origin enum stays unchanged. The operation id is correlated through that audit reference, never appended to origin or added to the event. Completion events carry no free-text reference (R3-14).
- **Completion after a crash.** The persistent subscriber handles `engine:request` too. It uses `actionLogService.list` to find the event's started/completed audit entries by tenant, resource and `runId`, validates the owner's `PrivacyErasureAuditContext`, requires `status: 'complete'` and matching immutable intent, reads the audit-only reference, and updates the existing operation rather than inserting a duplicate. Since the event precedes the completed entry, an event, started checkpoint or `status: 'incomplete'` never proves completion; otherwise queue reconciliation. A daily reconciliation and a retry of step two use the same lookup, including zero-change completions that emit nothing. An accepted `applying` row no longer expires; if a started run is incomplete, the recovery worker resumes only that stored intent through the normal guard. Inspect the matching entry in R3-14 `subjects[]`: `complete` is terminal only with zero `filesPending`; `not_found` is a separate terminal absence outcome, never a successful erasure. `incomplete` and `refused` stay visible with their codes. The aggregate `complete` flag is not a substitute for these per-subject outcomes. The user need not recreate a preview after committed changes. Store only the minimal typed actor reference (`actor_kind`, nullable `actor_id`), with no name/ACL snapshot; erased staff actor references are cleared by the module class, and server recovery uses an explicit system recovery actor while preserving the audit-only operation correlation.
- **Recovery of a failed portal handoff (R5-1).** C4 lets this page resume a portal-origin journal that the portal worker handed off in `failed_handoff`, under `data_erasure.subjects.erase` ([`../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-subject-erasure.md`](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-subject-erasure.md), "Origin-preserving portal recovery"). The requests tab takes the journal's run id. Preview calls `privacySubjectService.resumeErasureRun({ scope: { tenantId }, runId, actor, mode: 'dry-run' }, { requireReviewedPlan: true })`, where `actor` is the authenticated user id, and returns the reconstructed report and its `reportDigest`. Apply takes only `{ runId, reportDigest }`: the journal fixes the subject, exclusions, preference, reference and origin, so zod rejects every replacement subject, identifier, exclusion, preference, reference or scope field. The route recomputes the dry run and refuses a changed digest, then creates or reuses the operation for that `run_id` (type `erase`, origin `portal`, the journal's subject and exclusions from the report, the user as `actor_kind`/`actor_id`), stores the reviewed digest, claims it `applying` and attaches its single `ProgressJob` in the same transaction as a page erase, then enqueues after commit. A new preview may replace the digest only before C4 accepts the first adoption. A committed adoption retains its approved digest and job across retries. The worker calls `resumeErasureRun({ scope: { tenantId }, runId, actor, mode: 'apply' }, { expectedReportDigest: operation.reportDigest })` with the stored run id, actor and digest, which C4 records as `resumedBy`, under the same `startJob` and heartbeat keepalive as a page apply. Because `run_id` is saved before the apply, R4-4 rule (a) holds that run's evidence, and the event subscriber and reconciliation complete this row by `run_id`; the journal keeps its own reference, so no audit-only operation reference applies. C4 refuses a journal that is not in `failed_handoff`, belongs to another tenant or has mismatched evidence, and the route then stores nothing. An incomplete recovery leaves the journal in `failed_handoff` and the operation `incomplete`; an eligible retry reclaims the same row and job, and a repeated request while it is `applying` returns its status and job id. A stale approval without a committed adoption requires a fresh preview of that same journal.

C4's **Erasure-evidence expiry** has its own `privacy.erasure_evidence` set and `retention.privacy.erasureEvidenceAgeDays` (default 365 days, floor 30, assumption 20), separately from this ledger and C1 export evidence. R4-4 replaces the proof-contract handoff with C4's optional DI seam `privacyErasureEvidenceHold`, whose only method is `isPending({ scope, runId }): Promise<boolean>`. Expiry skips a run when any registered provider returns true or throws, counts it as `held_by_owner`, and retries on the next pass. With no providers, nothing is held through this seam; C4's terminal/hold/recovery rules still apply. Repair cannot assume indefinite completed audit availability. Missing or expired completed audit evidence leaves an unresolved operation visible for explicit recovery, never inferred complete. Open journals remain until reconciled. C4 specifies awaited capture in **The run**, step 10, **Optional completion capture — C9**, and per-attempt `PrivacyErasureAuditContext.verifiedAt` in **Audit entries**. Their implementation and C9's crash/restore-before-repair tests must still ship before protected completion is claimed; a specified handoff is not runtime protection.

**R4-4 — Engine owner signal.** `data_erasure/di.ts` registers exactly one value under `privacyErasureEvidenceHold`: a composite in `lib/evidenceHold.ts` over two own-row checks, this module's check below and replay's manifest/applied-pair check ([restore replay](2026-11-16-erasure-restore-replay.md), Architecture, "Erasure-evidence owner signal"). The composite runs both, returns true if either returns true, otherwise throws if either threw, and returns false only when both returned false. Implement this module's own-row check and contribute it to the composite registration. Its `isPending({ scope, runId })` returns true while that scoped run has an unresolved operation or reconciliation, or while any accepted erasure in that tenant is unresolved with no saved `run_id`, and false only after a successful own-row check establishes neither remains. Answer only from this module's scoped operation/reconciliation rows; do not query audit tables, another owner's tables or replay files to answer it. A failed read throws and therefore keeps the run `held_by_owner` for this pass. R4-4 requires this signal through apply-before-ledger-update and event-before-completed-audit recovery, including zero-change completions; **Data Models**, "R4-4 — Run correlation before the ledger update", covers the window before `run_id` is saved. Reconciliation of the existing operation clears its pending condition; terminal metadata expiry does not require a replacement proof checkpoint. Replay's check is combined with this one in the single composite registration; neither check is registered on its own, because a second Awilix registration under the same key would replace the first.

### A retention pass

A policy targets a retention set only. All callers use the same module service: the scheduled job and `retention-run --apply` upsert one apply-pass operation for `(tenant_id, policy_id, pass_date)`, where `pass_date` is the UTC date. Preview operations are separate and consume no apply budget. The apply pass stores a snapshot of the validated settings and their digest; another call on that date resumes that row or returns its terminal result. It never starts a second daily allowance.

**Lease and budget.** Lease state belongs to the pass, never the editable policy: `lease_owner` is a random token, `lease_until` is five minutes ahead and is renewed at least every sixty seconds while work runs. Claim, renewal, item reservation, cursor, counters and finalization are conditional on the same owner token and an unexpired lease. Reserve work and its budget durably before calling the OSS operation; reservations count even if the process crashes. Subject mode reserves one subject; record mode reserves at most the batch limit handed to the handler. Only subject-mode reservations count against the daily limit; record-mode reservations track in-flight work without a daily ceiling. Unused reservations may be returned only after a proven terminal result. This deliberately prefers less work over exceeding the limit.

A pass has at most one in-flight subject or record batch, stored with its run correlation or batch reference. Losing the lease stops new calls and fences every subsequent pass write by that worker. An expired lease with in-flight work is not permission to start overlapping work: reconcile its completion first; if completion cannot be proved, leave the pass `recovery_required` until the old worker is confirmed stopped and the idempotent work is recovered. Other workers may report this state but cannot erase another candidate. This trades availability for a firm daily bound. A new date does not bypass unresolved in-flight work for the same policy.

1. Revalidate the policy and its set declaration. A removed set, a floor that now exceeds the window or changed policy settings stop new work. A policy-setting change returns the policy to preview; it does not grant another same-day budget.
2. Check holds. A held set records `held`. Subject mode reads bounded batches through `privacyRetentionService.listFilteredCandidates({ scope, setId, olderThanDays, limit, after, acknowledgedDisabledSources })`, passing the policy's `acknowledged_disabled_sources`; `required_source_missing` is a skipped pass, without failure back-off. `required_source_disabled` (a required module not enabled and not named in the policy's acknowledgement) is a skipped pass without failure back-off, like `required_source_missing`; acknowledged disabled modules are listed on the pass and never reported as a clean source result. The pass owns its cursor; the next date starts at the beginning.
3. **R3-13 — No `related_subjects: include` anywhere.** `related_subjects` accepts `skip` (default) or `exclude` only. Policy automation evaluates candidates without per-person human review; the manual command alone requires an operator-reviewed signed list. Before reserving work, call the inactive-subject spec's shared `evaluateCandidates` for these explicit references, with the same `acknowledgedDisabledSources`. Newly active, no-longer-qualifying and absent outcomes are counted separately; unknown/missing/duplicate outcomes refuse the batch. Never re-enumerate in place of that check.
4. In preview, stop at evaluation and read-only R3-14 dry runs (`origin: 'engine:retention'`), with each candidate alone and the same skip/exclude closure rules; never call the apply-only `eraseCandidates` wrapper or create reservations/failure rows. In apply, skip held subjects and candidates whose failure back-off is not due. Reserve one slot and call `privacyRetentionService.eraseCandidates({ scope: { tenantId }, setId, olderThanDays, subjects: [candidate], related: policy.related_subjects, prefer, acknowledgedDisabledSources: policy.acknowledged_disabled_sources, actor, reference, origin: 'engine:retention' })`. That wrapper rechecks activity immediately before its R3-14 dry-run/apply calls and the OSS erasure guard rereads holds before mutation. It passes the candidate alone in `subjects`, uses the full reported closure only as `excludedRelated`, always excludes a portal account's CRM person, skips a related CRM candidate by default, and refuses a newly discovered closure until a fresh dry run. The engine adds no second erase loop. Consume `PrivacyRetentionExecutionReport` and its unmodified R3-14 results: complete, incomplete, refused, not-found, skipped, newly active and unknown remain distinct, including per-section outcomes and `filesPending`. Only verified completion removes failure rows; incomplete/refused outcomes back off. An absent candidate is not counted as an erasure.
5. Stop at the daily limit: default 100, integer 1–1000. No zero, negative, fractional, null-as-unlimited or unlimited value is accepted for a subject policy. Record mode has no daily limit (**Record-mode throughput**).

**Record mode** uses DEC-C, "Retention sets and holds", through the record-retention spec's sole batch driver `privacyRetentionService.run` and its exact `PrivacyRetentionRunInput` / `PrivacyRetentionRunResult`. Call `run({ scope: { tenantId }, setId, olderThanDays, target: policy.record_target, reference, max: batchSize, batchLimit: batchSize, after: savedNext, excludeSubjects, dryRun, enginePolicy: { policyId: policy.id, updatedAt: policy.updatedAt, allowUnmatchedHolds: policy.allowUnmatchedHolds } })`, where the validated target is `records` or `softDeleted`. Persist the returned `cutoff` and `next`, and in preview the result's `warnings` (**Preview and activation**); `cutoff` is the fixed UTC ISO eligibility timestamp, or null only when validation/refusal occurs before selection, and `next` remains the unchanged opaque continuation binding that cutoff, exclusions and policy identity. Resume with `after: savedNext`, never a caller-supplied cutoff, and never recompute it or call `pruneRecords` directly. Apply reserves one batch of 1–100 examined records under the pass's lease before this call; with `enginePolicy`, the service makes at most one handler call, even when it spends less than the reservation. Preview uses a bounded maximum with no apply reservation. The command exposes no such opt-out. The service rereads `privacyRetentionGuard` before every batch; a changed exclusion list yields `holds_changed`, stops further writes, preserves committed counts and requires a fresh preview. Guard errors, stale keys and explicit set holds always refuse. Unsupported held kinds skip as `hold_not_honourable`, unless that exact policy opts out; supported exclusions still apply. Both initial record sets, including `notifications.inbox`, declare `excludableSubjectKinds: []`: an `auth:user` hold also skips them because another user's text may mention that person.

Under **R3-8 — Retention drains its own pending file deletions**, the service drains origin `privacy:retention:<setId>` after each committed batch and retries its backlog at apply entry, even with no matching rows. The engine adds no drain. Persist returned `matched`, `deleted`, `filesPending`, `filesShared`, `next`, `done`, `complete`, `status` and `reason`; an incomplete result's `reason` becomes the pass's `incomplete_reason`. The owner calls a handler's post-commit failure `incompleteReason` and exposes run-level reasons through `PrivacyRetentionRunResult.reason`; the engine never reads an invented result member. Storage/drain failure or any remaining file makes the pass incomplete after committed row deletion. `done` alone is not completion while files or side effects remain. Keep the pass recoverable and retry through the same service. DEC-B, "The audit-history strip routine", and the action-log-retention spec own snapshot-only hold attribution and the three-record-pair matching rule; this engine supplies the narrow policy opt-out through the shared service context.

`matched`, `deleted` and `filesShared` are per-invocation counts from `PrivacyRetentionRunResult`; accumulate each reservation once, never recount prior batches. `filesPending` is the remaining post-drain backlog, not a count to sum across retries. For evidence-expiry policies, preserve the declaration's `runEvidence: 'aggregate'` selection and the record-retention owner's **Aggregate evidence for expiry runs** projection, `PrivacyRetentionAggregateSummary`. The driver writes that summary; the engine adds no second identifying `privacy.retention_run` entry and supplies no logging override in `PrivacyRetentionRunInput`.

**Record-mode throughput.** A record pass is not capped by a daily record count: a busy tenant can write more change-history or inbox entries a day than any fixed count, and the set would then never reach its configured age (action-log-retention spec, **Throughput**). Each `privacyRetentionService.run` call still reserves one batch of 1–100 examined records under the lease. A job runs a record pass for a 30-second run budget, calling the service one batch at a time until it returns `done` or the budget is spent, then enqueues one coalesced continuation for that pass on the module's queue immediately; the continuation resumes from the saved `next` under the same lease and in-flight rules, until the service returns `done`. Load is bounded by queue concurrency 1, the batch size and the run budget. If the next pass date begins while the previous record pass is not `done`, the previous pass is closed `incomplete` with `incomplete_reason = 'backlog_growing'`, once its in-flight batch is reconciled under the rules above, and the new pass starts from its own new cutoff, which includes the older rows. `retention-run --apply` on the same date resumes that pass and its continuation; it never starts a second one. Preview stops after one bounded batch.

**Preview and activation.** Preview counts without erasure, failure rows or an apply cursor. It stores `policy_settings_digest` and counts on its operation. The digest covers tenant, mode of set, set id, age/target, preference, related rule, daily limit (subject mode), acknowledged disabled sources, `allowUnmatchedHolds` and active state, excluding preview/apply mode and edit timestamps. Switching to apply requires that preview operation id and its count, and the digest must still match the locked current policy. Any execution-setting edit resets mode to preview and invalidates activation evidence. Preview cannot be used as a count-only confirmation. For a policy with `allowUnmatchedHolds`, the preview also stores in `counts_json.bypassedHolds` the ids of the active holds whose subject kinds the set cannot leave out, with their counts by kind, and the activation dialog lists them by hold id, subject kind and reason code; no address or reference is shown. Activation compares the current list with the stored one and requires a fresh preview when a hold was added. A record-mode preview also stores in `counts_json.warnings`, beside its counts, the distinct `warnings` codes of its `PrivacyRetentionRunResult` results. These are the record-retention spec's bounded dry-run codes, such as `REPLAY_ROLLOUT_UNCONFIRMED`, which the action-log retention handler fills from `actionLogService.isReplayRolloutConfirmed()` on a strip-mode dry run; the engine never reads them from `applyRetention`, whose DEC-B result carries none. The policies tab shows the latest preview's warnings, translated, with its counts and in the activation dialog. A warning never blocks activation and is not part of `policy_settings_digest`.

### Design decisions

| Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|
| The engine calls only the OSS service and handlers | It must never read another module's tables. |
| Policy and hold tables have no organization column | Their routes pass `orm.orgField: null`; operation rows separately store the execution scope for recovery. |
| A pass restarts from the beginning each day | A subject that failed, or whose hold was released, is seen again. A cursor that only moves forward would lose both. |
| Failures are rows with a back-off, not a stop | One record that cannot be erased must not block the set, and must not be retried every day forever. |
| Ledger coverage has an explicit boundary | Engine operations and event-announced external erasures are recorded; other operations remain in the action log. Engine-request events reconcile existing rows. |
| Holds are append-only apart from extension and release | See assumption 10. |
| A person's hold carries the derived ids of their addresses | An erasure by address is a separate subject; see assumption 7. |
| Identifier ids are only added, never removed | A hold never narrows. An old address can still key a guest basket or a claim. |
| A stale hold refuses every subject it could miss | A changed lookup secret silently breaks matching by derived id; refusing until replacement coverage is placed and the stale hold released preserves former-address protection. |
| A set that cannot leave a held subject out is skipped | See assumption 14. |
| Enabling the unmatched-hold opt-out needs the release feature too | It lets held records go, which only a user who may end a hold should decide; see assumption 14. |
| No policy can be shorter than its set's minimum | Floors and candidate handlers are declared on retention sets. |
| The module registers a data class for its own rows | Policies, holds and operations name the staff user who created them. |

### Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Deleting by SQL from the engine | Bypasses every module's rules. |
| Scheduler entries wired by hand | See assumption 2. |
| Free-text hold reasons | See assumption 9. |
| Encrypting or hashing the subject id in holds and the ledger | See assumptions 7 and 8. |
| Refuse every identifier subject while any subject hold exists in the tenant | Blocks every guest's erasure for one person's dispute; the derived ids make an exact match possible. |
| Store the held person's address hashes and compare them with the run's aliases | The guard sees one subject's kind and id, not the run's aliases; and a lookup hash is a pseudonym that matches columns, which a derived id does not. |
| Delete by age and ignore held subjects a set cannot attribute, unless a strict option is set | Held data goes by default. The opt-out, `allowUnmatchedHolds`, goes the other way. |
| Per-organization holds and policies | See assumption 6. |
| Resolving a person by identifier from the page | A request that runs for minutes; a queue and a place to keep names while it runs. |
| Passing the operation id into the erase run so `run_id` is saved before the first mutation | R4-4 adds no input member or handoff; the tenant-wide unsaved-run rule under **Data Models** closes the window from own rows. |
| Bundling sandbox sanitization | A different capability with a different risk. |

**R4-4 — Run correlation before the ledger update.** R3-14 dry run returns a null run id, and apply creates the run id inside the OSS run. An accepted page erasure, or a retention pass's in-flight subject step, therefore has no saved `run_id` between the apply call and its ledger update; the crash path recovers the association through the audit-only `privacy-operation:<operationId>` reference. Rule (b) is tenant-wide on purpose: an unsaved correlation could belong to any run, and C4's erasure-evidence journals are tenant-wide. Once reconciliation saves `run_id` or reaches a terminal result, only rule (a) applies to that work. A partial index on tenant over unresolved rows with a null `run_id` keeps rule (b) one indexed read. The cost is that one unresolved crash-window operation holds every erasure-evidence group of its tenant until reconciled; the program rule that unresolved work extends evidence accepts this, and the operations tab shows the backlog. No new input member or correlation handoff is added.

### Metadata retention and coverage

The classification objects use the exact `PrivacyTableClassification` from [`../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-table-coverage.md`](../../GDPR/2026-11-16-privacy-table-coverage.md), **API Contracts**; bare `retention_bounded` labels are not declarations. The daily own-table housekeeper invokes them through `privacyRetentionService.run` at those windows; their handlers retain active holds and accepted incomplete operations, and enforce the terminal/retry clocks below on every batch. Under DEC-C the service passes the guard's held subjects as `excludeSubjects`. These rows name subjects in clear, so the handlers leave out exactly the rows that name an excluded subject: as `subject_kind`/`subject_id`; inside an operation's `excluded_related`; among a hold's `identifier_subject_ids`, for a `privacy:identifier` reference; and, for `auth:user`, as `created_by`, `requested_by`, `released_by` or a staff `actor_id`. An operation with a non-empty `inflight_json` is never eligible. Other rows keep their clocks while a hold is active. A hold on a kind outside that list skips the set with `hold_not_honourable`, a guard failure refuses, and no unmatched-hold opt-out applies to these housekeeping calls. Expired previews and subject/policy cleanup may be removed earlier by the specified own-table lifecycle cleanup. Configuration policy rows remain `tenant_configuration` with per-column review reasons, not unbounded anonymous data.

Completed operations expire 365 days after `completed_at`. Unused previews expire after fifteen minutes and are removed on the next daily sweep. Accepted incomplete operations remain visible until a terminal result is reconciled; the page flags unresolved work rather than silently deleting it. Reference-only failure rows are removed after subject completion (all policies, via event and audit reconciliation), policy deletion, or thirty days after `last_attempt_at`, whichever comes first. Retry state is an optimization: aging it out does not prevent the next daily candidate scan.

Released or expired holds are removed 365 days after their terminal time; active holds never age out. Soft-deleted policy rows are removed after 365 days once no incomplete operation depends on them; active policy configuration stays until deleted. The daily module job performs these own-table deletes through the declared sets in batches of at most 100, with no subject payload in logs. Governance rows are excluded from generic history-retention sets unless the separate evidence-preservation rule permits deletion. Replay's applied rows/files have their own window and pending exception in the replay spec; ledger expiry never deletes replay evidence.

An unused preview is anchored at its expiry and so falls under the 365-day bound; the fifteen-minute lifecycle cleanup removes it much earlier, which the bound permits.

`dataErasureRetentionBounds` is registered in `data_erasure/di.ts` and implements C5's `PrivacyRetentionBoundHandler` read-only, on the supplied manager, with no write, enqueue or foreign table read. `resolve({ scope, boundId, now, em })` returns the declared targets exactly, with:
- `effectiveWindow: { value: 365, unit: 'days' }` for `data_erasure.governance_evidence` and `{ value: 30, unit: 'days' }` for `data_erasure.retention_failures`, the fixed windows of assumption 16;
- `mode: 'delete'`, or `'pending'` while the daily job holds an outstanding continuation for the tenant and set; `countingGraceEndsAt: null`, since the tables are new and have no counting period;
- `enabled: false`, `mode: 'off'` and `unboundedReason: 'retention_runner_unavailable'` when `privacyRetentionService` is not registered;
- `scheduled: true` only when the soft-optional `schedulerService` reports the `data_erasure:daily-maintenance` schedule registered and enabled;
- `workerState` as the scheduler reports it, otherwise `'unknown'`, never inferred from the schedule;
- `unboundedReason: 'hold_not_honourable'` when a registered `privacyRetentionGuard` returns a subject whose kind is outside the set's `excludableSubjectKinds`. A failing guard makes `resolve` throw, which is a verification gate, never an exemption.

`evaluateFixtureRow` reads the row by id within the tenant scope. It returns `not_found` for a missing or out-of-scope row, `not_eligible` for a row the eligibility rule keeps (an active hold, unresolved or pending work, a currently held subject), and `unbounded` with the projection's reason when there is one. Otherwise it returns `within_window` or `expired`, with `anchorAt` and `expiresAt` from the rule above. `reason` is the rule id, and no row value is returned. The replay spec's `data_erasure.replay_evidence` bound for `privacy_erasure_applied` is served by the same handler service from that spec's own predicates and window. The same handler also resolves bound ids `data_erasure.change_values` and `data_erasure.change_history` for ordinary change history in `action_logs`; their targets, predicates and projection states (including `backlog_growing` and `hold_not_honourable` as pending reasons) are defined in the [action-log-retention spec](2026-11-16-action-log-retention.md), "Retention bounds for C5/G6". Adding the entries preserves the default object's other members under R3-2.

**Governance action entries.** The action-log-retention spec's **Evidence outside the generic sets**, assumption 9, also assigns this module expiry of its `data_erasure.*` audit entries. Declare the concrete resource kinds `data_erasure.policy`, `data_erasure.legal_hold`, `data_erasure.operation` and `data_erasure.retention_failure` for this module's policy lifecycle, hold actions, operation transitions (including replay/acknowledgement) and audited failure transitions, and `data_erasure.replay_exception`, which the restore-replay CLI writes for deferral, quarantine and `protection_disabled` transitions ([restore replay](2026-11-16-erasure-restore-replay.md), Proposed Solution, "Deferral and quarantine", and the edge case "The manifest directory is unavailable"). For the first four, `resourceId` is the own-table row id; encrypted minimal context records the transition, operation correlation, lifecycle status and `terminalAt` when terminal, without raw identifiers or cleared values. For `data_erasure.replay_exception`, `resourceId` is the pending row id for a deferral, or that tenant's `privacy_erasure_replay_state` row id for quarantine (the tenant named by the file's path) and `protection_disabled` (one entry per affected (deployment, tenant) state row, scoped to that tenant); context holds only the run id or file id, the reason code and the expiry. Each replay-exception entry is terminal when written and becomes eligible for `data_erasure.audit_evidence` expiry at the configured age, with the same hold rules as the other kinds. This rule covers started and terminal entries together, rather than leaving the started entry or actor indefinitely. C1 settings commands keep `configs.module_config` / `privacy.settings.set` and do not acquire a `data_erasure.*` exemption; C1's **Settings-command evidence expiry** owns whole-operation expiry and excludes those exact groups from generic age-based history stripping/deletion.

Declare `retention.data_erasure.auditEvidenceAgeDays` in `privacy.ts.settings`: integer days, minimum 30, default 365 under assumption 17, read through R3-3. Declare `data_erasure.audit_evidence`, `targets: ['records']`, `minimumDays: 30`, `excludableSubjectKinds: []`, `unmatchedHoldsOptOut: 'forbidden'`, `handlerService: 'governanceAuditEvidenceRetentionHandler'`, `runEvidence: 'aggregate'`, with locale-key title/age basis. The daily maintenance job runs it through `privacyRetentionService.run` using the effective configured age; a shorter caller window refuses. These are audit-entry ages only: the existing 365-day metadata-row, thirty-day failure-row and fifteen-minute preview clocks above stay unchanged.

The handler lists those exact kinds through DI `actionLogService.list`; it never reads audit tables. It derives eligibility from trusted terminal transition evidence and rechecks its own resource/dependency state under the module's locks. Active policy configuration/holds, accepted incomplete operations, pending replay/file work, invalid or missing terminal proof and unresolved references defer deletion and are reported. If a metadata row has already expired, a validated terminal audit marker plus a successful own-module dependency check must still prove eligibility; a missing row alone does not. Any active subject hold or explicit exclusion skips the entire evidence set as `hold_not_honourable`; failed guard reads refuse, and `allowUnmatchedHolds` cannot override this. Release permits expiry at the original terminal age, without resetting it.

For eligible terminal resources, call the OSS-owned DEC-B `actionLogService.applyRetention` with the exact tenant, `mode: 'delete'`, fixed cutoff, bounded limit/cursor, `includeResourceKinds` restricted to the five named kinds and `includeRecords` naming only the eligible resource pairs. Delete whole entries, including actor, reference, context, snapshots and ids; never strip them into actor-only fragments. Keep aggregate counts/dates/set id only in the expiry summary, with no actor, reference or old operation/subject/run ids. This dedicated set supplies finite expiry for evidence excluded from generic history; C4 erasure journals and C9 manifests retain their separate owner windows and recovery gates.

**R3-9 — Organization-scope guard on every DSR route.** Every route file wraps every exported handler (`GET`, `POST`, `PUT`, `DELETE`, and any future HTTP method) in `withTenantWideScope(handler)` from the module's new `api/withTenantWideScope.ts`. This includes factory results: construct `crud`, then export `GET = withTenantWideScope(crud.GET)` and likewise POST, PUT and DELETE only where supported. Never export a raw factory handler or depend on a factory hook.

The wrapper resolves authentication and effective tenant/organization ACL through the existing auth/RBAC and `resolveOrganizationScopeForRequest` service path (`packages/core/src/modules/directory/utils/organizationScope.ts`). Require the registered `em` and `rbacService`, and successfully load the effective ACL (`rbacService.loadAcl`) before treating a null allowed list as unrestricted; the scope resolver's missing-service fallback is never sufficient evidence of permission. The super-admin input is the trusted effective auth/ACL decision, never a request field. A missing tenant or unresolved/failed scope refuses; only `isUnrestrictedOrganizationScope({ isSuperAdmin, allowedOrganizationIds: scope.allowedIds })` may pass. Restricted scopes return 403 before calling the handler, so `mapInput`, aliases, commands, reads and policy deletion are all behind it. Per-method feature metadata remains required and wildcard-aware; the wrapper does not replace it. This rule includes replay's status route. A source-enumerating unit test scans every module API route and fails on any exported HTTP method not wrapped, including re-exports and destructured exports.

The menu/page gate uses the same effective ACL rule. Custom hold action commands enforce `enforceCommandOptimisticLock` using their locked current version before any mutation. Custom hold actions attach `buildOptimisticLockHeader(record.updatedAt)` with `withScopedApiRequestHeaders` inside `useGuardedMutation`; `surfaceRecordConflict(err, t)` presents 409 and `retryLastMutation` is in the injection context. CLI and daily operator jobs use explicit tenant scope and have no browser ACL delegation.

Settings writes attach `buildOptimisticLockHeader(setting.updatedAt)` with `withScopedApiRequestHeaders`, including null-on-create semantics supplied to the service, and call `surfaceRecordConflict(err, t)` on stale updates or concurrent insert. Their `useGuardedMutation` context includes `retryLastMutation`; refresh before retry. The own command uses `enforceCommandOptimisticLock` for an existing setting and the configs service enforces its expected version atomically.

**Policy validation and the unmatched-hold opt-out.** Enabling `allowUnmatchedHolds` returns a field error for a subject-mode policy and for any set whose declaration carries `unmatchedHoldsOptOut: 'forbidden'`, the optional member that the record-retention spec adds to `PrivacyRetentionSetDefinition` (default `'allowed'`). Evidence owners declare it on their sets: C1's `privacy.export_evidence` and `privacy.settings_evidence`, C4's `privacy.erasure_evidence`, tenant exit's `privacy.tenant_export_evidence`, record retention's `privacy.retention_evidence`, `audit_logs.export_evidence`, this module's `data_erasure.governance_evidence`, `data_erasure.retention_failures` and `data_erasure.audit_evidence`, replay's `data_erasure.replay_evidence` and security's `security.alert_signals` and `security.alerts`. The check reads the declaration through the R3-2 registry, so a new evidence set is covered by declaring the member, without an engine change; the record-retention service refuses the same combination at run time. The opt-out action requires the release feature only for enabling, because disabling only restores protection. The policies tab shows the opt-out control only to a user holding both features.

Default role features: administrators get view, settings, policies, holds and export; erase and hold release go to the super-administrator role where it exists. Administrators therefore cannot enable the unmatched-hold opt-out until they are also granted hold release. A feature takes effect only for an administrator with tenant-wide organization access (assumption 13). CLI: `mercato data_erasure retention-run [--policy <id>] [--apply]`, preview by default, and `mercato data_erasure register-schedules [--apply-system-history]`; the separate system-history command is specified above.

**Retention guard contract.** `privacyRetentionGuard` is a DI registration, not a route. It implements the record-retention spec's `PrivacyRetentionGuard` exactly: `excludedSubjects({ scope, setId })`, which reads only the scope tenant's holds. `setId` may also be the fixed id `forms.submission_retention` that scheduled forms retention passes; it is answered as **How a hold matches** states: no set hold can name it, subject holds and their identifier references are returned, and a class hold the guard cannot attribute, including one on `forms.submissions`, throws.

## 📝 Edge Cases & Failure Scenarios

| Case | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Policy on a set whose declaration disappears | The policy is shown as inapplicable and skipped |
| Hold placed during a retention pass | Checked per candidate, so the subject is skipped from then on; one already erased is gone |
| Hold expires | It no longer blocks; expiry is evaluated at check time |
| Hold released, subject past the window | Erased by the next day's pass |
| The hold table cannot be read | The guard refuses; the erasure fails with the guard's code |
| Two administrators edit one policy | The second gets the standard conflict |
| Two workers start the same policy/date | One apply-pass row exists. Only its live lease owner may reserve work or commit progress |
| A pass is interrupted | Same-date calls resume its row and remaining budget. Unresolved in-flight work blocks overlap and a new day until reconciled; stale owners cannot commit progress |
| Event delivery fails for a command-line erasure | The ledger misses it until mandatory completed-audit reconciliation runs; configured C9 protection synchronously captures external evidence independently of delivery |
| The same event is delivered twice | The unique `run_id` makes the second a no-op |
| Scheduler absent | Policies never run; the page shows the last run as "never" with the reason |
| The `privacy` module is disabled | The engine does not load; its flag check fails at generate time |
| No user in the tenant holds the erase feature | The requests tab says so and names the feature |
| A caller holds the features but is limited to some organizations | Every route returns 403 and the page says tenant-wide access is needed; nothing is read |
| A caller is limited to an organization list that happens to include every current organization | Still restricted: the list does not grow with the tenant, so the rule looks at whether a list exists, not at what it contains |
| Policy changes after preview | Activation refuses a settings-digest mismatch and requires a new preview; the policy returns to preview on any execution-setting change |
| A staff user who created holds is erased | The module's own data class keeps the rows and clears the references |
| A captured/current held address is erased with `--email` | Token-confirmed apply with related-subject preflight satisfied reaches the guard and returns `subject_held`; no address-keyed data changes |
| An address added since hold placement | For an initialized hold, the next guard resolves current aliases and appends its id before matching. R5-11 holds carrying `'none'` first await the daily pass or extension and refuse tenant erasure and destructive retention in the meantime. A change after the final guard read remains a concurrency limit |
| A held person changes an address | Old ids stay. Guard refresh/extension adds current ids in the same epoch. A key change never overwrites the former-address epoch |
| Two people share an address and one is held | An erasure by that address, and an erasure of the other person, are refused, naming the hold. Fail closed: records keyed by that address may be the held person's |
| A hold on an address, and an erasure of a person who has it | Refused: the guard compares the person's current derived ids with identifier holds |
| The lookup secret changes | Every active hold carrying an id is stale under R3-10. Guarded identifier/person/portal erasures and retention refuse until replacement coverage is placed and the stale hold released; extension cannot refresh the old epoch |
| Alias computation fails with a secret configured | Creation/extension rolls back with `hold_identifier_resolution_failed`; an active hold whose current aliases cannot be read makes the guard refuse |
| No lookup secret | Identifier holds refuse; person/portal holds store `'none'`, empty ids and null computation time and are not stale. If a secret is later configured, all guarded destructive erasure and retention in that tenant refuse with a pending-computation reason until the next daily pass or extension computes ids and adopts the current check atomically (R5-11); a successful empty computation also ends the pending state. Failure preserves the sentinel and the refusal. Another tenant follows its own guard state |
| A hold on a CRM person, and a policy on a set that declares no person kind | The pass is skipped with `hold_not_honourable` every day until the hold ends, or until a user holding the release feature enables `allowUnmatchedHolds` and a fresh preview listing the bypassed holds is activated |
| A hold on a staff user, and a policy on `notifications.inbox` | The set declares no excludable kind, so the default pass skips with `hold_not_honourable`; a separate explicitly opted-out policy may proceed with the documented snapshot risk |
| A policy manager without the release feature tries to enable `allowUnmatchedHolds` | Refused by the opt-out action; POST/PUT cannot set the flag at all |
| `allowUnmatchedHolds` on a set declaring `unmatchedHoldsOptOut: 'forbidden'` | Field error at validation; the record-retention service refuses it at run time too |
| A hold on a staff user, and an erasure by one of that user's addresses | Not matched: only person and portal-user holds carry identifier ids. The holds page tells the administrator to add a typed identifier hold for each of the user's addresses |
| A hold on a person, and the governance metadata sets | Rows naming that person stay until the hold ends; other rows keep their clocks. A hold on a kind the metadata sets do not declare skips them with `hold_not_honourable`. Engine action-audit entries skip for any hold |
| The queued apply cannot be enqueued after the claim | The operation stays `applying`; worker-start or daily reconciliation resumes its stored intent, and a repeated request returns its status |
| A queued erasure runs longer than the progress stale timeout | The worker's keepalive heartbeats the job, so the stale sweep does not fail it; a job the sweep failed before the worker started is restarted by `startJob`, and the tab reads the operation's own status |
| The OSS retention command, for a tenant with a hold | When the set cannot leave the held subject out, `privacyRetentionGuard`'s answer makes it skip the set with `hold_not_honourable` (status `skipped`, exit 1); nothing is deleted |
| A hold on a retention set | Its passes and the OSS command for it are refused with reason `held` |
| A required source module of a subject set is disabled | Without the policy's acknowledgement the pass is skipped with `required_source_disabled`, without failure back-off; with it the pass runs and lists the acknowledged modules, never reporting their activity as clean |
| More due records than one continuation job can examine | Continuation jobs run one at a time, back to back, until the service returns `done`; a pass still behind at the next pass date closes `incomplete` with `backlog_growing`, and the new pass starts from its own cutoff |
| A tenant is soft-deleted | Its rows keep their tenant id; its evidence policies and metadata housekeeping keep running until the evidence sets are empty |
| Recovery names a portal journal not in `failed_handoff`, another tenant's journal, or replacement fields | Refused before anything is stored or changed |
| An accepted erasure crashes before its `run_id` is saved | Erasure-evidence expiry counts every run of that tenant as `held_by_owner` until reconciliation saves the run id or reaches a terminal result; other tenants are not held |

## 📝 Risks & Impact Review

#### A policy erases records the tenant needed
- **Scenario**: An administrator sets a short window on contacts.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: any configured subject-retention set
- **Mitigation**: Preview operation and its settings digest must match before apply; edits return the policy to preview; set floors, skip/exclude only and the shared daily budget bound a subject run. Related subjects never enter the candidate list implicitly. A disabled required source refuses the pass unless the policy acknowledges it, and the acknowledgement is part of the settings digest.
- **Residual risk**: An informed administrator's choice is irreversible. Activity in an acknowledged disabled module is invisible to the pass.

#### A hold is missed
- **Scenario**: A hold exists and an erasure goes ahead.
- **Severity**: Critical
- **Affected area**: every erasure in the tenant
- **Mitigation**: The direct subject is stored and matched in clear, so its hold does not depend on a key, map or encryption setting. An initialized person/portal-user hold appends current derived ids at the guard, and an identifier may be held directly. R3-10 supplies one stale predicate, computation errors refuse, and rotation cannot discard former-address protection. R5-11's `'none'` sentinel blocks tenant erasure and destructive retention after a secret appears until the daily pass or extension atomically establishes the first key check; `holdRefresh.test.ts` covers success, empty results and failures without replacing an established epoch. The guard refuses when it cannot answer. The engine checks holds per candidate, and skips a retention set that cannot leave a held subject out; the OSS retention command and scheduled forms retention do the same through `privacyRetentionGuard`. Unit tests, TC-PRIVACY-GOV-001 and TC-PRIVACY-GOV-004 cover the identifier and retention cases.
- **Residual risk**: SQL and modules' independent delete commands bypass the guard. Without the enterprise guard, the OSS retention command has only operator-supplied `--exclude-subject`; operational pruners have no subject holds (R3-5). An address added after a final guard read, or added and removed between reads, remains a timing gap. A hold on a staff user or another kind without identifier ids does not cover that subject's addresses unless the administrator adds typed identifier holds, as the holds page instructs. `allowUnmatchedHolds` deletes what a set cannot attribute; the OSS command offers no such opt-out.

#### Holds stop retention for a whole tenant
- **Scenario**: One person is held, and the tenant's notifications, change history, export and settings evidence, this module's action-audit entries (`data_erasure.audit_evidence`) and replay's settled evidence (`data_erasure.replay_evidence`) are kept past their windows for as long as the hold lasts; a hold on a kind the governance metadata sets do not declare also keeps released holds, completed operations and retry rows; the lookup secret changes and every identifier and person erasure is refused; or its first configuration leaves guarded erasure and retention waiting on R5-11 computation.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: retention policies on sets; erasures after a secret change
- **Mitigation**: The skipped passes and their reasons are listed on the policies tab; the governance metadata sets leave out only the rows naming a held subject of their declared kinds; `allowUnmatchedHolds` per policy on sets that allow it, enabled only by a holder of the release feature; stale holds are listed with the replacement-and-release procedure; extension cannot clear an old key epoch. First-key pending refusals name the computation, which the next daily pass or extension performs without releasing the hold.
- **Residual risk**: Data is kept longer than a policy says while a hold is active, which is the direction a hold intends. In a tenant that always has an active hold, the whole-set skips of evidence sets that cannot attribute their entries last until no hold is active. A hold on an identifier must be retyped after a secret change. Without a running daily job, or while computation fails, R5-11 refusals persist until a successful extension or later daily pass.

#### A hold is ended by someone who may only create them
- **Scenario**: A user with the manage feature shortens a hold or points it at another subject.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: holds
- **Mitigation**: No update route; extend/release require the version and serialize under the row lock. Expiry moves only forward, null is unbounded, released rows refuse, and identifier ids only accumulate within the same epoch. Two-contender tests pin this behavior.
- **Residual risk**: None through the API.

#### A policy manager lets held records go
- **Scenario**: An administrator with only the policy feature enables `allowUnmatchedHolds`, so a pass deletes records a set cannot attribute while a person is held.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: record policies on sets that allow the opt-out
- **Mitigation**: POST/PUT cannot set the flag; the opt-out action requires `data_erasure.holds.release` as well as the policy feature to enable it, records an audited reason code and returns the policy to preview. The preview lists the bypassed holds, and activation refuses when a hold was added since. Evidence sets declare `unmatchedHoldsOptOut: 'forbidden'`. `unmatchedHoldsOptOut.test.ts`, TC-PRIVACY-GOV-003 and TC-PRIVACY-GOV-004 cover it.
- **Residual risk**: A user who may release holds can still enable the opt-out on an allowed set; that is the same power as releasing the hold, and the audit entry records it.

#### Retention floods the audit log
- **Scenario**: A first apply pass meets a hundred thousand candidates, or a record set grows by more rows a day than any fixed count.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: action log, event queue, the tenant's database
- **Mitigation**: Subject mode: default 100, range 1–1000; a unique policy/date pass shares durable reservations between scheduled/manual calls. Record mode has no daily count, so a set can reach its configured age: one reserved batch of at most 100 per call, a 30-second run budget per job, one coalesced continuation at a time and queue concurrency 1 bound its load. Owner-token renewal fences progress; unresolved in-flight work prevents overlapping takeover.
- **Residual risk**: A long tail: at the default limit a large subject backlog takes months. A record backlog that grows faster than continuations clear it is reported as `backlog_growing` until a pass reaches `done`.

#### An expired lease leaves unknown work
- **Scenario**: A worker loses its lease while an erasure or record batch is still in flight and no terminal audit/result is visible.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: scheduled and manual retention for that policy
- **Mitigation**: Persist the reservation and one in-flight step, fence stale-owner progress, and require completion proof or a confirmed stopped worker before recovery; never grant another date's budget around it.
- **Residual risk**: Retention may wait for operator recovery; lease expiry alone cannot prove that a remote worker stopped writing.

#### The ledger identifies people
- **Scenario**: Operation rows are read by someone without access to the data.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Affected area**: `privacy_operations`
- **Mitigation**: Subject kind/id and minimal intent metadata; encrypted reference, tenant-wide ACL, 365-day terminal evidence window and thirty-day failure window; completion and policy deletion remove retry rows.
- **Residual risk**: Subject ids remain pseudonymous personal data and may resolve to an exported/anonymized record. Identifier ids are keyed pseudonyms testable with secret material. Accepted incomplete operations remain beyond the terminal window until resolved; the page exposes that backlog. Rows naming a held subject stay until the hold ends, and a hold on an undeclared kind defers the whole set.

#### Erasure evidence expires before ledger reconciliation
- **Scenario**: A completed erasure's audit group reaches its age while an accepted operation still needs it for crash recovery.
- **Severity**: High
- **Affected area**: operation recovery and erasure-evidence expiry
- **Mitigation**: R4-4 requires the `privacyErasureEvidenceHold` provider to return true from own scoped rows while the operation or reconciliation is unresolved; read failures throw. C4 counts either as `held_by_owner` and retries. Before `run_id` is saved, the tenant-wide unsaved-run rule under **Data Models** holds every run of the tenant. `operationReconciliation.test.ts` and TC-PRIVACY-GOV-006 cover correlated rows and the null-`run_id` crash window.
- **Residual risk**: While an accepted erasure has no saved `run_id`, every erasure-evidence group of its tenant is held, so one unreconciled crash keeps that tenant's evidence past its age. Unresolved work or repeated read failures can also retain evidence beyond its age; successful reconciliation is needed before a later expiry pass can proceed.

#### Cross-tenant operation
- **Scenario**: A request body names another tenant's policy or subject.
- **Severity**: Critical
- **Affected area**: every route
- **Mitigation**: Scope comes from trusted auth, never the body; tenant predicates apply to every row and service call, with negative same- and cross-tenant tests. The retention guard reads only the scope tenant's holds.
- **Residual risk**: Low.

#### Cross-organization operation by a restricted administrator
- **Scenario**: An administrator whose ACL is limited to one organization holds the engine's features. The subject routes pass no organization, and the contract reads that as every organization of the tenant, so this administrator exports or erases a person in another organization. They could also read that organization's holds and operations.
- **Severity**: Critical
- **Affected area**: every route; irreversible erasure across organizations
- **Mitigation**: R3-9 wraps every exported handler before `mapInput` or any domain read/write; an enumerating unit test prevents new unwrapped methods. TC-PRIVACY-GOV-003 covers policy DELETE, hold command creation, extend/release and every subject/ledger method. Replay applies this rule to status and notifications.
- **Residual risk**: A tenant whose administrators are all organization-restricted cannot use the engine until one is given tenant-wide access. The page says so. Organization-level delegation is out of scope (see assumption 6).
